## November 21, 2026 — Universal opposed checks and critical failures (LATEST AUTHOR APPROVAL)

**Universal Opposed Check:** When one character actively challenges another character's applicable ability, use the existing **Naked-d20 six-difficulty-category resolution** and the same **defender/attacker relative-statistic ratio ladder** used in the current physical Combat Speed accuracy/evasion pilot. For a particular contest, identify the **initiator/attacker** and **holder/defender**, identify the specific eligible opposing statistics, and calculate `R = defender_stat / attacker_stat` when both are positive. Determine the initiator's category by the author-approved pilot cutoffs: `R < 0.20` **Certain**; `0.20 <= R < 0.80` **Easy (4+)**; `0.80 <= R < 1.25` **Average (8+)**; `1.25 <= R < 2.50` **Hard (12+)**; `2.50 <= R < 5.00` **Very Hard (17+)**; `R >= 5.00` **Impossible**. Roll **one d20** for categories that require it and apply existing lawful categorical shifts and caps. This is an **opposed-statistic/category check**, not automatically two independent d20 rolls; this is how Combat Speed opposition already works. Any contested skill, medium seizure or other use must identify its **own** applicable opposed statistics and permitted modifiers before using the template; it does not automatically replace a separately approved Power-ratio seizure rule or completed specialist check without reconciliation. Zero-valued statistics and ties outside the stated category bands remain contextual OPEN cases.

**Failure margin:** For a check with a numerical success target `T`, a failed result `r < T` has **failure margin `F = T - r`**. Thus a Hard 12+ attempt rolling 8 has `F=4`; rolling 7 has `F=5`; rolling 3 has `F=9`. This calculation is compatible with the existing Naked-d20 thresholds and avoids pretending a static opponent rolled its own d20. **Critical failure** is the new **universal term for failure by 9 or more** (`F >= 9`) on opposed checks and other checks with an applicable numerical target. This term alone does **not** add universal automatic damage, equipment destruction, critical misses, or story penalties; each activity's approved critical-failure outcome governs. An automatic Certain/Impossible result does not have a numerical d20 failure margin unless another rule explicitly provides one.

**Circle-hacking application:** To overcome a Reactivity-secured alchemical circle, the initiator must lawfully possess the applicable learned **Autonomy III Reactivity** capability. Oppose the **circle maker's applicable effective INT** (defender) against the **spellhacker's applicable effective INT** (attacker) using the above universal ratio/category method. The circle maker need not be physically present to provide their recorded creation-statistic defense; note the original eligible INT at installation, unless the circle is lawfully reconstructed. Successful contest authorizes the requested breach/use only to the extent legally achievable under the circle's other rules (installed CL, physical reach, actual mana, Haze loss, prerequisites). On failure: **F=1–4:** may try again (ordinary legal attempts and actions still apply); **F=5–8:** denied and **locked out for 10 minutes**; **F>=9:** **critical failure**, activate applicable security alarms and **lock out for 24 hours**. This explicitly extends the author's 9–12 alarm band to 13+ rather than silently leaving more serious failures unassigned. If the circle has no applicable alarm, do not conjure one for free. Authorization duration/scope after success, retries after a critical failure, lockout identification versus alternate hackers, spoofing, countermeasures and any costs/action requirements for hacking remain OPEN.

**Entropy and rounding cross-reference:** For a channel/circle limit with a fractional computed capacity, **round DOWN** at the relevant final capacity step (`floor`); do not round up a half-capacity unfamiliar circle, a ritual-scaled installed limit or a legal user ceiling. **Environmental Haze entropy is an additional independently applicable modifier** alongside cooperative and weekly-ritual entropy, **not additive percentage-point penalties that sum to 100% and erase assistants' mana**. Preserve already-approved sequential cooperative/weekly attrition: e.g. ally 100 MP -> 50 after the cooperative 50% loss -> 25 after the weekly 50% loss; in Haze V, applying the further 50% environmental loss yields **12.5 effective MP before final applicable integer rounding** (and the fixed underlying capacity/throughput ceilings remain separately enforced). Exact ordering for contexts with additional effects or individually varied local Haze is OPEN; never replace the existing cooperative-weekly result with 0.

## November 20, 2026 — APPROVED ELEMENTAL STATUS RESISTANCE FORMULA (LATEST)

**Author-approved formula for each applicable elemental status type:**

```text
Elemental SR = ceil( ((effective END + 0.65 × applicable MR)^2 × ER_element) / 0.70 )
```

Here **effective END** includes qualifying equipment bonuses and normal effective-attribute effects; **applicable MR** means the legal Magic Resistance for the relevant target/status context, subject to existing armor coverage, named materials, exceptions and separate defenses; **ER_element** is the *applicable existing named element-specific resistance multiplier* (use **1** where none applies). Do not invent a new ER source or assume a numerical ER value for an unresolved material. **Ceiling is applied once at the end** of the calculation, not separately to MR weighting, the squared term, or the elemental multiplier. MR coefficient **0.65** and divisor **0.70** are now explicitly **CANON**, superseding older 'unapproved typed SR equation' notices. This replaces the older standalone **SR = effective END²** calculation for *elemental typed statuses*; retain that earlier value only where a distinct rule specifically calls for END² outside this elemental-SR procedure.

**Normal legal elemental contact contributes 100% of its eligible delivered elemental Power to the matching status buildup**, without the old universal ×0.70 offense coefficient. Maintain the existing actual-contact, interception, medium, power, magnitude, three-turn same-element accumulation/refresh, and element-specific resistance rules. **Do not equate buildup to final injury stages or physical weapon damage**: use the actual eligible *elemental* Power reaching the status-delivery step. Direct-injury Power/damage and other spell damage calculations are not automatically multiplied by 1/0.70 or changed by this status-only normalization. Do not remove independent status application requirements, defenses, or specific elemental effects.

**Preserve attack-form exceptions relative to the new 100% ordinary buildup:** Ordinary Fire **100%**, Blue Flame **50%**, White Flame **0% and no refresh**; Wakecraft's dedicated no-damage Freeze Attack **200%** of otherwise equivalent eligible Freeze magnitude; other established spell, enchantment or status-specific adjustments apply only when already canonically defined. Applicable thresholds remain **0.5×, 1×, and 4× elemental SR** with element-specific exceptions (including Ice's separately defined 12× Entombed threshold); this update does not change severity outcomes, tick cadence, durations or any previously approved attack costs.

**Rationale and balance boundary:** Algebraically relocates the experimental 70% ordinary elemental-buildup coefficient into the precomputed defensive threshold: `Power×0.70 / OldSR = Power / (OldSR/0.70)`, apart from final rounding. E.g., effective END **50**, MR **40**, ER **1** gives `ceil((50+0.65×40)^2/0.70) = 8,252`, and a lawful elemental Power **3,000** adds **3,000** ordinary buildup. The current combat simulations were performed under their recorded historical experimental policies; **no post-approval balance rerun or simulator engine migration is claimed**. This is not a blanket approval of an independent formula for Psycraft/mental or other non-elemental status systems; their previously approved procedures remain. Interaction of Water's percentage MR erosion with recalculating ongoing typed SR and other previously open timing/rounding interactions remains **UNRESOLVED**.

**Precedence:** Any older sections here or in `00_Mystaria_Canon_Index.md`, `10_Canon_Changelog.md`, or `22_Combat_Endurance_and_Shimmer_Guard.md` saying the 0.65 MR weighting, typed elemental SR combination, or universal 0.70 elemental coefficient are 'unapproved' or that elemental SR uses END² alone are **SUPERSEDED** by this entry. ## November 20, 2026 — AUTHOR APPROVAL: Universal active-status reduction and severity downgrade (LATEST)

**Approved universal rule:** Any legal **status-buildup reduction** affects the **currently active status immediately**, not merely buildup for an untriggered future application. Across **all statuses**, lowering the target's remaining buildup **below the 4× resistance threshold** downgrades an active **Severity II** condition to **Severity I**; lowering buildup **below 1× resistance** ends the active condition entirely. Long duration does **not** make a status immune to sufficient buildup reduction. **Examples:** Reduce active 4× Charm beneath 4×: control/allegiance override ceases, leaving only the lesser 1× suggestibility and source-specific penalty while eligible buildup remains. The same two-tier threshold logic applies to other status conditions, including those reduced by Wakecraft Cleanse, nonmagical Encourage where eligible, and a future Dispel Potion once that item is developed. For future Dispel Potion, the potion's actual amount and price are **not yet approved**; for Encourage, the **INT²** reduction values remain a **provisional** proposal.

## November 19, 2026 — AUTHOR APPROVAL: Basic Manashifting shields, slow passage and barrier-breaking (LATEST)

- **Creature-anchored White shield:** A normal basic magical overshield is **directional, anchored to its target, and follows the target's torso orientation**. Knocking its bearer prone rotates its protected arc along with that torso, potentially leaving ordinary attackers an opening. It remains protective on **each eligible attack** during its duration, not a one-hit resource pool. Its normal defensive presence does **not** prevent allied attacks from leaving or entering, and allies' attacks are ignored by the shield.
- **Fast versus slow passage:** The shield is an obstacle to **fast, harmful strikes**: a rapidly swung sword meets resistance and can rebound. **Slow deliberate penetration** is physically possible: a careful foe may slowly reach a hand or sword through the field, but that slow poke does **not** become an injurious strike merely by touching flesh. Legitimate approach, bodily passage, positional control and grappling can bring attackers inside the shield; ordinary basic anchored shields **do not protect against close-range attacks within a grapple**. This does not allow arbitrarily relabeling a fast attack as a slow one; determine lawful attack effects using the ordinary combat and positioning rules. A trip or other maneuver must itself be legally delivered; the shield is not automatically bypassed because a maneuver is called a trip.
- **One target, one anchor shield:** An individual target may bear **one ordinary anchored shield** at a time. Multiple separately cast directional shields remain permissible across distinct eligible targets, with actual action, MP, duration, upkeep, and Channel Limit restrictions; do not invent a generic prohibition on multiple shield castings.
- **Timing and Power, author-selected baseline:** A basic anchored White Mana shield applies **100% of its eligible spell Power for the current turn**, against each attack within its protected arc; the **three-round** version applies **50% of eligible spell Power** against each such attack during that duration. The analogous basic **Red imbuement** adds its independently calculated spell-Power rider to **all eligible attacks** over the same one-turn 100% or three-round 50% options, never multiplies an entire physical attack. No extra attacks are generated. Existing action economy and Channel Limit still govern the casting. Larger-area and specialist Concordance improvements are not established by this entry.
- **Black counter:** An eligible Black Mana attack contacting a magical shield **subtracts its own applicable Power from the shield's remaining protective Power**, possibly unraveling it entirely. The reduced protection **stays reduced for the remainder of that casting**, rather than refilling each round. Black's previously author-selected wound-exploitation bonus is **+50% of its own base spell Power per existing bodily wound, capped at +100%**; that bodily-wound bonus does not automatically apply to a shield, which has no bodily wounds. Global additive-rider philosophy remains: independently calculated mana/spell effects do not multiply the completed combined attack.
- **Barrier wall (White and Green structure interface):** Unlike permeable creature-anchored shields, a **world-anchored structural barrier** stops physical travel from either side, blocks allied as well as hostile attacks, and is **probably transparent** (transparency is a design proposal, not locked). The user considers **two or three structural wound states**, but explicitly defers the number, thresholds, and actual break sequence until structure-creation mechanics are designed. Black reduction to magical barrier Power remains relevant; exact interaction with a partially broken structure remains open.
- **Open for calibration:** Exact directional arc (90° versus 180° was suggested but not approved); facing/turning and slow-infiltration timing checks; whether a shield's Power subtracts before or after other defenses; the detailed multiple-hit shield contest; base spell Power, MP and Channel costs; barrier geometry, transparency, structure wounds, and relationship between Green-created physical matter and White walls; Concordance's specialist superior protection. # Mystaria — Magical Media and Contested Spellcasting

**Status:** Author-approved qualitative framework, November 13, 2026. Quantitative MP/Power formulas and individual technique revisions remain pending. This document clarifies universal philosophy without changing existing SP prices, ranks, prerequisites or Channel Limit equations.

## November 14, 2026 — Turn-scale Channel Limit and prepared magical defense

**Channel Limit is the caster's shared magical throughput for the turn.** On their own turn, the caster allocates capacity between any offensive magic and **at most one specific prepared reactive defensive spell**. Medium recovery, contests and other applicable channeling also draw from the same limit. A prepared defense uses the capacity reserved on that turn, even if activated off-turn. **A caster cannot borrow from their next turn's Channel Limit.** Actual MP expenditure draws from the usual reserve at the spell's normal cost. Refreshing Channel Limit does not restore MP. Existing attribute-based Channel Limit calculations still apply.

**Declare the spell and its narrow trigger on the caster's own turn.** Valid examples are “the next attack against this specified target” or “the next attack by this specified attacker.” A trigger cannot be based on whether an attack would hit or on a generic watch for the first attack that hits or attempts to hit any ally. When the declared event occurs, the caster may activate the prepared spell at a legitimate response opportunity, provided the required physical medium, access and other spell prerequisites are still available. The spell uses normal MP costs. The prepared defense expires at the **beginning of the caster’s next turn**, whether used or unused. Protection beyond that point requires casting a separate spell with sufficient duration or eligible maintenance. Exact response timing and priority, and the interaction with complete spell seizure's prior preparation or next-action forfeiture, remain unresolved.

**Mixed physical and magical action:** A character may combine otherwise legal normal physical actions and magic in one turn, but their **applicable Channel Limit is halved, rounded down** and they may make **no more than one attack total** that turn. This does not grant bonus actions, and it is not permission to stack a physical strike and damaging spell as two independent attacks. Maneuver qualification, timing of commitment to mixed actions, and interactions with specialist rules remain unresolved.

**Mixed-action example:** A Bloomkin may fire a crossbow as their one attack and prepare an eligible magical shield for a nearby Thornguard. “The next attack targeting my knight” is a valid trigger. “The next attack that hits my knight” depends on the outcome and is invalid.

**Example:** A Wakewright with access to suitable liquid water could use a legal spell to place an ice wall on their turn, or prepare a specific defensive spell to raise it when the next attack against a named target triggers it. The wall placed in advance can change the battlefield and can be seen and maneuvered around. Either use requires an eligible technique, sufficient medium and its normal MP cost.

Other illustrative defenses include a pyromancer contesting incoming flame and a Naga using soothing Cipherlight to suppress aggressive intent, subject to successful casting and the target's resistance. These examples grant no new techniques. Each use requires its own approved prerequisites, costs and legal timing.

**Preserve existing specialized exceptions:** Drakyn Wyrmcraft Overchannel and Serpentbreaker intentionally affect Channel Limit; approved Sorcery surcharge and metamagic exceptions remain governed by their own dossiers. Warlock Manacraft uses external mana and its own Handling Capacity rules: a blanket copy of ordinary soul-channel calculations onto Warlock handling is NOT authorized. Approved MP/SP/rank costs, passive item effects, Guard/Double Strike rules and other action restrictions remain in force until directly revised.

## Environmental media are Craft-specific

Every magical Craft requires its own suitable medium, tool, or environmental condition. Greater mastery expands what a practitioner can accomplish, but **does not universally expand access to the medium**. Environmental denial, interference, and removal of required equipment must remain viable tactics even against highly experienced casters.

- **Wakecraft:** Requires accessible **liquid water** to manipulate and maintain water-based constructs. Skills such as Deepdraw allow recovery of liquid from difficult sources, including minute bodily moisture (sweat, urine, and other available fluids), at appropriate cost and with appropriate ability. Water may be frozen or vaporized, but no longer remains a directly manipulable medium in those states. Specific trained techniques may recover vapor as liquid or melt ice to regain control. A spell can require both MP and a volume of water.
- **Pyromancy (future racial Craft):** Requires a qualifying existing flame, as small as a match even for a novice's amplified flame. Individual techniques may demand more existing fire, and may consume it. An identity-defining option is inhaling a great blaze, extinguishing it into smoke, and releasing a concentrated heat projectile. Smoke is specialized ammunition reserved for appropriate learned smoke techniques. Pyromancy's exact skill branches, costs and projectile rules remain undeveloped.
- **Naga Psycraft:** Requires functioning focusing **gems** (not computational Orbs). Uses extremely bright, information-bearing **Cipherlight**, the established canon spelling, which can penetrate some materials and eyelids. More external light does not amplify Psycraft. Blocking eyesight can improve resistance at the expense of situational visual awareness; it is not automatic immunity. Appropriate sensory contact, rather than exclusively open-eye sight, can deliver effects. Target resistance persists. Specific Psycraft rank identities and effects require a separate redesign, especially Psychokinesis; do not silently delete the existing approved catalogue.

Existing matter can remain after a spell: launched water remains water, and propagated flame remains flame while supportable. Spent magical energy becomes depleted and later recycled; matter is not generically consumed with MP. Casting requirements may specify a minimum physical volume of medium; definitions of units and thresholds are pending.

## Magical contests: one Power statistic

When compatible casters contest the same ongoing spell or medium, use **existing effective spell Power**, not a separate Control statistic.

- Challenger Power **below** the original effective spell Power: fails to seize control.
- Challenger Power **at least equal** to original spell Power: **medium seizure**. The opponent's control of that medium ends. The challenger may manipulate or deny that medium according to their own Craft. On the turn they acquire medium control, **they cannot cast an additional spell**.
- Challenger Power **at least twice** the original effective spell Power: **complete spell seizure**. The challenger commands the existing spell as if its caster, within applicable Craft compatibility and without replenishing its remaining duration, resources, or medium. This requires giving up an action on the prior turn to prepare **or** surrendering their next action. Exact reactions/turn sequencing in contested initiative remains to be reconciled with combat rules.

A challenger can commit additional MP to raise the Power of the contest attempt within their ordinary casting/channel constraints. That expenditure is not recoverable for later spells, including when the attempt fails. There is no repeat bidding exchange as a default procedure. Seizing a medium is not free acquisition of the original caster's special technique; a subsequently cast new spell pays its normal activation cost. Winning a psychic duel against a third party does not automatically bypass that person's innate resistance.

### Craft-specific consequences of medium seizure

- **Wakecraft:** Seized water can be moved aside, where it may remain accessible to the original caster, or be frozen/vaporized to deny direct control. The opponent may spend their action and appropriate mana to recover it as liquid if their learned techniques allow. Melting/recovery uses channel capacity in the current turn; exact amount depends on later numerical spell rules. If an active water dragon is completely vaporized, the original maintained spell **ends**; recondensing its water does not automatically revive that spell.
- **Pyromancy:** A pyromancer seizing another's fire can inhale/extinguish it into smoke, which may then serve as ammunition for specialized learned smoke magic. When an active flamethrower loses its viable fire (for example through total immersion), its maintained spell ends.
- **Psycraft:** Ordinary successful contests dispel the opposing psychic effect rather than creating a parallel physical substance to move aside. In a third-party mental contest, a friendly psychic may bolster the target's will and reduce the effective Power of hostile influence, even if the target cannot fully escape domination. The target's resistance still applies. A temporary interruption of the Cipherlight stream need not automatically end an established mental effect; the caster may redirect/resume the stream, but destruction of the required focus ends it. Exact persistence and intervention timing are discipline-specific pending rules.
