## Full approved source (editorially normalized)

# The Games of the Gilded Coil

Approved Naga Cultural Canon — Sports, Competitive Traditions, and Imperial Entertainment

The Naga should possess one of Mystaria's most elaborate competitive cultures. Where the Thornguard celebrate individual valor through tournaments, the Naga celebrate mastery, ingenuity, control, and the ability to overcome an opponent through superior preparation.

Their enormous wealth, sophisticated Manatech, abundant enslaved labor, and control over entire artificial ecosystems allow them to organize competitions that would be impossible elsewhere.

Most importantly, these should be genuine professional sports. There are famous champions, organized leagues, wealthy sponsors, dedicated fan communities, bitter rivalries, and entire careers built around them.

The six major competitive traditions each support different kinds of adventurers.

## I. The Coilrun — The Everchanging Arena



Athletic competition · Team sport · Imperial favorite

The Coilrun is the most popular spectator sport in the Gilded Coil.

It takes place in enormous circular arenas whose terrain is continuously rearranged through Manatech.

Competitors race through an increasingly dangerous environment to seize a ceremonial golden crown from the arena's center and bring it back to their team's starting position.

Unlike an ordinary race, the course never stays the same.

Kobold engineers control enchanted platforms, gates, barriers, artificial waterways, and shifting terrain through elaborate Orb networks.

Naga competitors use their powerful serpentine bodies to climb, constrict, swim, leap between structures, and wrestle opponents for control of the crown.

A typical match involves two teams of five competitors, supported by dedicated engineering crews. Matches are divided into timed rounds, with the arena changing between rounds and at predetermined intervals.

What makes it distinctly Naga: Winning requires physical prowess, environmental awareness, adaptability, and an understanding of complicated mechanisms. A strong competitor can lose to an intelligent one who understands how the arena will change.

### Professional Rivalries

Naga Matriarchs sponsor teams bearing their Coil's colors. Competitors may include both free citizens and enslaved athletes.

Kobold engineers are celebrated as brilliant tactical minds, although their owners generally receive official credit for their accomplishments.

Certain competitions allow an engineering crew to design a limited number of arena alterations before the match, creating intense rivalries between Kobold Workshops.

### Adventurer Opportunities

- Compete as professional athletes or temporary substitutes.
- Investigate sabotage of arena machinery.
- Protect a famous competitor from a rival family's agents.
- Recover rare components needed to construct a new arena obstacle.
- Investigate allegations that a team has been receiving unauthorized information through the Coilnet.

The Coilrun functions as the Naga equivalent of a major professional sports league.

## II. The Worldspindle Trials — The War of Gardens



Terraforming competition · Environmental engineering · Aristocratic prestige

The Worldspindle Trials are perhaps the most culturally important competitions in the Naga Empire.

Held over several weeks, these contests challenge competing teams to transform designated plots of difficult terrain into flourishing ecosystems.

Every team receives a similar-sized territory, a fixed mana allocation, and access to standardized terraforming equipment.

They must use these resources to produce the most impressive, productive, and sustainable environment.

A team might turn a rocky wasteland into a beautiful forest, while its rival produces an intricate garden of warm lakes, waterfalls, and flourishing medicinal plants.

### Judging

The competition is judged on five equally weighted criteria.

| Criterion    | What judges consider                                           |
| ------------ | -------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Beauty       | Visual spectacle and artistic design                           |
| Productivity | Agricultural output and useful resources                       |
| Stability    | How well the ecosystem survives without intervention           |
| Efficiency   | Mana consumed relative to results                              |
| Harmony      | Successful integration with local Starspawn and native ecology |

The final category is especially important. A garden that collapses after its machinery is disconnected scores poorly regardless of its appearance.

Contestants are often given an additional month after construction to prove their ecosystem is sustainable.

### The Political Importance

Worldspindle Trials are not merely entertainment.

Winning teams frequently receive prestigious commissions to develop actual Imperial territory.

A Kobold Chief Artificer who wins several consecutive tournaments might become one of the most valuable engineers in the Empire.

A Naga Matriarch who sponsors a particularly successful team can secure lucrative agricultural contracts, political alliances, and influence over future infrastructure development.

### Adventurer Opportunities

- Collect rare seeds, minerals, and magical components.
- Negotiate with Starspawn that inhabit the competition grounds.
- Discover why a rival team's ecosystem is mysteriously dying.
- Protect an experimental garden from sabotage.
- Explore forgotten Imperial ruins for lost environmental engineering techniques.

This competition would be especially attractive to Heroes chosen by Surrender.

## III. The Golden Conquest — Imperial Siege Games



Tactical combat · Team competition · Military prestige

The Golden Conquest is the Naga Empire's answer to the knightly tournaments of the Canopy Kingdoms.

Instead of simple duels, competitors participate in elaborate simulated sieges.

Two teams are assigned opposing fortifications, each containing a magical command Orb.

Their objective is to seize or disable their opponent's Orb while defending their own.

The matches take place inside specially constructed arenas containing destructible walls, barricades, automated defenses, and terrain that can be modified through Manatech.

Combat is physical and genuine, although lethal attacks are prohibited and protective enchantments reduce serious injuries.

### What Makes It Different

Competitors are divided into specialized roles.

Commanders coordinate the team and determine strategy.

Vanguards are frontline combatants responsible for breaking through enemy defenses.

Engineers operate portable Manatech, repair fortifications, and construct temporary obstacles.

Infiltrators specialize in bypassing defenses and disabling enemy equipment.

Wardens protect the command Orb and coordinate defensive systems.

Naga are highly respected as Vanguards and Commanders, while Kobolds, Duskpaws, and other skilled peoples excel in specialist roles.

The games encourage mixed-race teams because different abilities offer genuine tactical advantages.

### Military Consequences

The Empire's officers take Golden Conquest results extremely seriously.

Successful competitors may receive military commissions, lucrative sponsorships, or invitations to train Imperial forces.

Foreign teams are occasionally invited.

A Thornguard knight might discover that their Concordance defenses are excellent against conventional attacks, only to find a particularly inventive Kobold has constructed an obstacle that makes approaching the battlefield almost impossible.

### Adventurer Opportunities

- Enter tournaments as mercenary competitors.
- Train a struggling team's combat specialists.
- Investigate illegal equipment modifications.
- Acquire rare defensive enchantments for an upcoming championship.
- Protect a team's command Orb from sabotage before a match.

## IV. The Thousand Locks Regatta — Mastery of the Waters



Racing · Watercraft · Engineering · Annual championship

Held primarily in Serrath Quay, the Thousand Locks Regatta is the greatest maritime sporting event in the Gilded Coil.

Competitors race through a vast network of waterways controlled by ancient Imperial floodgates.

Unlike conventional sailing races, the Regatta takes place through an environment where water levels, currents, navigable channels, and artificial obstacles change throughout the competition.

The course is regulated by standardized Manatech systems.

Racers must therefore rely on their own judgment rather than expecting predictable conditions.

### Three Major Divisions

The Golden Wake: Crewed racing vessels navigate a long course through the city's canals, competing for the fastest completion time.

The Serpent's Passage: Individual competitors race through narrow underwater tunnels, flooded ruins, and winding aquatic courses. Naga and Tidekin are particularly prominent.

The Engineer's Crown: Teams design and construct their own mana-powered racing vessels under strict limitations on materials, mana consumption, and equipment.

### The Tidekin Rivalry

Tidekin are especially accomplished competitors, combining natural swimming abilities with blue-mana Wakecraft.

Naga aristocrats sometimes resent their repeated victories.

Their own people possess powerful serpentine swimming abilities, but Tidekin frequently demonstrate better understanding of currents and water behavior.

A particularly outspoken Tidekin champion might become one of Serrath Quay's most popular public figures while simultaneously irritating nearly every Naga official responsible for the event.

### Adventurer Opportunities

- Compete in racing divisions.
- Recover a lost vessel from dangerous waterways.
- Escort experimental racing equipment.
- Investigate mysterious changes in the canal currents.
- Retrieve a valuable component from the submerged Imperial machinery.

## V. The Hollow Crown Expeditions — Competitive Archaeology



Exploration · Archaeology · Adventuring · High-risk competition

The Hollow Crown Expeditions are the Naga Empire's most dangerous prestigious competition.

Teams of professional explorers enter ancient Imperial ruins and compete to recover valuable artifacts, document discoveries, and solve lost technological mysteries.

Unlike ordinary treasure hunting, these expeditions are organized events with official registration, sponsors, and formal scoring.

Participants receive points for recovering intact artifacts, discovering previously unknown chambers, mapping dangerous areas, and returning meaningful historical information.

Destroying significant artifacts incurs penalties.

Some competitions use known ruins with carefully prepared routes. The most prestigious expeditions involve largely unexplored sites where the risks are genuine.

### Why Naga Love Them

The Naga are deeply conscious of their civilization's inheritance.

A successful expedition might recover a Serpentari relic, reveal the location of a lost Worldspindle component, or discover information concerning an extinct Great Coil.

Winning an important Hollow Crown Expedition can establish a Matriarch's prestige for decades.

### The Role of Manatech

Explorers frequently carry portable Orbs, Magic Mirrors, mana sensors, and specialized archaeological instruments.

Kobold artificers are particularly important in interpreting ancient machinery.

However, equipment becomes less reliable when teams venture far from established Imperial infrastructure.

This encourages adventurers to develop genuine survival skills.

### Adventurer Opportunities

The competition itself is essentially an adventure.

Other opportunities include guarding competing teams, recovering missing explorers, translating unfamiliar inscriptions, investigating suspicious archaeological claims, or discovering that a supposedly abandoned ruin is still inhabited.

These expeditions could also become a natural way for Heroes to uncover Mystaria's oldest secrets.

## VI. The Starwarden Pursuit — The Living Challenge



Wilderness sport · Tracking · Starspawn relationships · Competitive exploration

The Starwarden Pursuit is an elaborate wilderness competition held in specially maintained nature reserves surrounding major Imperial cities.

Teams enter a controlled landscape and attempt to locate, approach, and successfully complete challenges involving designated Starspawn.

The Starspawn are not simply targets.

They possess independent intelligence, understand mortal speech, and can communicate through Starsong.

The most prestigious versions of the competition use Starspawn that willingly participate in exchange for food, territory privileges, companionship, or other negotiated benefits.

Some Starspawn become repeat competitors, developing recognizable habits and rivalries with particular teams.

### Competition Formats

The Great Pursuit: Teams track an elusive Starspawn through difficult terrain and attempt to obtain a designated token it carries.

The Guardian's Challenge: A powerful Starspawn defends a particular location. Competitors must find a means of reaching an objective without seriously injuring the guardian.

The Living Accord: Teams compete to establish genuine cooperation with unfamiliar Starspawn and accomplish a shared task.

The Wild Circuit: Participants travel through several Starspawn territories, completing environmental and navigation challenges.

### The Naga Controversy

Conservative Naga sometimes prefer more coercive forms of Starspawn handling, while reformers emphasize voluntary partnership.

The distinction matters because Starspawn are immortal-minded and can remember mistreatment indefinitely.

A Starspawn forcibly captured for one tournament might spend the next century making the organizers' lives miserable.

Surrender's followers are especially interested in the Living Accord division.

### Adventurer Opportunities

- Enter as a competitor or Starspawn handler.
- Negotiate with a dangerous Elder Star.
- Rescue competitors trapped in an unexpected magical disturbance.
- Investigate manipulation of the reserve's environmental controls.
- Discover why several normally peaceful Starspawn have abandoned the competition grounds.

## VII. The Great Coil Circuit — Professional Competition

All six sports are connected through the Imperial institution known as the Great Coil Circuit.

It regulates major professional competitions across the Gilded Coil, providing standardized rules, athlete registration, scoring, sponsorships, and championships.

The Coilnet broadcasts matches and results through public Magic Mirrors in taverns, plazas, markets, and noble estates.

Sports become shared cultural events rather than isolated aristocratic entertainments.

A Naga family in Sathryx might follow its favorite Coilrun team with the same passion a Thornguard household devotes to a knightly order.

### The Imperial Sporting Calendar

| Season           | Major event                  | Host city                        |
| ---------------- | ---------------------------- | -------------------------------- |
| Early spring     | Worldspindle Trials          | Velmora                          |
| Late spring      | Starwarden Pursuit           | Various Inner Garden reserves    |
| Summer           | Thousand Locks Regatta       | Serrath Quay                     |
| Autumn           | Hollow Crown Expeditions     | Silent Works frontier            |
| Winter           | Golden Conquest Championship | Sathryx                          |
| Year-round       | Professional Coilrun League  | Major Imperial cities            |
| Every four years | The Grand Coil Games         | Rotating Imperial capital venues |

The Grand Coil Games are established as a prestigious multi-sport event, attracting competitors from foreign kingdoms; their precise comparative global prestige is not separately quantified.

## VIII. Rivalries, Slavery, and Celebrity

This sporting culture offers an especially interesting dimension to Naga slavery.

Enslaved athletes can become enormously famous without becoming free.

A Kobold engineer may design three championship-winning Coilrun arenas and become a household name across the Empire, yet still legally belong to a Matriarch.

A Tidekin racer might earn enormous prize money, most of which legally belongs to the Coil sponsoring them.

A particularly gifted Bloomkin technician could become the public face of a famous engineering team while having no legal claim to its inventions.

This produces an elaborate hierarchy of celebrity.

### The Three Classes of Competitors

| Class              | Legal position                       | Typical circumstances                                                                                       |
| ------------------ | ------------------------------------ | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Noble Champions    | Free Naga aristocrats                | Wealthy sponsors, political prestige, family rivalries                                                      |
| Contract Champions | Free citizens or foreign competitors | Salaries, prizes, sponsorship agreements                                                                    |
| Collared Champions | Enslaved competitors                 | Owners control contracts and usually claim prizes; competitors may receive privileges or negotiated rewards |

The most successful Collared Champions can enjoy considerable luxury.

Their owners may provide exceptional living conditions, expensive equipment, excellent training, and substantial personal attention.

But their freedom remains restricted.

Some owners promise emancipation after a championship victory. Others make the conditions increasingly difficult to fulfill.

A famous champion who publicly criticizes their owner could become the center of an enormous political scandal.

More progressive spectators might celebrate an emancipated Kobold champion as a symbol of Naga modernization.

Conservative Matriarchs may see precisely the same individual as a dangerous example of disobedience.

### The Championship Economy

The Circuit supports countless professions beyond the athletes themselves.

There are Kobold designers, commentators, Mirror technicians, Starspawn interpreters, trainers, physicians, professional judges, security guards, equipment merchants, investigators, and independent adventurers.

Vren financiers invest in teams and sponsor competitions. Duskpaw networks profit from legitimate trade and illicit gambling. Corvyn witches sometimes work as magical integrity inspectors, using Veilsight to identify prohibited enchantments.

The Mirrorwardens investigate match fixing and falsified results.

The Spindlewatch oversees competitions involving critical environmental infrastructure.

And the Scale Guard struggles to maintain public order when rival supporters decide that their team's loss must have been caused by deliberate sabotage.

## IX. Adventure Potential

The most useful feature of these sports is that they continually generate new quests without requiring an existential threat to the world.

| Quest category       | Example                                                                                 |
| -------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Athletic competition | A professional Coilrun team needs a substitute competitor                               |
| Exploration          | A Hollow Crown expedition discovers an unopened Serpentari vault                        |
| Investigation        | Someone is altering Coilnet records to conceal championship fraud                       |
| Escort               | A valuable racing Orb must reach Serrath Quay before the finals                         |
| Sabotage             | A rival Matriarch offers a secret payment to ruin another team's Worldspindle Trial     |
| Diplomacy            | A Starspawn refuses to participate unless an old grievance is settled                   |
| Protection           | A famous enslaved champion faces threats after publicly demanding freedom               |
| Engineering          | An unstable arena mechanism requires rare materials from the Silent Works               |

These are excellent sources of goddess-specific divine quests as well. Arrest might reward investigating match fixing, Surrender could reward fair competition or protecting vulnerable participants, and Baphomet might reward exposing a hypocritical champion or humiliating a corrupt aristocrat.

## X. A Distinctly Naga Sporting Identity

Of these six established sports, Coilrun, Worldspindle Trials, and Hollow Crown Expeditions are the three most culturally significant.

They express three aspects of Naga civilization:

- Coilrun: Mastery of the body and a changing environment.
- Worldspindle Trials: Mastery of nature and civilization itself.
- Hollow Crown Expeditions: Mastery of history and the inherited secrets of the Serpentari.

The Golden Conquest provides military spectacle, the Thousand Locks Regatta celebrates the Empire's waterways, and the Starwarden Pursuit tests relationships with Mystaria's magical wildlife.

Together they give the Naga a much more active, competitive, and ambitious culture. Their Matriarchs don't merely recline in luxurious palaces—they own teams, commission arenas, personally compete, feud over championships, sponsor expeditions, and spend fortunes attempting to prove that their Coil is the greatest in the Empire.

A century-old rivalry might have begun because one Matriarch's Coilrun team defeated another's by a fraction of a second.

And if the losing Matriarch has spent the intervening century preparing her revenge, that is considered entirely reasonable behavior.
