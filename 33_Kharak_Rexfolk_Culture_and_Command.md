# Kharak — Rexfolk, The Crown's Fangs

**Status:** PARTIALLY APPROVED CANON. Initial development and explicit approvals November 7, 2026. **Kharak** is the approved racial name; **Rexfolk** is a descriptive name. New precise mechanics, fashion and full language remain unresolved. Approved legal designations, cross-cultural practices and Khan epithet/maxims are below; his name, naming system, and Honored Defeat are now approved; the Serpentari monarch's identity remains open.

## Race and broad direction (author-established)
- Anthropomorphic tyrannosaur mortals closely associated with the Naga Imperium, among the largest mortal races short of Serpentari. Common, but less populous than Duskpaws.
- The Imperium's feared heavy warriors and core military muscle; dedicated work slaves comparatively rare.
- Desired red-mana affinity and highest ordinary playable-race Strength. Huge bites should rival smaller races' greatswords; enormous weapons are intended to have inherently higher damage multipliers. **Final numerical stats, affinities as mechanical entries, bite multipliers, and equipment rules remain UNRESOLVED** pending balancing and equipment review.
- Rexfolk must **not** be represented as intellectually weak. Author's minimum proposed base INT is **15**, ideally **17** if balanced; not yet an adopted full stat line. Preserve the universal 25%-increment racial modifiers rule.
- Formidable, rough, tough, intimidating warrior presentation. Detailed fashion, language, and naming customs to be developed separately.

## Approved cultural philosophy: The Burden of Freedom
- Rexfolk value demonstrated strength, discipline, strategic competence, and the ability to unify and lead. Greatness encompasses command, determination and dominion, not only muscle.
- A worthy hierarchy and allegiance are sources of identity and honor. **Freedom as unwanted separation from worthy service is profoundly insulting** in mainstream culture; clanlessness is a deep burden. This is a culturally dominant belief, not a universal psychological compulsion.
- Slavery is widely embraced as a social institution, with pride in service to a powerful Naga master, **provided superiority and fitness to command are earned**. An attempt to enslave a Rexfolk without proving such authority can provoke furious resistance.
- Independent clans survive; many function as mercenaries, and some as bandits. After defeat or a legitimate demonstration of superior power, even independent clans may offer service.
- The philosophical framing approved by the author: *the weak serve the strong, the strong serve the greater, and the greatest must bear responsibility for those beneath them*. The attendant cultural dishonor of clanless freedom is approved in substance; literal texts and precise quotations remain creative material.

## Approved military rank framework
The Rexfolk use ancestral clan-derived titles for professional Imperial roles:
| Title | Approximate function |
|---|---|
| Fang | Private / ordinary warrior |
| Bloodfang | Veteran warrior |
| Clawleader | Sergeant / small unit leader |
| Warhowler | Lieutenant / multiple squads |
| Warclaw | Captain / warband |
| Warchief | Colonel / several warbands |
| High Warchief | General / army or major regional command |

**First Fang** is a separate position: the highest-ranking Rexfolk directly serving a given Matriarch, irrespective of the holder's military rank or her household's scale. A provincial Matriarch may have a High Warchief as First Fang; a modest household might have a Clawleader. Precise troop sizes, insignia, and promotion rules remain open.

## Approved history
The ancestral Rexfolk were formidable nomadic conquerors and accomplished warriors, capable of rapidly uniting armies but repeatedly destabilized by clan rivalries, betrayal, and succession struggles. Their problem was sustaining political unity, not lack of intelligence.
- The ancient Serpentari defeated them, devastated or destroyed their homeland and held them under severe **physical and magical restraint**, much as early Drakyn were constrained, treating them as living weapons.
- Over generations, many Rexfolk recast this catastrophe as the beginning of lasting order and even thanked the Serpentari; this is an ideological memory, not necessarily impartial history. Independent clans can remember differently.
- Naga eventually developed closer ties with Rexfolk through cultural compatibility and practical necessity, integrating them as soldiers and commanders. **Approved mutual cultural influence:** Rexfolk introduced a norm of blunt competence-based military criticism, deeply personal warrior oaths and battle-proven prestige into some Naga military households, while Naga introduced recorded ranks, durable Coil-centered allegiance, formal challenge procedures, strategic logistics and legal precedent into Kharak commands. Military Naga may speak bluntly among officers and Kharak commanders may be courtly negotiators. Luxury armor ornaments, warband insignia and military trophy accessories are approved cultural directions, but no compulsory garment design or mechanical effects are set.
- The author proposes a legendary near-unifier, a great Khan who founded the practice of honorable leadership challenges, whose eventual defeat by the Serpentari came through a noncombat demonstration of strength associated with the earliest Imperial sports. **His identity, exact competition, circumstances, historical chronology and relationship to existing approved six sports are not yet canonized in detail.**

## Approved Right of Challenge, open procedure
- The **First Fang directly beneath a Matriarch** holds both the right and moral duty to challenge clear failures of leadership; cowardly silence amid catastrophic incompetence is shameful.
- Challenges may arise from military failures, bankruptcy, damaged reputation affecting leadership, or inability to discharge material obligations, and should be tested against **documented evidence, defined criteria and precedent**, not just subjective displeasure.
- This applies to small Matriarch-led gangs as well as great armies. Legal proceedings can lead to **Imperial reorganization** of service, including transfer of a small unit to a more suitable Matriarch or competitive offers of service for a major Warchief.
- **Desertion** remains rebellion; properly sanctioned **reorganization within the Imperial hierarchy** is distinct. Spurious and overeager challenges can incur demotion or punishment.
- **Drakyn** are particularly favored intermediaries and judges, in keeping with their oath culture and transnational overlapping territories. They do not automatically replace the Conclave of Coils, Scale Register or existing Coil Tribunals.
- **Approved designations:** The **Court of Fangs** is the challenge forum; an **Oathspeaker** argues evidence and precedent for parties; a **Crown Arbiter** judges such disputes, with Drakyn especially respected in the role. Challenges require demonstrable rather than merely rumored failures; review can address substantial financial and household failures as well as military disasters. Reorganization may transfer warriors and registered service but not automatically convey all estates or debts. Greater contested cases may receive Drakyn arbitration; smaller disputes need not require a dragon's physical attendance. Exact thresholds, appeals, enforcement, dragon jurisdiction and transfer documentation remain unresolved.

## November 7 — Approved families, religion, tattoos and age-of-service custom

### Family organization
- A **Hearth** is a biological/adoptive family household of parents, children, close relatives and chosen carers; a **Warclan** comprises multiple Hearths and oath-sworn, sometimes unrelated members. The Imperial **Coil** is the superordinate allegiance for serving Warclans. Clan affiliation and household obligations are intensely meaningful; severing them is neither casual nor automatically socially accepted.
- Hatchlings are cherished and ferociously protected. Adults commonly share rearing, strategy education and family history; mothers and fathers can both lead families and military units by proven competence, not an exclusively sex-based status. A parent's physically intimidating reputation may unintentionally isolate a child from peers.
- Harm or threatened injury to a Kharak hatchery is a particularly explosive crisis, even for normally rule-minded parents. A Matriarch allowing such harm may face a desperate, furious challenge from affected parents; this **does not automatically give all parents formal First Fang standing** or guarantee impartial process. Whether a special collective petition or emergency challenge mechanism exists remains open.
- Expulsion from a Warclan can bring severe social disgrace and clanlessness but need not erase bonds of parenthood or relatives' private loyalty.

### Oaths, status and childhood exemption
- Kharak are **exempt from hereditary enslavement**, an expressly approved exception to ordinary Imperial maternal-status inheritance. An underage Kharak is not automatically owned merely because their parents are.
- The **First Oath** occurs on becoming a young warrior, ordinarily sometime in the teenage years: acceptance of adult-like responsibilities toward the Warclan, but **not** Imperial enslavement.
- The **Second Oath** occurs upon reaching age for military service. A Kharak publicly embraces service to a particular Coil; this is normally the proudest day of youth and is marked with the traditional **Tatao**. No universal numerical age, form of legal registration, or permitted coercion safeguards have been established.
- Rare individuals decline or walk away from this path, remaining clanless or seeking new allegiance, including adventuring. They are not thereby automatically enslaved. Others may opportunistically court, manipulate or attempt capture, but involuntary service is not rendered legitimate by their vulnerability.

### Tatao (singular and collective traditional name)
- **Tatao** are Kharak traditional tattoos openly proclaiming their allegiance. Their designs visually coordinate with their collars and identify both their owning **Coil** (most prominently) and **Warclan**; their skin displays whose cause they serve.
- They signify chosen or socially expected belonging, rank and durable obligation rather than impartial historical witnessing. This distinguishes them from Corvyn tattoo and Ink Name traditions.
- Transfers of service are serious and require deliberate handling of ancestral/previous tattoo marks. No precise design language, body placements, magical properties, removal methods or mandatory re-inking procedures are settled. **Tattoo and collar are not substitutes for Scale Register legal records**.

### Religion
- **Arrest** is the principal deity venerated by Imperial Kharak, whose fairness, vows, disciplined challenge and accountability are interpreted through their own honor-based hierarchy. More independent and wild Warclans commonly honor **Baphomet**, especially her aspects of revenge and freedom from unearned dominion.
- Neither faith negates Kharak intelligence, individual dissent, or the existing approved beliefs of Naga and Kobolds.

### Names and legendary continuity
- **Kharak names:** strong, short, consonant-rich personal names followed by stable Warclan names; earned heroic or military epithets may follow. Mates can retain independent clan names or join through formal allegiance. Both sexes may draw from overlapping name patterns.
- Approved examples from initial proposal: Kharzug, Thorgat, Borghan, Durgan, Varkul, Ghorak; Vraksha, Kharza, Rhazka, Tharka, Zhurra, Vorga. Warclans: **Vornak, Kharvul, Thrazgor, Gorvash, Dravok**. Example meanings and histories remain proposals unless explicitly established.
- **Kharzug Vornak, the Khan of the Unbroken Word** is the approved founding strategist and philosopher; he is most commonly cited by clan name **Vornak**. His descendants still serve the **highest-ranked Naga Matriarch** (normally the High Matriarch) and their Warclan is among the most coveted, distinguished and prestigious in the world. Exact succession and household ownership formalities are not established.
- **The Honored Defeat is approved:** Vornak willingly honored the outcome of a fair, nonlethal strategy contest against a Serpentari sovereign, ancestral in spirit to today's Golden Conquest. The victor kept the literal settlement but the Kharak nevertheless endured humiliating restraints and long captivity. The Serpentari opponent's name is NOT YET CANON.

## November 7, 2026 — Hatchbane (APPROVED)

**Hatchbane** is the approved Kharak name for the especially reviled crime of harming a Kharak egg or hatchling, reflecting the Warclan's paramount obligation to protect its young. A perpetrator is called a **hatch-traitor** (author-approved spelling). The term **Hatchbane** supersedes the unapproved proposed crime name *Hatchtreason*; the perpetrator term *hatch-traitor* supersedes *Hatchtraitor*. The full statutory scope, culpability standards (intentional injury, kidnapping, reckless endangerment, negligent harm), sentencing, and relation to emergency Court of Fangs petitions remain **OPEN**; the existence of the crime does not imply every missing egg or accident automatically establishes guilt.

**Terminology correction (November 7, 2026):** The author corrected the crime name from the typo *Harchband* to **Hatchbane**. **Hatch-traitor** remains the perpetrator term.
