# Kobold Fashion in the Gilded Coil

**Status:** CANON for explicitly approved foundational and Presentation/Demonstration Dress material; remaining optional ceremonial details are marked **PROPOSAL / NOT YET CANON**.
**Last author approval:** November 6, 2026.
**Related files:** `02_Races.md`, `11_Visual_Style_Guide.md`, `25_Imperial_Collar_Culture_and_Restraints.md`, `18_The_Gilded_Coil_Imperial_Gazetteer.md`, `10_Canon_Changelog.md`.

## I. Approved character of Kobold fashion — CANON

Kobolds prize technical accomplishment and practical equipment as features of style. The Imperial baseline permits mature and provocative silhouettes, but Kobolds express it through modern-fantasy engineering dress: fitted laboratory coats, short high-low skirts, utility belts, goggles, tool straps, restrained leather or latex accessories and personal instruments. Their approved red Kobold engineer illustration is a principal clothing reference, not an approval of oversized character anatomy. Kobolds are small-bodied; avoid accidentally depicting them as human-sized or seven-foot tall.

**Practicality remains central even among prestigious household specialists.** A distinguished enslaved Kobold may wear an impeccably maintained mechanic's outfit, professor's clothing or other scholarly/technical garments rather than impractical aristocratic costume. Petticoats are a prime example; tailored jackets, waistcoats, short academic coats, layered skirts, decorative cuffs and eyewear are compatible wardrobe options, but their exact prevalence is not prescribed as universal fashion.

**Spectacles are especially common in casual Kobold dress.** Their eyes are naturally poor at long-distance vision, adapted to ancestral cave environments where seeing far was less important. This is a qualitative racial biology statement, not an approved universal visual-acuity formula or mechanical attribute penalty. Some Kobolds imprinted upon keen-sighted peoples, such as the Corvyn, are exceptions; the exact mechanism, extent, timing or statistical effect of such an exception is **UNRESOLVED**. Do not assume imprinting grants every ability or specific DEX/SPD bonuses.

**Free Kobolds often wear markedly more worn, haggard and self-modified equipment than enslaved household specialists.** With no masters imposing uniform-maintenance policies, their laboratory coats are commonly torn, burnt, patched or stained while they focus on projects they regard as more important. This is a cultural tendency, not proof of status or a rule that all free Kobolds are unkempt. Well-maintained uniforms can belong to enslaved specialists serving prestigious households. Individual exceptions are normal.

**Craftsmanship and achievement are sources of prestige.** The author's approval retains the proposed idea of cherished personal tools worn or carried visibly as practical ornaments and reminders of achievement. No universal tool-ranking system, named award, ritual or ownership law has been established by implication.

## II. Existing Orb interface and legal distinctions — CANON

An enslaved Kobold's ordinary functional Orb Collar is a simple smooth metal neck band with **one central colored Orb**, without outward buckle, clamp, exposed lock or ring hardware. It is a tool and interface as well as Imperial identification. Orbcraft-capable Kobolds can interpret a dense information stream through it; a small local aurora during paw-gestures may give untrained viewers a soft headache. This is not the full Coilnet Rainbow. Technicians normally keep a compatible Orb on their person; **free Kobold professionals particularly favor Orb-bearing bracers** rather than obligatory identification collars.

The extremely expensive Mirror, Trance and Coilsting Collars are separate specialized Imperial restraints, not standard additions to every engineer's outfit. A maintained uniform, embroidered household insignia or functional collar does not alter a person's legal rights.


## III. Presentation Dress and Demonstration Dress — CANON (approved November 6, 2026)

Kobold engineers, scholars and inventors commonly distinguish **Presentation Dress**, which honors the audience, from **Demonstration Dress**, which honors the work. This is an established **cultural convention**, not an official uniform class, legal dress code, mandated purchase or standardized item set. Individual preference, occupation, household policies and the occasion all matter.

### Presentation Dress: The Respectable Scholar

Presentation Dress is appropriate for formal lectures, academic ceremonies, examinations, noble audiences, significant public presentations, and unveiling inventions before dignitaries. It communicates intelligence, professional prestige and respect for the audience. It is typically clean, composed and deliberately maintained; lavish wealth is not required.

Typical elements include well-tailored academic or short coats, fitted blouses or waistcoats, neat petticoats and layered short skirts, polished eyewear (including ordinary glasses and specialized lenses), and a prominent well-kept tool or personally designed instrument. Within Imperial norms its silhouette may remain sensual. Household-affiliated enslaved professionals may wear maintained or owner-provided outfits; free professors may preserve a single carefully repaired formal outfit. Formal Orb equipment follows existing legal and practical conventions: a collared worker's central Orb Collar remains functional, while free technicians commonly favor Orb bracers.

A significant personally cherished tool or invention may be displayed more **symbolically** at a ceremony, honoring an achievement. A highly polished tool is not proof of competence; prestige rests in actual accomplishments.

### Demonstration Dress: The Working Genius

Demonstration Dress is the practical workwear selected when a device must be tested, repaired or operated: workshops, hands-on examinations, engineering competitions, invention demonstrations and hazardous technical showcases. It is selected for **function rather than cleanliness** and is not a special outfit that must be purchased.

Common features include weathered lab coats, scorch marks, repairs, patched pockets, secured skirts or petticoats, rolled or protected sleeves, protective gloves, goggles or spectacles, utilitarian belts/harnesses, and genuinely useful devices or custom modifications. Injuries and mechanical hazards still warrant appropriate safety equipment. Free Kobolds may especially retain battered, personally modified coats because nobody forces uniform replacement. Enslaved Kobolds can be equally accomplished but may be required to maintain a more presentable uniform.

**Authentic wear** acquired through work is respected in some Kobold professional circles. Artificial burns or torn cloth do not confer professional prestige, and damaged clothing is not a guarantee of expertise; careless or inexperienced workers can damage their coats too.

### The Working Coat

A Kobold may treasure a lab coat used through years of research, adaptation and experimentation. A collection of personal patches, replaced sleeves and intelligently positioned pockets may be more important to its wearer than its market value or visual condition. Replacing such a coat can mean discarding familiar customized equipment and cherished personal history. Wealthy, celebrated free engineers may wear very worn coats by choice, while still owning an immaculate formal outfit.

There is no mandatory age, patch count, award or official designation for a Working Coat.

### The Proud Tool and professional prestige

Personally significant tools and instruments may serve both as working implements and markers of achievement. Their display in **Presentation Dress** is more commemorative; in **Demonstration Dress**, what matters most is that the instruments work and can be used. A Naga patron might commission an expensive golden replica as a gift, but it could be more useful as a symbol than as a functional tool. Neither ownership of a decorative tool nor disdain for ornament is a universal Kobold value.

### Cultural tensions and variation

Naga authorities may expect immaculate clothing at court or a noble-sponsored demonstration, even while a Kobold engineer prefers damaged but carefully adapted workwear. Under slavery, an owner may control uniform maintenance and presentation; free Kobolds enjoy greater choice in what they wear. Such distinctions reflect **autonomy and institutional control**, not innate talent or an ironclad visual test of legal status.

A careful free Kobold can dress immaculately; a brilliant enslaved engineer may be covered in soot. Lectures generally invite polished Presentation Dress, while working demonstrations favor practical attire, but the distinction is customary rather than an enforceable binary.

> Presentation Dress demonstrates the respect one has for an audience. Demonstration Dress demonstrates the respect one has for the work.

## IV. Further ceremonial and social-detail proposals — NOT YET CANON

The following options **remain unapproved** even though the Presentation/Demonstration distinction is now canon. These are suggestions for later development rather than fixed ceremonial requirements.

1. **Academic Gala as a named celebration:** An institutionally recognized festival or gala with miniature functioning replicas of famous inventions and distinctive formal observances.
2. **Formalized Proud Tool etiquette:** A strict cultural taboo against wearing a tool one cannot use, universal workshop judgments, or set honors determined by a tool's appearance.
3. **Patch-based prestige:** Recognized coat-patch meanings, uniform inspection rules, or rank inferred from repairs. The underlying free-versus-enslaved maintenance tendency and attachment to working coats are already canon.
4. **Mandatory professor ensemble:** A prescribed set of waistcoats, lace, ribbon bows, skirts, magnifying lenses, and satchels for Kobold academics. These are valid optional fashion examples, not a required costume.

## V. Limitations

No compulsory Kobold fashion laws or demonstration uniforms, numeric eyesight penalty, new racial attribute multipliers, specific imprint transfer mechanism, universal ceremonial rituals, standardized tool-emblems, additional Orbcraft ranks or numerical equipment effects have been approved by this fashion ruling. Existing racial statistics, Craft rules and imprinted-name customs remain authoritative in their owning references. Individual illustrations are not approved merely because they resemble these clothes.
