Choose the person you want to play, then give them the tools to do it. A race shapes your body and heritage; attributes describe your broad capabilities; training develops particular strengths; skills give you things to do.

## Start with an idea

Pick a role you would enjoy: an armored protector, a nimble duelist, an archer, a spellcaster, a craftsperson, or a mixture. You do not need to settle every future purchase now. Think about how you will help the group both in and out of combat.

## Choose your race and background

Read [the races of Mystaria](/world/races/) for their bodies, cultures, and magical traditions. Your race affects more than a stat bonus: hands, size, movement, senses, and access to particular Crafts can change how you use equipment and approach problems.

With your GM, record your race's starting attributes and racial modifiers. Also decide where you grew up, your legal status, and your relationship to the society around you. [The three great regions](/world/regions/) provide very different beginnings.

## Understand the five attributes

| Attribute | What it helps you do | Build considerations |
|---|---|---|
| **Strength — STR** | Hit hard, carry equipment, and contribute to physical accuracy. | Useful for powerful weapons and for making room in your loadout. |
| **Dexterity — DEX** | Aim precisely, evade, handle refined equipment, and perform delicate physical work. | Supports both offense and defense; individual weapons may have handling requirements. |
| **Endurance — END** | Have more natural HP, carry equipment, and contribute to evasion. | Supports durability. Mana-generating races also use effective END in their natural MP rules. |
| **Intelligence — INT** | Understand and use complex magic; contribute to magical accuracy. | Check the prerequisites of the actual Craft you want to learn. |
| **Skill** | Earn additional Skill Points and Training Points when you allocate points to it. | Gives more room to develop abilities and trained statistics. |

Attributes describe broad potential. A high attribute does not teach an unlearned spell, grant a specialized mundane skill, or replace the physical equipment an action needs.

## Keep three separate budgets

| Budget | Spend it on | What to record |
|---|---|---|
| **Attribute points** | Your base attributes. | Where each allocated point went, especially points invested in Skill. |
| **Skill Points — SP** | Abilities, Crafts, mundane skills, and useful options. | Each purchase's actual cost, rank, and prerequisites. |
| **Training Points — TP** | Improvements to individual derived statistics, such as accuracy, evasion, damage, or HP. | Purchased ranks, their costs, effective benefits, and remaining TP. |

At level 1, you begin with **2 unspent attribute points, 2 SP, and 2 TP**, before any purchases or additional earned Skill bonuses. These are in addition to your racial starting attributes and applicable racial entitlements. Allocate the attribute points before calculating your final budgets; each point allocated to Skill grants another 2 SP and 2 TP. Keep all three remaining balances visible.

SP and TP are independent pools. Spending one does not spend the other. [The training guide](/learn/training/) explains income, escalating purchases, and how to plan a useful build.

## Buy a few things you will actually use

Choose your main way to contribute. Then cover its practical weaknesses: a damage dealer needs a way to land attacks; a defender still needs something useful to do; a caster needs lawful mana, learned abilities, and any required medium or implement.

Read [magic and disciplines](/learn/magic/) before buying magical skills. Different traditions have different access rules, resources, and costs. Having enough SP is only part of qualifying.

## Choose weapons, armor, and other equipment

Pick a weapon with a clear job. Check its damage, reach, attack forms, hands, Weight, and handling requirements. Armor reduces incoming damage but uses part of the same carrying budget as your other gear. A shield needs its own lawful handling and defensive use.

Your shared equipped **Weight capacity is max(0, floor(20 + effective STR + effective END))**. Add up the whole equipped loadout before calling it legal. Do not confuse a weapon's independent rating with your personal attributes or training.

Start with [equipment explained](/learn/equipment/), then [enchantments](/learn/enchantments/) if you are buying magical gear.

## Finish your sheet

- Record effective attributes after racial and other eligible modifiers; retain fractions until a calculation asks you to round.
- Record ordinary maximum/current HP and, if you are a qualifying Hero, Shimmer Reserve separately.
- Record accuracy, evasion, damage, armor, Weight, and your purchased training.
- Record MP by its actual source and color, plus any Leywell reserves and Soul Strain.
- Check skill prerequisites, equipment requirements, and the resources needed for your usual actions.
- Give yourself a reason to travel with the party.

Your first session needs a playable person with a few dependable choices. You can grow into the grand, improbable plan later.

Read [combat at the table](/learn/combat/) for the two-action turn, attacking, pressure, and preparation. Keep [health and defense](/learn/health/) nearby for damage, Shimmer, and recovery.


## Practical strength

A reference one-time waist-height lift in pounds is 100 × (max(0, effective STR + 10)/15)². Actual grip, leverage and anatomy matter.
