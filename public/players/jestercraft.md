Jestercraft begins with **living Regalia**: a magical outfit with its own voice and relationship to its wearer. Its fabric and Stitches are real resources with positions, states, limits, and vulnerabilities.

## Six discipline trees

| Tree | What it develops |
|---|---|
| **Hemweaving** | Perception, masquerade, puppetry, and the divided performer. |
| **Curtainwall** | Protection, energy attunement, reservoirs, and recovery. |
| **Rag Doll** | Flexibility and the wearer's unusual thread anatomy. |
| **Tailor's Measure** | Reality-altering ribbon properties. |
| **Black Masquerade** | A second skin of magical fabric. |
| **Seamcraft** | Predatory thread and Silk Road movement. |

## Fabric on the battlefield

Track what is worn, extended, Draped into the environment, Frayed, recovered, or lost. Regalia does not supply unlimited cloth everywhere at once. Binding needs a lawful physical fabric source and valid contact; its medium and the condition it causes remain separate things to track.

Cover, shielding, cutting, burning, protected space, and the wearer's actual position matter. A clever configuration is useful because it works with those physical constraints.

## Purchases and resources

Jestercraft uses its own component ledger, including 3-SP modifiers and 4-SP applications. Do not replace that structure with a generic Craft price. Fabric/Stitch quantities, recovery rules, and declared actions still govern what a learned component can do.

The current fabric-capacity and several quantitative interaction rules remain incomplete. Use the table's declared Regalia sheet rather than adopting a proposed capacity example as a free baseline.

## Conditions and counterplay

Binding disables a specified part or restrains the whole body according to severity. The fabric remains physically relevant: removing or damaging the actual medium can be meaningful counterplay. [Conditions](/learn/conditions/) explains the shared HP-system framework.
