Where you come from affects whom you can trust, what you can own, where you may travel, and what others expect of you. A society's public ideals and the choices of an individual person can differ sharply.

## Power in the three regions

| Region | Organization | Social tension |
|---|---|---|
| **Canopy Kingdoms** | Sovereign kingdoms, noble houses, regional High Kings, and negotiated confederation. | Prosperity and protective ideals coexist with hereditary enslavement. |
| **Imperium** | Centralized hierarchy with advanced institutions and infrastructure. | Free-citizen abundance rests on unequal rights and extensive exploitation. |
| **Scarlands** | Rival courts, guilds, private/coercive powers, and contractual hierarchy. | Exceptional wealth concentration and precarious freedom. |

## Canopy citizenship

**Avowed** are hereditary free citizens of any race. Emancipation, patronage, distinguished service, or asylum can lead to citizenship. Freedom is recognized across the kingdoms in principle; residence is guaranteed through the patron house. Permanent relocation still depends on receiving-house and property rules.

Avowed status does not automatically confer nobility. Prejudice, house feuds, and local laws can complicate travel even where freedom is recognized.

Enslaved households may have private family apartments, leisure, and some economic participation, while still lacking fundamental freedom. Those comforts vary and do not erase ownership or dependency.

## Religion

Arrest, Surrender, and Baphomet represent different concerns and disagree about authority, love, punishment, power, and enslavement. People's worship, institutions, and personal convictions need not align neatly.

Religious language can carry deep cultural meaning. A character's everyday faith should follow their upbringing and individual views rather than a mandatory racial stereotype.

## Naming and speech

Races and regions have established naming customs, idioms, compliments, insults, and taboos. Treat them as cultural tendencies. A person's upbringing, profession, social position, and temperament influence how they speak.

In ordinary speech, magical energy is commonly named by color: someone asks for “blue” rather than a technical quantity of “mana.” Use technical language when characters are actually discussing mechanics, scholarship, or engineering.

For a character name, consult the [approved namebook on GitHub](https://github.com/MyStalker/mystaria-codex/blob/main/24_Racial_Naming_Conventions_and_Namebook.md) with your GM. The [linguistic reference](https://github.com/MyStalker/mystaria-codex/blob/main/12_Languages_and_Linguistic_Customs.md) supplies the detailed cultural expressions.

## Clothing and identity

- **Bannerweave** coordinates Thornguard and Bloomkin heraldry; **Twinmantle** names especially matched outfits.
- **Lastwear** is Duskpaw clothing visibly associated with a previous wearer's death, often within a broader salvage aesthetic.
- Kobold **Presentation Dress** suits formal demonstrations; a cherished **Working Coat** may show years of practical repairs.
- Tidekin **Kin-knots** connect dress with Raft ties; aquatic clothing respects their movement and bodies.
- Kharak **Tattao** can continue visually into clothing or armor patterns. Their industrial and martial dress varies by work and Coil.

These are useful starting points for your own character. They do not establish a compulsory uniform or personality.
