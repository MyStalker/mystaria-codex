An enchantment can improve your attributes, add a magical effect, or combine a smaller attribute benefit with an effect. Choose the mode for what you want the item to do, then check how it is powered.

## The three modes

{{table:enchantment-modes}}

These modes are mutually exclusive choices. Physical Quality, enchantment strength, occupied slots, and Leywell capacity are distinct parts of the item.

## Attribute bonuses and Soul Strain

Blank and Mixed attribute bonuses remain active while the item is legally equipped and supplying its effect. Their ongoing cost is **Soul Strain**. You do not reactivate those stat bonuses every five rounds.

Soul Strain and MP are different limits. Check your actual strain rules before filling your loadout with stat-enhancing items. Centaurs receive full normal equipment attribute bonuses and apply their racial reduction to aggregate equipment strain, with the established once-only upward rounding.

## Powered magical effects

Activated effects use compatible stored **Leywell MP**. The usual powered window is five rounds unless the named effect specifies otherwise. The magical effect itself does not add Soul Strain merely because it is active; a Mixed item's separate stat bonus still does.

Leywells store particular colors. Their standard capacity tiers are **10 / 20 / 30 / 40 / 50 MP**. Refilling uses the appropriate transfer rules and physical access. Capacity is not a new spell repertoire and does not waive a real-medium requirement.

## Does switching it on cost an action?

| What the enchantment does | Action treatment |
|---|---|
| Modifies something you can already lawfully do, such as igniting a blade or enabling a movement mode | Switching it on is action-free during your turn, while still spending required MP. The actual attack or movement follows its own rules. |
| Performs an independent attack, movement, or active defense | Requires its specified action commitment. It uses the same two-action turn and one-offensive-attack limit as other actions. |
| Performs a prepared active defense | Commit the required action(s) and a narrow, outcome-independent trigger on your own turn. |

Having several enchanted items does not grant extra attacks. Two active ring slots remain two active ring slots; changing equipment involves real item interactions.

## Effect strength and slots

For the generic weapon enchantment catalogue, the number of **legally occupied slots** determines the listed effect strength. Full values are stronger than Mixed values. Use the values printed for the named enchantment.

Some names require exactly three slots; Rank Measure requires one. A price formula does not create installation permission, extra slots, or a missing color/MP assignment.

::: detail Finished-item Gem prices for generic active effects
For an otherwise legal host priced at **P**, with **s** actual occupied slots, the completed Mixed item costs **P × (1 + 0.15s)**; Full costs **P × (1 + 0.20s)**. These totals include the host and legitimate built-in installation.

At six legal slots the totals are 1.9P and 2.2P. Use s = 3 for a three-slot-only effect. This formula is for those generic active effects, not Blank stat-only items, and does not make mana refills free.
:::

## Choose an effect for your usual play

An effect is most useful when you can satisfy its trigger repeatedly and lawfully. A reach effect needs the right geometry. A paired-weapon effect needs both weapons usable. An elemental effect needs genuine contact or delivery and must respect shields, insulation, and resistance.

Browse [the weapon enchantment catalogue](/reference/weapon-enchantments/) for eligibility, triggers, Mixed/Full values, limits, and counterplay. Some installation and conversion details still need GM confirmation in the HP/training rules.
