# The Silks and Chains of the Eternal Coil
## Naga Fashion, Adornment, and Ceremonial Dress

**Status:** APPROVED CANON — November 6, 2026.
**Region:** The Gilded Coil / Naga Imperium.
**Primary people:** Naga.
**Authority:** Explicit author approvals of the Imperial fashion philosophy, complete Naga wardrobe dossier, and final circlet, ceremonial binding, and political-interpretation clarifications.
**Related canon:** `02_Races.md`, `11_Visual_Style_Guide.md`, `18_The_Gilded_Coil_Imperial_Gazetteer.md`, `25_Imperial_Collar_Culture_and_Restraints.md`. This dossier owns Naga fashion specifically, without replacing other peoples' clothing and collar rules.

> The Naga have not forgotten their chains. They have gilded them.

## I. Imperial Fashion Philosophy

Naga fashion is sensual, carefully composed, and intensely status-conscious. Exposed skin is not inherently shameful; bare shoulders, backs and midriffs, revealing necklines, fitted tops and short silk drapes can all be respectable public and formal attire. Imperial standards of decorum focus more on quality, composure, status, and the suitability of ornaments and insignia than on covering the body. Fashion is highly provocative by many outside standards but does not automatically carry sexual or enslavement meaning.

**The body is not shameful.** Sensual silhouettes are culturally normal, and individual preferences vary.

**Adornment demonstrates standing.** Quality, craftsmanship, inherited pieces and the correct presentation of status matter more than the quantity of fabric. A wealthy enslaved specialist can have more luxurious attire than a poor free citizen, without gaining freedom thereby.

**Chains remember.** Naga adornment transforms the visual language of their ancestors' enslavement into symbols of lineage, prosperity, and present self-rule. This is a cultural interpretation, not proof that every Naga endorses the current institution of slavery.

Naga fashion commonly favors short, dancer-like silks instead of voluminous robes; fitted chest wraps and tops; exposed midriffs and shoulders; short hip panels; sculpted waist pieces; metal armlets, necklaces and tail jewelry; circlets; and both delicate and ostentatiously heavy chains and manacles. Luxury emphasizes extensive jewelry rather than fabric bulk. Refined harness motifs **are not** the defining Naga fashion influence; historical chains and manacles are.

## II. Historical Origin and Contemporary Meaning

The Serpentari created Naga as privileged overseers, tutors, administrators and managers. Though ranked above many enslaved peoples, ancient Naga themselves remained owned by Serpentari aristocrats. The fall of the old empire left their descendants to become rulers of the Gilded Coil.

The history of servitude is reflected in modern fashion. Ornamented chains and manacles explicitly reclaim forms once associated with captivity. In present-day court fashion they proclaim that the Naga are slaves to no one. Naga of different political convictions interpret this legacy differently; this is not a universally agreed celebration.

Among traditionalists, such ornaments often celebrate inherited authority and liberation from direct Serpentari bondage. The **Sovereign Coil** may stress independence and self-mastery; **Keepers of the Empty Throne** may stress historical continuity and reverence for their creators. **Unbound Daughters** may challenge the contradiction of glorifying one's own liberation while placing other peoples in chains. These are political tendencies, not compulsory beliefs of every member or newly granted abilities.

## III. Basic Silhouette and Garments

Naga have a humanoid upper body and a long serpentine lower half. Dress is shaped accordingly; they do not wear ordinary bipedal skirts, stockings or trousers as though they had legs.

**Chest wraps and fitted tops.** Common styles include short crossed silk bands, close-fitting tops, structured bodices, and ornamental chest pieces. Coverage varies by preference and occasion.

**Short hip drapes.** Narrow or asymmetrical silk panels descend briefly from the waist or transition into the tail, recalling dancer's costume elements, not floor-length robes. Expensive cloth often features concentrated borders and carefully placed detailing.

**Waist and torso ornaments.** Chain girdles, metal belts, shaped plates, gemstone settings and carefully arranged short drapery frame the torso.

**Tail adornments.** Light chains, ornamental bands, hanging gems and decorative fittings frequently accent the upper tail; most of the tail remains uncovered. Heavy ceremonial ornaments may be impractical by deliberate design.

**Arm and neck jewelry.** Wrist and upper-arm cuffs, decorative manacles, necklaces, chokers and torques are widespread. Their materials, finish and symbolism distinguish chosen fashion from compulsory restraints.

Sensuality remains common in everyday streetwear, professional outfits and formal court clothing. It is neither a mandatory uniform nor evidence of an individual's profession, legal status, availability or sexual role.

## IV. Fashion Across Social Class

### Poor Naga

A poor Naga can remain recognizably Imperial in simple, flattering chest wraps, short drapes, copper or brass fittings, modest beads, and one or two cherished or inherited chains. Financial hardship limits material luxury, **not** the culture's comfort with revealing garments. Impoverished Matriarchs traditionally preserve at least a simple copper circlet.

### Citizens, Merchants and Professionals

Established citizens tend toward cleaner tailoring, practical garments adapted to their professions, moderate jewelry, light chains, armlets and tidy waist ornaments. A merchant may wear conspicuous gemstones; a laboratory worker may dress for instruments and safety. Practical attire does not make a person unfashionable.

### Noble and Wealthy Naga

Aristocratic clothing has the silhouette of an especially richly adorned court dancer, not a ruler buried in flowing cloth. Silks remain short and deliberate; jewels, elaborate cuffwork, body chains, precious metals, asymmetry and sculpted ornament deliver grandeur. A noble may wear very little fabric and a fortune in jewelry. Heavy chains may clink, sway, inconvenience movement and be deliberately oversized as a display of leisure, wealth and prestige.

Higher status does **not** require physically heavier dress in every setting; some nobles favor delicate perfection over lavish bulk. Taste, family tradition and circumstance vary.

## V. Matriarchal Circlets

**A circlet is the essential customary symbol of a Naga Matriarch's dignity and household authority.** A Matriarch of even the poorest Coil is traditionally expected to own and wear one, commonly copper.

This is an **expectation of custom and social respect**, **not a universally binding law**, magic requirement, or guarantee of aristocratic position. A Matriarch might remove it during sleep, bathing, private relaxation, dangerous work or other appropriate circumstances.

Circlet material often loosely reflects household means and prestige:

| Material | Typical customary association |
|---|---|
| Copper | Poor or humble household; still dignified |
| Bronze or brass | Modestly established household |
| Silver | Prosperous or respected Coil |
| Gold | Wealthy or aristocratic Coil |
| Gem-set precious metal | Great wealth, powerful lineage or ceremonial display |

**These are common associations, not legal grades or rigid rank requirements.** A powerful Matriarch may choose an inherited copper circlet for sentiment, while a wealthy but politically minor family might display gold. Designs range from a simple fitted brow band to lightweight, jeweled circlets with heraldic or serpentine motifs. They are usually more open and restrained in silhouette than large crowns.

When formally representing her Coil, receiving visitors, attending public business or presiding at court, a Matriarch is especially expected to present her circlet correctly. Improper insignia or negligent formal presentation can be more embarrassing than extensive exposed skin.

## VI. Decorative Chains and Manacles

Chains and manacles are signature Naga accessories and may be deliberately impractical. They can be light or heavy, often excessively jeweled, regal, sculpted, engraved, polished or inherited. A large quantity of expensive metalwork can make the garment's scant fabric seem secondary.

**Ornamental manacles** may encircle wrists or upper arms, in matched or mismatched sets. They may resemble historical restraints but should not be treated as working restraint devices merely because of their appearance. Oversized, dramatically weighty cuffs may advertise that the wearer need not perform manual labor.

**Chains** can cross the chest, drape from shoulders, hang at the waist or coil around the tail. Precious metal, gems and craftsmanship are prized; the wearer may accept clinking, swaying and incidental inconvenience. Wealthy fashions may be spectacularly excessive. Physical practicality is intentionally *not* the standard governing ornamental chain fashion.

Chosen jewelry and imposed restraints are **not equivalent**. A free Naga's optional manacles proclaim status and agency; an enslaved worker's compulsory collar records ownership. Their visual resemblance can become an object of political argument, discomfort, pride, or satire.

## VII. Courtly and Aristocratic Dress

Formal Naga dress combines short fine silks, fitted torso garments, waist or tail adornment, jeweled manacles, chains, carefully arranged gemstones and appropriate household or institutional insignia. Matriarchs wear their customary circlets. Some dress in thin, delicate strands of jewelry; others choose grand metal pieces, heavy chains and clusters of jewels.

Presentation communicates hierarchy through craftsmanship and recognizable symbols rather than coverage. Tailoring, lineage ornaments, matching sets and clean arrangements can be socially scrutinized. Revealing dress alone is normally not scandalous. The Naga's own practical restraints, specialized Imperial magical collars and other peoples' legal identification collars must not be conflated with her voluntary fashion jewelry.

## VIII. Ceremonial Dress and Ancestral Memory

Naga ritual clothing is **significantly more overtly reminiscent of the apparel and restraints of their ancestors' Serpentari-era enslavement** than ordinary fashion. Ceremonial ensembles may use visibly historical cuff forms, elaborate collars, connecting chains, reduced silk coverage, lineage ornaments and circlets. These forms can look much closer to the restraint-coded attire worn by enslaved populations today.

The cultural meaning is deliberate: remembrance, inherited status, historical continuity, freedom or protest, depending on the people and ceremony. This can be solemn, celebratory or politically charged.

Established ceremonial contexts include:

- **Remembrance of ancestral servitude:** special restraint-inspired jewelry and recognizable historical motifs.
- **Matriarchal succession:** symbolic transition from ancestral obligation to leadership, potentially presenting ceremonial chains before formal assumption of the circlet.
- **Coil ancestral observances:** heirloom or reproduction cuffs, chains and jewelry.
- **Conservative court ceremonies:** prominent symbolism connected with the old Serpentari Imperium.

These ceremonies are cultural practices, not magical abilities or compulsory uniform scripts. Variations among families and factions are normal.

**Crucial limit on literal binding:** Ordinary participants wear *symbolic* or decorative historical restraints. **Physically binding oneself is an exceptional, overt statement, primarily associated with zealots and abolitionists staging protest.** Zealots might pursue an extreme literal reenactment out of devotion or belief, while abolitionists may bind themselves to confront spectators with the contradiction between ancient Naga servitude and present-day slavery. The act is not a universal part of rituals, required for succession, or evidence that everyday jewelry is functional restraint hardware. Precise practices and historical reproduction techniques are not standardized further.

## IX. Everyday, Domestic and Occupational Dress

**Domestic:** Short wraps, light tops, soft fabrics and familiar jewelry allow comfort; a Matriarch may remove her circlet privately. Affluent householders might still wear expensive ornaments by preference.

**Civil administration and court:** Structured yet sensual attire, visible appropriate insignia, carefully composed jewelry, and Matriarchal circlets when applicable. Formal hierarchy matters.

**Magical scholarship:** Naga Psychics and scholars favor arrangements compatible with study, concentration and handling instruments. Jewelry grants no Psycraft or other magical abilities.

**Engineering and industrial labor:** Protective attire and the safety of machines take precedence. Dangling chains may be removed or secured for dangerous work. Fashionable impracticality is an aristocratic choice, not an instruction to sabotage a Worldspindle.

**Military and security:** Real armor and equipment must obey established mechanical constraints. Ornamental cuffs and chains do not create armor, protection, free hands or passive bonuses.

## X. Regional Variations Within the Imperium

The shared Gilded Coil silhouette permits local preferences, not isolated mandatory costumes.

- **Sathryx:** Capital fashion prizes courtly precision, highly polished metal, formal circlets and coordinated hierarchy.
- **Velmora:** Garden-oriented refinement tends toward delicate ornament, graceful short drapes and lighter jewelry.
- **Serrath Quay:** Commercial wealth and maritime exchange encourage imported gemstones and conspicuous eclectic finery.
- **Kheth-Vael:** Industrial prosperity tends toward precisely manufactured metalwork, sharp tailoring and ornament associated with technology.

These are customary tendencies, not immutable rules; travelers, households and artisans exchange styles across cities.

## XI. Relationships With Other Peoples and the Politics of Fashion

The ruling Naga aesthetic influences, but does not erase, the fashion of enslaved and free Kobolds, Tidekin, Moontouched, Bloomkin, Duskpaws and other residents. Existing collars remain defined by their owning references: functional Kobold Orb Collars, two-ring Tidekin Harborbands, and other approved racial variants. Magical Mirror, Trance and Coilsting Collars are separate expensive specialized restraints, not default fashion.

A jeweled Naga manacle might exceed the total value of a worker's clothing; its owner may choose it freely while the worker cannot remove their own compulsory collar. The visual irony is acknowledged within the culture. Traditionalists may claim historical triumph and continuity; abolitionists, especially Unbound Daughters, may challenge the institutional hypocrisy. Do not treat the criticism as universally held by Naga or as new settled political institutions.

## XII. Approved Visual Treatment

Use Mystaria's Direction 4 detailed ink-and-color fantasy aesthetic, preserving Naga anatomy and the natural range of fabric, metal, skin and scale colors. Purple is Mystaria's identifying visual color, not a requirement that every Naga wear purple.

**Representative study set:** poor Matriarch with copper circlet; everyday citizen with light chains; practical professional with relevant tools; heavily jeweled aristocrat; noble Matriarch with formal circlet; overtly historical ritual participant; appropriately equipped industrial worker. In art, use short dancer-inspired silks, expressive jewelry and clearly chosen ornamental chains, not default harness motifs or excessively long robes.

## XIII. Canon Boundaries

This is **approved cultural and visual canon**. It does not introduce mechanical armor statistics, Craft ranks, Skill Point or mana costs, new magical powers, legal dress mandates, formal statutory circlet grades, compulsory literal ceremonial bondage, or new city-level governments.

The exact shapes of historical Serpentari restraint hardware, item prices, and standardized technical designs remain **UNRESOLVED**. Individual illustrations still require explicit artistic approval, independent of this fashion dossier.

**Historical context remains canon:** the Serpentari owned ancient Naga overseers; present Naga rule the Gilded Coil and maintain institutions of slavery justified through order and hierarchy. The approved costume tradition neither erases that history nor asserts that Naga are now universally subject to bondage.

**Summary:** The Naga keep their silks short, their jewels magnificent, their matriarchs circleted, and their ornamental chains deliberately useless. Their ritual dress remembers bondage more plainly. They have not forgotten their chains; they have gilded them.
