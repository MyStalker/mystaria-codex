# Duskpaw Fashion — Salvage, Caches and Lastwear

**Status:** APPROVED CANON — Duskpaw salvage fashion, Lastwear, and author-approved Imperial/Scarlands Latchjack and punitive Lockjack uniform customs (November 6, 2026). Unresolved garment mechanisms and numbers are marked accordingly.
**Owners:** Duskpaw race and Caches (`02_Races.md`); linguistic customs (`12_Languages_and_Linguistic_Customs.md`); Scarlands and Imperial context (`20_Nymbrin_Wetcraft_and_Hexen_Canon.md`, `18_The_Gilded_Coil_Imperial_Gazetteer.md`); collars and emancipation icon (`25_Imperial_Collar_Culture_and_Restraints.md`).

## I. Practical Salvage and Mixed Clothing — CANON

Duskpaws hate wasting useful things. **Torn, mismatched, patched, visibly repaired, repurposed and layered clothing is exceptionally common** in Duskpaw self-chosen dress. Deliberate asymmetry and unexpected combinations of fabric, fittings or accessories often express pride in the intelligence of reuse and a garment's accumulated history rather than material poverty. Even relatively wealthy Duskpaws may choose these looks over purchasing matching new outfits.

This habit is consistent with their history as forced scavengers, their resourcefulness, and the value their Caches place on small finds. The emphasis is **usefulness and personal choice**, not a universal inability to maintain clean clothes.

**Important correction for institutional contexts:** Enslaved Duskpaws, particularly those in the Scarlands and Naga Imperium, can be compelled to wear standardized, serviceable, visibly regulated uniforms instead of freely assembled salvage clothes. A Duskpaw's personal clothing preferences do **not** override a master's demands; clothing and control of presentation are meaningful distinctions between free dress and enslaved labor. The established **Latchjack** uniform, its occupational variants, the punitive **Lockjack**, and related Scarlands harness/collar differences are described in Section VII.

## II. Lastwear — CANON

**Lastwear** is the established Duskpaw fashion term for **clothing visibly associated with its previous wearer's death**. The association is supposed to be recognizable, not completely disguised or erased. Repairs may stabilize the garment while leaving characteristic signs of its history visible, such as a repaired tear, slash, scorch or other evidence when appropriate to its actual past.

The broad cultural practice includes three **descriptive, non-legal** uses rather than three fixed official grades:

- **Salvaged Lastwear:** An otherwise useful garment reclaimed after a stranger's or former wearer's death; rejecting it solely for its history can seem wasteful.
- **Memorial Lastwear:** A garment retained in remembrance of a beloved friend, relative or other important person.
- **Trophy Lastwear:** A provocative or triumphant piece associated with an enemy, defeated oppressor or adversary; context and wearer intent matter.

The three motives can overlap. Wearing Lastwear does not mean every Duskpaw celebrates death or participates in violence, and the clothing is not automatically a sacred object. Some appreciate its value; some cherish the deceased; some make a defiant political statement; some deliberately make others uncomfortable.

An aristocrat who pays to have perfectly good clothing falsely distressed to imitate death-marked Lastwear is more likely to be **mocked for wastefulness and pretension** than to cause universal solemn outrage. Reactions vary by individual and context, especially where genuine bereavement is involved. Manufactured damage is not genuine Lastwear's history.

## III. Caches and Clothing Histories — CANON

Duskpaw Caches already maintain communal **Hoards**. Garments can be shared, traded, gifted, borrowed, repaired, inherited and adapted between Cache members. Old clothes may accumulate alterations and personal histories over years or generations; their wearers may value the story of a garment more than uniformity or its original price. Ownership, gifts and memorial attachments still matter. A communal culture of reuse does **not** license taking cherished memorial clothing or other Cache property without consent.

## IV. Formal Dress — CANON

Duskpaw formal attire does not have to become pristine or match perfectly. Carefully selected asymmetrical garments, elegant items combined with ragged heirlooms, salvage with personal stories, and ingenious repurposing may be judged beautiful or prestigious. A visibly repaired coat can be more significant to its wearer than a new matching garment. The meaning of objects can supersede fabric value, but this is a tendency rather than a universal rule at every court or workplace.

## V. Emancipation and the Open Lock — CANON

**The open lock** is already a recognized Duskpaw emancipation symbol, distinct from a compulsory closed or functional restraint. Some free Duskpaws repurpose broken locks, opened shackles or discarded restraint fittings as chosen fashion accessories. These objects may celebrate liberation or reclaim once-coercive materials. This voluntary adornment must not be confused with the existing **Nightlock** slave collar, whose design and legal function are unchanged.

## VI. Geographic and Social Boundaries

Duskpaws are especially common in the **Scarlands**, and also prominent in the **Naga Imperium**. Free individuals may wear patchwork, Lastwear and Cache-linked finery; enslaved people may be dressed according to their masters' occupational standards even when they would prefer self-chosen salvage styles.

Scarlands precedent includes repaired, weathered and personalized design language, but a regulated uniform can still be tidy and fit for work. Imperial outfits tend toward polished, identifiable manufacture and occupational standardization. The author's newer explicit decision establishes **jumpsuit-style Latchjacks** for both regions, with the Imperial casual-industrial silhouette and harsher Scarlands harness/collar presentation documented below.

## VII. Latchjack Occupational Uniforms — CANON (November 6, 2026)

**Latchjack** is the approved common Duskpaw occupational work-uniform family, especially associated with Duskpaw slavery in the **Naga Imperium**, with related versions in the **Scarlands**. This supersedes the original proposal to use only a short working jacket with an apron as the central silhouette. **The defining garment is a jumpsuit or one-piece coverall**, not a Kobold-style laboratory coat. It remains suited to a small raccoonfolk body, with tail accommodation, work-oriented storage and a recognizably Duskpaw silhouette.

### VII.A. Imperial Latchjack: relaxed industrial workwear

The standard Imperial Latchjack is a relatively tidy, maintained **work jumpsuit**, but not necessarily pressed flat or uncomfortable-looking. **Wrinkled uniforms, backwards caps and oversized working gloves are exceptionally common**, lending the style a distinctive scruffy-but-regulated industrial character. An angled or diagonal opening, practical pockets, reinforced working areas and utility attachments are shared design elements adapted from the original silhouette proposal. An outer apron, pocket panel or additional outer jacket can be added when the work requires it, but is **not** a mandatory separate defining garment.

Within the setting's mature, sensual Imperial clothing style, **adult female Duskpaws commonly wear the jumpsuit's upper front partly open, revealing some cleavage**, particularly because working in it all day can be hot. This is a practical/common styling tendency, not compulsory exposure, a special legal classification or proof of sexual availability. Opening the collar area does not remove or negate any compulsory **Nightlock** slave collar; protective requirements during hazardous work still take precedence.

Masters or supervisors can require presentable uniforms without banning ordinary wrinkles, slouchy caps or working gloves. The apparent casualness does **not** mean that an enslaved worker has freedom to change their assigned outfit.

### VII.B. Approved occupational variations

The Latchjack is adapted to five established occupational uniform directions. These are **descriptive design variants**, not newly legislated occupations, formal ranks or permission to work.

1. **Reclaimer:** Salvage, recycling, sorting and refuse recovery. A rugged jumpsuit with divided storage or tool provision and sturdy handling gloves; particularly evocative of Duskpaw ancestral salvage labor. **Primary recognizable reference for the uniform family.**
2. **Underworks Hand:** Sewer, duct, drain, lock and infrastructure maintenance. A close-fitting or securely adjusted Latchjack with safeguarded storage and abrasion protection suitable for climbing and tight routes.
3. **Hazerunner:** Hazardous recovery or contaminated-site work, especially relevant to the Scarlands. A Latchjack adapted for actual protective outer equipment, appropriate sealed layers or breathing protections. **Ordinary cloth is not Haze protection** and no resistance or immunity is granted by this costume alone.
4. **Swiftfoot:** Courier, dispatch, freight and warehouse movement. A lighter, movement-friendly jumpsuit with secure document/parcel pockets and restrained straps for climbing and crowded streets.
5. **Storekeeper:** Stockroom, inventory, records or controlled-goods handling. A more polished, properly fitted version emphasizing organized compartments and accountability, without becoming a Kobold academic coat.

Each job may alter fabric weight, closures, useful tools, glove design and protective coverage. **No fixed occupation badge code, color chart, toolkit requirement or numeric garment statistic has been approved.** The maker may adapt patterns to local conditions.

### VII.C. Lockjack: punitive and non-removable Latchjack

**Lockjack** is the approved proper name for a **punitive Latchjack designed so the wearer cannot remove it unaided**. It is normally imposed for disciplinary reasons and remains substantially **the same working uniform in ordinary function** rather than granting different occupational abilities.

**Old-fashioned prison-style stripes** are a defining visual feature. **Bells are often fitted to make stealth more difficult**, drawing attention to the wearer and publicly indicating the conspicuous disciplinary status. This aesthetic is intended to make it obvious that the wearer has been punished; it does not establish any particular offense, standard sentence, official stripe code or automatic criminal conviction.

**Approved security principle (November 7, 2026):** The Lockjack's garment-retention mechanism is **independent of the Nightlock collar**. Its authorized release is under a **designated supervisor's control**, rather than the wearer's. It must remain usable for **ordinary work and basic hygiene**, including necessary care and cleaning without requiring the Duskpaw to be able to independently remove the uniform. This is a functional design requirement, not an approved locking schematic, certification, permission hierarchy, or extra magical restraint.

The exact retention hardware, servicing approach, garment-changing and washing procedure, emergency access and safety safeguards, and quantitative detection/stealth effects remain **UNRESOLVED**. There is **no new numeric stealth modifier**, universal magical suppression, special Fraycraft interaction or automatic collar behavior. A Lockjack must not be mistaken for a redesigned Nightlock collar.

### VII.D. Scarlands adaptation: visible Vren domination

Scarlands Duskpaws also wear forms of the Latchjack for corresponding work, but the common presentation is **more openly coercive**. In addition to the work jumpsuit, **firmer harnesses and prominent metal slave collars** emphasize ownership and control, in keeping with Vren masters' overtly exploitative attitude. Compared with the relatively polished and administratively organized Imperial version, these garments may be hardwearing, visibly repaired and fitted with stronger restraint-style accessories.

The **Nightlock remains the approved Duskpaw collar identity**. The Scarlands ruling authorizes conspicuous **metal-collar presentation and harsher harness fittings**, not a separate newly named collar with invented locking or magical mechanics. Whether exact Nightlock construction differs by region, how it integrates physically with the extra harness, the materials and fittings, and any actual escape or Fraycraft checks are **UNRESOLVED** until developed separately.

This regional distinction reflects the coercion of slavery; it does not imply every Vren has the same personality, every enslaved Duskpaw wears an identical pattern, or that free Duskpaws must wear slave harnesses.

### VII.E. Interaction with free Duskpaw fashion

The prescribed Latchjack and Lockjack stand apart from the freedom to choose torn and mixed self-made outfits or **Lastwear**. Free Duskpaws can reuse an old Latchjack, unpick or reconfigure its pockets and wear it in their preferred salvage style, but this possibility does not alter the standardized nature of a master's issued uniform.

## VIII. Mechanics and Production Limits

This fashion record does not add Fraycraft ranks, skill costs, armor protection, immunity to Haze, new legal categories, or numerical stealth effects. A Lockjack is coercively non-removable by its wearer as a **narrative/wardrobe ruling**, with its functional construction still unresolved. **Protective equipment must actually be suitable to hazards**; a decorative scarf does not substitute for a functional Haze mask. Artist-created garments and illustrations require separate user approval. Historical provenance of individual Lastwear garments depends on the character and story rather than an automatic certification system.
