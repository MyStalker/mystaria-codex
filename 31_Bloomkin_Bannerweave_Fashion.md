# Bannerweave — Bloomkin and Thornguard Heroic Fashion

**Status:** APPROVED CANON — November 6, 2026.
**Cultural owners:** Bloomkin tailors, Shieldmates and their Thornguard knightly partners, principally within the Canopy Kingdoms.
**Reference precedence:** Latest explicit author approvals; `02_Races.md`, `10_Canon_Changelog.md`, `11_Visual_Style_Guide.md`, `12_Languages_and_Linguistic_Customs.md`, `24_Racial_Naming_Conventions_and_Namebook.md`, `25_Imperial_Collar_Culture_and_Restraints.md`.

## 1. The Hero's Tailor

Bloomkin are gentle, whimsical and exacting craftspeople whose aspirations are surprisingly grand. Their particular visual ambition is to **inspire awe**. They revere heroic stories, the gods' champions and legendary figures, and construct costumes that translate almost storybook notions of heroism into beautifully functional clothing. Their idealized sense of what a hero ought to look like may be naive or excessively theatrical, but their remarkable tailoring skills are more than sufficient to make the designs impressive.

Bloomkin are often the **personal tailors** of Thornguard knights. Their craftsmanship makes their partners' capes, heraldry and heroic presence possible, while they construct equally striking little squire or Shieldmate outfits for themselves. This is not an abandonment of Bloomkin playfulness or kindness; it is a distinct expression of ambition, artistry and self-importance.

## 2. Bannerweave and Twinmantle

**Bannerweave** is the established name for the complementary heroic fashion tradition shared by Thornguard knights and Bloomkin Shieldmates. The artistic direction is **Bloomkin-led**. Thornguard may contribute personal requirements, heraldry, oaths, equipment constraints and taste, but the accompanying Bloomkin tailors commonly take primary charge of composition and craft.

**Complementary heraldry** is essential: the pair need not wear miniature duplicates. Their colors, Banner emblems, decorative elements and shapes answer or complete one another. A broad knightly cape and a compact Shieldmate mantle might carry different parts of one heraldic motif; together, the design presents a single heroic pairing.

**Twinmantle** describes a particularly synchronized or carefully composed Bannerweave ensemble, especially when the two garments visibly echo one another. It is a name for the fashion presentation, **not** a new Concordance rank, magical state or equipment property.

The partnership can involve exaggerated knightly capes, carefully embroidered insignia, shaped collars and cuffs, tailored doublets and tunics, short mantles, fine stitching, and miniature armor-inspired ornament. The shape of the garments must remain compatible with equipment, movement and the real physical role of the Shieldmate. Magnificent-looking costume is not itself armor and grants no mechanical protection.

## 3. The Gallant Squire and Other Heroic Archetypes

Bloomkin frequently style themselves as legendary squires, heroic companions and accomplished champions, not merely as servants or assistants. Recognizable fashion archetypes include:

- **The Gallant Squire:** Small cape or mantle, tailored doublet, gloves, useful boots, theatrical heraldry and a splendidly upright silhouette.
- **The Arcane Shieldmate:** Beautifully stitched coat or short robe with a practical working fit, heraldic correspondence to the knight and design motifs emphasizing skilled shaping of white magic.
- **The Royal Companion:** Extravagant courtly tailoring, tiny epaulettes, formal trim, richly embroidered insignia, and an almost comically magnificent mantle.

These are broad fashion approaches, **not official ranks, required costumes or new institutional titles**. Individual Bloomkin may vary considerably. Adult dress follows Mystaria's established mature visual direction while remaining species-appropriate.

## 4. Grand Introductions and Self-Proclaimed Champions

**Grand introductions are a recognizable, charming cultural custom among Bloomkin participating in noble, knightly and Shieldmate life.** Even squires and renowned craftspeople may adopt lengthy, fanciful epithets and announce themselves with theatrical pride. Nearly every Bloomkin in this cultural setting is eager to declare themselves a champion or an extraordinary hero, frequently with confidence far exceeding their physical size.

Examples of boastful introductions or informal honorifics:

- “The greatest wielder of the white to have ever lived!”
- “The Hope of the Canopy!”
- “Champion of a Hundred Banners!”
- “The Peerless Needle of the Canopy!”
- “Defender of Every Hearth!”

These **illustrate a pattern of self-aggrandizing presentation**, not a set of exclusive sanctioned titles, proven feats or established named individuals. The examples do not override Bloomkin personal names and craft-derived family names in `24_Racial_Naming_Conventions_and_Namebook.md`.

Their Thornguard companions often **pretend to be unmoved or exasperated** while privately finding the proclamations endearing. This is a recognizable dynamic, not a universal personality requirement. Bloomkin rhetoric can coexist with their existing precise, earnest workshop language. The grandeur is often playful, but their pride in their magical and technical contributions is sincere.

In ordinary speech Bloomkin typically speak of **the white**, rather than using the academic term `mana`. A boast about wielding the white does **not** mean a Bloomkin can independently generate it.

## 5. Heroic Inspiration and Reverence for Purple

The exploits of heroes, legendary champions and the gods' favored figures deeply inform Bloomkin imagination. They may adapt the colors, capes, silhouettes or insignia associated with revered historical or legendary exemplars, without requiring each heroic story to have a standardized official uniform.

**Purple is particularly revered in Bloomkin fashion.** It may appear in significant embroidery, heroic trims, important ceremonial garments or treasured insignia. The reverence is cultural and spiritual, consonant with Mystaria's established purple Shimmer identity; it does not grant magical power, force every outfit to be purple or replace the five approved mana symbols. Color use remains diverse.

When a Thornguard becomes a celebrated legend, skilled Bloomkin may **compete enthusiastically for the honor of becoming that knight's Shieldmate**, motivated by admiration and the prestige of participating in a living heroic story. This competition does not create a new legal selection procedure or guarantee mutual consent under the existing Oath of Protection. The Thornguard knight's actual Concordance partner, service status and personal relationships must still follow established canon.

## 6. The First Fitting

**The First Fitting** is an established Bloomkin-led personal and artistic milestone: the first completed, coordinated Bannerweave wardrobe a tailor creates for a Thornguard knight and themselves. It is a matter of immense professional pride, often involving the refinement of complementary heraldry, matching shapes and practical tailoring.

Bloomkin may pursue astonishing detail and a theatrical ideal of heroism, and experienced knights may gently negotiate the number of flourishes or trailing capes. The moment is socially meaningful, **not** a required spellcasting ritual, magical bond, new profession gate, legal transfer, fixed ceremony or source of skill bonuses. The exact manner of celebrating it varies by pair and community.

## 7. Craft Authority and Power Within Unequal Partnerships

**Bloomkin lead the fashion design** even where Thornguard outrank them in a feudal or slaveholding hierarchy. A great knight depends upon the Bloomkin's ability to shape both clothing and white magic. Thornguard therefore have strong personal and practical reasons to treat their tailors and magicians with care and respect. This does **not** repeal the established **Oath of Protection**, remove enslavement, erase coercion, or guarantee kindness from every knight.

Some traditionalist households may prefer a majestic Thornguard centerpiece with a clearly subordinate-looking squire, and some Bloomkin enjoy the pageantry of that role. Others resent any implication that they are mere decorative assistants. Reformist pairs may favor outfits of comparable prominence, consistent with the already established ideal of the **Perfect Promise** and voluntary equal partnership.

Existing Concordance mechanics are unchanged: **Thornguard supply white magical energy, Bloomkin shape it**. Their heroic claims and garments do not independently generate mana, alter spell costs, grant new ranks, strengthen barriers, or make a partnership voluntary merely through aesthetic symbolism.

## 8. Distinction From Other Peoples and Other Regions

Unlike the cozy, domestic, sentimental fashion presently established for Moontouched, Bloomkin knight-and-squire style strives for **awe, spectacle, personal heroism and polished craftsmanship**. Both groups remain affectionate peoples of Surrender, but their dominant costuming cues are different.

Bannerweave is especially associated with **Canopy Kingdoms** and Thornguard-Bloomkin pairs. Bloomkin living in the Gilded Coil retain the option to dress as craftspeople, companions or heroic squires and to express their own identities; they are **not** automatically paired with a Thornguard or dressed in Bannerweave.

The existing collar visual distinctions remain unchanged: **Maid's Choker** in the Canopy Kingdoms and **Petalwork** elsewhere, where appropriate to enslavement and regional custom. Bannerweave is an **outfit and cultural tradition**, not a new collar type, legal status, or additional restraint.

## 9. Visual and Linguistic Production Boundaries

In approved Mystaria visual production, show the size contrast between **3–4 foot Bloomkin** and **5–7 foot Thornguard**, with complementary heroic costumes rather than identical duplicates. Bloomkin should look genuinely accomplished and proud, not reduced to mascot proportions or a joke. Small scale and adorable appearance can coexist with gallant posing, technical excellence and precise needlework. Purple may bear special cultural emphasis without flattening varied palettes.

Dialogue may include spontaneous, extravagant self-introductions, playful heroic titles and affectionate Thornguard reactions. Respect existing Bloomkin artisan precision, Surrender's language, ordinary speaking of magical energy by color, and the severity of already established slurs.

**No new magic ranks, SP/MP costs, official heraldic register, mandated heroic wardrobe, numeric effects, defined historic heroic portraits or named NPCs have been added.** Individual character illustrations remain subject to the author's separate approval.
