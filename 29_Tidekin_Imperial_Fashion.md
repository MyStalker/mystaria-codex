# Tidekin Fashion of the Gilded Coil
## The Raft, the Harborband, and the Moving Water

**Status:** APPROVED CANON (November 6, 2026), reflecting explicit author approval of the full five-part Tidekin fashion proposal and the later preference for close-fitting swimwear with bikini-style tops common in casual dress.
**Owners and precedence:** This document owns Tidekin clothing customs within the Naga Imperium. Racial anatomy, Rafts, Kin-knots and Saltbound status are governed by `02_Races.md`; Harborbands and Imperial identification by `25_Imperial_Collar_Culture_and_Restraints.md`; Wakecraft mechanics by `13_Wakecraft_The_Six_Arts_of_the_Living_Tide.md`. It adds no magic, legal category, price, or Skill Point mechanics.

## I. Fashion Principle

Tidekin clothing values **freedom of movement, clever construction, and personal attachment over elaborate ornamentation**. These aquatic otterfolk swim readily, cooperate in Rafts, and are comfortable with attire others might consider quite revealing. Swim-friendly clothes are a practical everyday wardrobe, rather than only garments for recreation. Naga-Imperial sensual fashion is present, but does not erase Tidekin priorities or the cultural importance of kinship.

**Author-approved preference:** The defining everyday silhouette is **close-fitting swimwear**. Bikini-style tops are **very common in casual attire**, alongside fitted pieces, short skirts, abbreviated jackets and sleeveless tops. There is no general requirement to wear long flowing fabric; any loose fabric is typically optional, removable or secured before swimming and work.

## II. Materials and Garments

Tidekin wear close-fitting, body-adapted tops and bottoms, short skirts, sleeveless garments, fitted accessories, and brief jackets when useful. Water resistance and mobility govern construction: **quick-drying textiles**, corrosion-resistant fittings and compact fastenings are favored, along with protective items chosen for the specific job. Their apparel can be playful, fashionable, and mature without being impractical underwater. Additional loose, flowing or translucent layers can be added for occasions and removed or secured for swimming.

Leather and other materials may be used where suitable, but no fixed proprietary fiber, magical waterproof property, exact manufacturing technology, or universal dress code is established. Aesthetic preferences vary by Raft, occupation, wealth and personal taste.

## III. Kin-knots and Personal Adornment

**Kin-knots** are already established cords shared between Tidekin loved ones. They also have a strong place in fashion: they can be integrated into bracelets, waist cords, fastenings or other personal accessories, provided their wearer can protect or secure them during swimming. Their importance comes principally from **the person who gave them and the relationship they represent**, not market price or jeweled value.

Polished favorite stones, keepsakes, braided cords and colorful materials complement these sentimental ornaments. Fashion can express affectionate Raft identity, but there is no mandatory knot pattern, universally legible color code, rank, or official system of classification approved.

## IV. Saltbound Clothing and Occupational Adaptation

Tidekin enslaved within the Naga Imperium are frequently called **Saltbound** and are assigned to maritime, canal, maintenance and related labor. Workwear remains **swim-capable and task-specific**:

- **Canal and lock workers:** Close-fitting garments, compact belts, protective items and little loose material near equipment.
- **Maritime specialists:** Flexible water-resistant clothes, streamlined tools and accessible small equipment containers.
- **Household attendants:** More carefully tailored, sometimes decorative attire that still permits comfortable movement and aquatic needs.
- **Prestigious navigators and Wakewrights:** Higher-quality practical garments, well-kept personal ornaments and, where appropriate to existing Wakecraft, carried water supplies or suitable containers.

These are aesthetic and occupational tendencies rather than an additional legal classification or new magical abilities. Owners may furnish clothing for status display, but costly attire does not imply freedom.

The approved **Harborband** remains the usual Saltbound identifying design: at least **two separate streamlined corrosion-resistant metal neck rings**, with the **upper indicating assessed value** and the **lower security/obedience classification** in the owner's administrative system. Specific official marks, colors, grades and procedures are unresolved. The classifications reflect owners' assessments, not objective moral worth. Ordinary Harborbands are not automatically expensive magical restraints.

## V. Free Tidekin Fashion

Free Tidekin commonly show **colorful individuality**: mixed fabrics, distinctive cords, colorful swimwear, mementos of travel and carefully kept personal ornaments. Unlike the generally standardized workplace dress of Saltbound, their outfits may appear more improvised or personally expressive. This does not require that free Tidekin look poor, messy or ragged; a free Tidekin may have impeccable taste, while an enslaved Tidekin can personalize whatever clothing is permitted. Clothing alone is never a conclusive test of legal status.

## VI. Social and Ceremonial Clothing

Formal Tidekin attire generally emphasizes **movement rather than stiffness**. Close-fitting foundations may be dressed with short decorative fabrics, polished stones, braided cords, sentimental keepsakes, and optional light or translucent removable layers. Kin-knots and ties to a Raft can be made especially visible at celebrations or formal gatherings. This remains compatible with the wider Imperial comfort with sensuality, but avoids blindly copying the Naga's intentionally burdensome heavy jewelry.

There is no approved compulsory outfit, specific festival costume, magical garment effect, or universal rite for exchanging particular garments. Existing Raft and Kin-knot lore remains the authority for familial meaning.

## VII. Visual Reference and Boundaries

Show Tidekin as **small otterfolk, approximately four to five feet tall**, keeping their established anatomy and aquatic mobility. Distinguish swim-oriented garments from impractical land-only costumes. Ordinary casual outfits often have bikini tops; layering may vary with occupation and scene. Saltbound working outfits should accommodate the known Harborband and realistic task safety. Free Tidekin should look individual rather than obligatorily disheveled.

Follow `11_Visual_Style_Guide.md` for approved illustration style and individual artistic approval. No image has been approved through this clothing ruling.

**Unresolved:** Specific Harborband symbols/colors/grades; exact materials or equipment prices; universally fixed ceremonial styles or Kin-knot display codes; any game-mechanical impact of fabrics. No Wakecraft skill ranks, mana costs or racial abilities are modified.

**Summary:** Tidekin dress for moving water and meaningful connections. Their fitted swimwear, colorful accessories and cherished Kin-knots belong to a living aquatic culture, whether worn freely or adapted under Imperial constraint.
