# Imperial Collar Culture, Regional Slavery Philosophies, and Orb Interfaces

**Status:** Mixed: approved user rulings are labeled **CANON**; design options not explicitly approved are labeled **PROPOSAL**. **Updated:** November 6, 2026.  
**Authority:** Latest user decisions, `01_World_and_Shimmer.md`, `18_The_Gilded_Coil_Imperial_Gazetteer.md`, `08_Other_Crafts.md`, `02_Races.md`, `11_Visual_Style_Guide.md`, `10_Canon_Changelog.md`.

## The three powers and the institution of slavery — CANON

**This comparison should appear near the first explanation of slavery that a player encounters.**

| Society | Governing justification | Primary characterization |
|---|---|---|
| **Naga Imperium / Gilded Coil** | **Enslave others for order and hierarchy.** | Registers, inherited roles, engineered administration, control as an ideal of civilization. |
| **Thornguard / Canopy Kingdoms** | **Enslave others to protect the weak, whether they like it or not.** | Paternalistic guardianship, enforced dependency, hereditary protection and obligations. |
| **Vren / Blood Courts** | **Enslave others to exploit the weak and shamelessly increase their own power.** | Deliberate exploitation, personal leverage, contracts, and accumulation of advantage. |

These are the prevailing institutional rationales, not universal beliefs held by every individual or moral endorsements. Regional and individual dissent remain possible. Existing Thornguard/Bloomkin, Naga/Kobold, and Vren institutions are not replaced.

## Approved regional collar aesthetic — CANON

- **Naga Imperium:** Precisely manufactured; polished and hierarchical; formal insignia and registration when appropriate.
- **Scarlands:** Darker, repaired, individualized, worn, with repurposed fittings.
- **Canopy Kingdoms:** Fine craftsmanship, heraldry, botanical motifs, engraving, woven ornamentation.

Each race adapts the regional language to its anatomy and tradition. The legal distinctions between ordinary fashion, ceremonial markings, and actual captivity remain important.

## Imperial collar culture — CANON selection of proposals

- **Public identification:** Mixed. Basic visible classifications can be recognized broadly; finer records and detailed meanings depend on Imperial officials and the Scale Register.
- **Luxury:** Class-dependent. Ordinary working collars are more standardized; wealthier owners can commission finer fittings and individualized decoration.
- **Temporary silencing:** Both ceremonial/disciplinary and security-related. Existing rules that gags and muzzles are not normally worn continuously during regular work remain operative.
- **Scale Register:** Every enslaved person in the Gilded Coil wears an identification collar, typically with owning Coil insignia and a Scale Register-recognized mark. Collars are not automatically sophisticated magical restraints.
- **Regional custom and racial fashion do not automatically imply enslavement** for any particular individual.

## Racial collar decisions — CANON visual direction

- **Kobolds:** Simple smooth metal band with **one colored central orb**, no exposed rings, clamps, buckles, or locks. Unlike decorative jewelry alone, orb collars are primarily usable **tools**. Technical professionals generally keep an orb on their person; **free Kobolds especially favor orb-bearing bracers**. Kobold style is modern fantasy laboratory wear, fitted coats, utility harnesses and restrained use of leather/latex accessories; the user's supplied red Kobold engineer image is a principal fashion reference, though not embedded here.
- **Tidekin:** The **Harborband** generally comprises **at least two separate, streamlined corrosion-resistant metal rings** fitted to their elongated necks. The **upper ring indicates assessed value**, and the **lower ring indicates security/obedience classification**. Exact symbols, colors, numerical grades, and administration have not been determined. Ordinary designs should remain practical for aquatic labor. These distinctions represent owners' assessments, not objective moral qualities.
- **Moontouched:** Moonlace as the selected collar design.
- **Bloomkin:** Maid's Choker within the Canopy Kingdoms; Petalwork outside.
- **Duskpaws:** Nightlock collar; the **open lock** is an approved cultural symbol of emancipation. Do not conflate a free person's chosen symbol with the closed lock of compulsory captivity.
- **Corvyn:** Hexlace commonly; Inkband for those considered a lower security risk. Corvyn fashion favors elaborate witch/wizard robes, especially Veilweaver with Midnight Witch influence, and grand hats or hoods as a major cultural touchstone.
- **Driders:** Verdict Collar.
- **Untouched Serpentari:** Serpent's Circlet.
- **Drakyn:** Conqueror's Band for work slaves; Jewel Torque for respected individuals.
- **Centaurs:** Herd Torque, usually substantially padded within; saddles carry social and ceremonial significance. Preserve the existing **Saddle Vow**: a freely offered and accepted proper riding saddle constitutes marriage; work gear and battle saddles do not automatically do so.
- **Firstshaped:** Ancestral Adaptation, following parent-race and actual anatomy.
- **Nymbrin:** Botanical Filigree commonly; Golden Vial for especially ornate or prosperous individuals.
- Naga, Thornguard, and Vren collars were intentionally reserved from this cross-racial design selection; do not infer final race-specific collar schematics.

## Orbcraft, Cipherlight, and Kobold collar interfaces — CANON

**Computational Orbs** process information and **Cipherlight**. **Magic Mirrors** convert visual information into Cipherlight and Cipherlight back into visual information. A compatible **Orb or Magic Mirror can control Kobold machinery**. A practitioner capable of Orbcraft may interact with an Orb's datastream **directly**, without a Magic Mirror serving as a visual screen: their brain comprehends the information-rich stream. No universal new Craft rank or Skill Point cost is established.

A Kobold can run their paws over the central orb of an **Orb Collar**, using control gestures. The interface produces a **small localized aurora**. Ungifted observers staring directly at it develop a **soft headache**; an Orbcrafter experiences a dense, intelligible information stream capable of conveying far more than a picture. This is a local interface glow, not automatically a full atmospheric Coilnet **Rainbow**.

**Hazard exception:** Extremely vibrant or intense Rainbows can still exceed even a trained Orbcrafter's comfortable processing ability. Existing danger from direct active Cipherlight exposure remains relevant to ungifted observers; the blanket interpretation that Orbcrafters suffer the same confusion is superseded. Exact safe exposure thresholds, headache duration, and overload mechanics remain unquantified. This does not waive the separate calibrated-retinal **Mindprism** requirements for the Mnemonic Arts.

## Three high-cost Naga Imperial magical collars — AUTHOR-DIRECTED DESIGN, CANONICAL CONCEPT; UNRESOLVED NUMERICS

These are **specialized, expensive items used as needed**, not universal features of identification collars. Their forms are deliberately conspicuous and utilitarian, **overriding ordinary racial collar designs**. Do not assign unstated crafting recipes, specific mana points, resist checks, ranges, duration or purchase prices.

### Mirror Collar

A **walking recording camera**: captures what its viewpoint can see. **Cannot broadcast**. Its records must be **manually plugged into an Orb for review**. It is not a remote, real-time watcher, not omnidirectional, and not automatic knowledge of private thoughts. Recording capacity, viewing angle, power, security and retention are not yet quantified.

### Trance Collar

When active, induces intense hypnosis, strongly stifling higher reasoning if the wearer fails to resist. The affected person becomes severely impaired and can only shamble through simple instructions, not undertake intellectual or technical tasks. **Very high mana demand** makes sustained use impractical; short use is more feasible. Kobold technical workers **almost never** use these, because accidental activation during delicate work can precipitate disaster. Exact resistance method, activation duration, power costs, post-effect recovery and safeguards remain unresolved.

### Coilsting Collar — APPROVED NAME

**Commonest of the three magical restraint types**, though still an expensive specialized collar compared with mundane identification. Uses **medium mana** relative to the other designs and normally requires **deliberate manual activation**. An optional installation may trigger when the collar leaves a specified **Rainbow coverage area**; this requires defined network coverage rather than omnipresent tracking. **No universal intent, thought, whisper, weapon-handling or secret-detection triggers**. It cannot activate at arbitrary locations on an owner's whim without an actual signal or control route. Specific thresholds, shocks, safeguards, signal reach, mana amounts, and resistances are unresolved. **Coilsting Collar** is the author-approved proper name for this Imperial restraint. The former descriptive working title **Shock Collar** is superseded.

**Superseded naming proposal:** **Censure Collar / Censureband** was never approved and is not an operative name.
