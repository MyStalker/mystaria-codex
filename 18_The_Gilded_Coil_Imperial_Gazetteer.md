## November 6, 2026 — Approved Naga fashion and chains of ancestral memory

**Naga fashion:** Everyday and court dress use short dancer-like silks, prominent jewelry and revealing but socially ordinary silhouettes. **Imperial philosophy:** Naga justify slavery as a structure of **order and hierarchy**, distinct from Thornguard protection-of-the-weak paternalism and Vren shameless exploitation for power. Moonlace, Petalwork and Nightlocks remain distinct racial designs; an open lock is approved Duskpaw emancipation symbolism.

**Orb interface clarification:** Computational Orbs process Cipherlight; **Magic Mirrors specifically convert pictures to Cipherlight and back**. A compatible Orb or Mirror can control Kobold machinery. Orbcraft-capable Kobolds can directly comprehend Orb datastreams without a Magic Mirror. Gesturing at an Orb Collar projects a small local aurora: ungifted onlookers staring at it get a mild headache, while Orbcrafters interpret a rich information stream. Very intense atmospheric Rainbows remain a possible processing hazard even to trained Orbcrafters. This refines, rather than erases, the direct Cipherlight hazard for ordinary viewers; no Mindprism exception is implied.

**Expensive specialized magical collars (author-directed concepts):** (1) **Mirror Collar**, a locally recording wearable camera with **no broadcast**, manually connected to an Orb to review footage; (2) **Trance Collar**, high-mana hypnosis suppressing reasoning in insufficiently resistant wearers, leaving only simple tasks, rarely suitable for Kobold engineers; (3) **Shock Collar**, medium-mana and the most common magical restraint of the three, normally manual activation, with optional boundaries tied to a *specified* Rainbow area. All override racial collar silhouettes for practicality; none is assumed to read thoughts, secrets or intent or to transmit omnipresent signals. Numeric costs, thresholds, duration and trigger details remain unapproved. **Censure Collar / Censureband is a name proposal only.**

## Cipherlight, Rainbows, and the Physical Coilnet — approved November 4, 2026

### Mindprism, Echoes and the Mnemonic Arts — approved November 4, 2026

The **Mnemonic Arts** are an elite, expensive application of Cipherlight-based **Orbcraft and Mirrorcraft**. Carefully calibrated **Mindprism** installations focus encoded Cipherlight through a person's **retinas** and into the brain, allowing memory recording, direct upload, genuine **Skillprints**, and real-time **Dreamcasting**. **Mindwrights** calibrate each client. Ordinary direct exposure to Cipherlight is still dangerously confusing and cannot safely replace Mindprism equipment.

A memory recording is an **Echo**, held in an **Echo Orb**. **Echoing** preserves the source memory but consumes **extravagant amounts of mana** and causes intense neurological strain; a broad **Deepprint** can take days or years. A **Mindcut** works much faster by actually erasing the source memories, potentially causing severe or permanent injury and deliberate amnesia. Magic does not reverse the technology's specific neurological strain; recovery requires time, and extreme harm can persist. A basic recording is comparably expensive to **purchasing a firearm**, with no fixed Gem price established.

**Echoes cannot be destroyed by mortal technology.** They naturally return to their **living original owner** when not held by an actively maintained, mana-consuming **Everseal**. Even copies implanted in living minds remain connected to their original owner; if all copies in living recipients are lost through death or forgetting, they return to the still-living owner. **The original owner's death ends the automatic return mechanism**. Returned information does not heal underlying brain injury. Divine memory workings remain separately governed.

Extremely rare **Skillprints** can bestow purchased Shimmer skills **without spending SP**, provided existing level, racial and skill prerequisites are satisfied; approximately **5 SP worth of assimilation per month** is the approved modest safety benchmark, with neurological risks beyond it. These are especially prized as medium- and high-level adventuring rewards, not routine shortcuts. **Dreamhouses** sell real-time **Dreamcasts**, a major entertainment industry distinct from compressed stored-memory uploads.

**Echo Vaults**, maintained with ongoing Everseals, preserve family history, sensitive intelligence, and stolen or deposited memories; **Memory Bonds** put memories up as collateral. **Living Reliquaries** are enslaved people used as intelligent repositories of foreign Echoes. The brutally exploited **Hollow Reliquaries** have much of their own autobiographical memory cut away. This never grants infinite biological capacity or removes their intelligence and personhood. For the full approved terminology, limits and unresolved technical details see [`19_The_Mnemonic_Arts_and_Echo_Economy.md`](19_The_Mnemonic_Arts_and_Echo_Economy.md).


**Cipherlight** is the approved name for the specially encoded, information-bearing light used by Kobold **Orbcraft** and **Mirrorcraft**. Computational Orbs encode information into light; compatible Orbs and Magic Mirrors relay, process, or interpret its messages. Beams travel to and between great **lighthouses** operating as optical communication relays. This adds a physical transmission mechanism to the existing Coilnet, not a new mana color or an independent magical Craft.

**Direct-beam hazard.** Looking straight into active Cipherlight causes **extreme confusion** as its densely encoded information overwhelms perception. The beam is hazardous even when not designed as a weapon. Exact exposure thresholds, resistance checks, and recovery times remain **UNSPECIFIED**.

**Rainbows.** The region covered by an active Coilnet is called its **Rainbow**. A Rainbow has a recognizably visible, aurora-like shimmer or pattern in the upper atmosphere. Ordinary traffic produces a subtle, aesthetically unobtrusive display; unusually heavy simultaneous information traffic makes it brighter, more conspicuous, and more intense. A Rainbow indicates an active network and approximate coverage, **not** the identity, contents, or precise locations of its communications. Even a small independent Coilnet can produce a noticeable Rainbow: one appearing around an isolated **Drakyn lair** can draw local attention. This atmospheric phenomenon is distinct from the Shimmer's purple identity and the five mana symbols; no exact colors, heights, or visibility distances have been specified.

**Underwater and concealed routes.** Cipherlight may travel **through water when guided by reflective surfaces**. Submerged or underground reflective pipelines can therefore move messages without a chain of exposed lighthouses, making those transmission routes potentially more secure and harder to intercept physically. Concealing the route does not necessarily hide an operating network's Rainbow. Security depends on the actual installation and traffic rather than an automatic immunity to spying.

### Mirrorwake interaction — a powerful vulnerability

Because Cipherlight is transmitted through illuminated water, **Tidekin Mirrorwake is exceptionally capable of detecting its passage** and recovering **strong, persistent impressions** left in water exposed to the transmission. A skilled Wakewright may find evidence of activity along submerged pipelines or other reflective waterways even where the transmitting apparatus is hidden. The impressions are unusually intense; they do **not** automatically present decrypted, readable Coilnet messages.

Cipherlight can also **overwhelm Mirrorwake impressions**. A sufficiently intense transmission may obscure, contaminate, or drown out water memories of unrelated events. This can be deliberately exploited to **conceal evidence**. With **particularly sophisticated preparation and manipulation**, Cipherlight may also be used to **plant misleading or false evidentiary impressions** in water, potentially deceiving an investigator. This is not effortless or universally successful, and Mirrorwake remains a contestable investigative technique rather than an infallible recording device. The exact method, MP cost, required skill ranks, and standards for distinguishing falsification from authentic impressions are **UNRESOLVED**. This new interaction supplements, but does not replace, Mirrorwake's approved five ranks, costs, and principle that water remembers what it witnessed.

A covert network is especially conspicuous where no Rainbow previously existed; it can be less obvious within an existing busy Rainbow. In waterways, Cipherlight's strong Mirrorwake traces add a second avenue of discovery, while deliberate contamination or forgery creates a corresponding threat to forensic trust.

# The Gilded Coil — Heartland of the Naga Empire

Mystaria Geographic Encyclopedia · Approved regional canon (November 4, 2026)

“The Serpentari conquered the world and built monuments to their greatness. The Naga inherited those monuments and built civilization beneath them. The Kobolds, naturally, are the reason the lights still work.”

## I. Overview

| Characteristic              | Description                                                                            |
| --------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Regional name               | The Gilded Coil                                                                        |
| Political entity            | The Naga Empire                                                                        |
| Location                    | Southeastern Mystaria, across the warm Imperial river basins                           |
| Capital                     | Sathryx, the Hollow Throne                                                             |
| Government                  | Imperial matriarchy governed through hereditary Naga Coils                             |
| Dominant race               | Naga                                                                                   |
| Principal enslaved peoples  | Kobolds, Moontouched, Bloomkin, Duskpaws, Tidekin                                      |
| Other significant residents | Vren, free members of several races, a small but influential Corvyn population         |
| Technology                  | Orbcraft, Mirrorcraft, Coilnet, Imperial Manatech                                      |
| Religion                    | No unified state goddess; considerable Serpentari ancestor-veneration                  |
| Economy                     | Magical technology, agriculture, manufacturing, commerce, information, and slave labor |
| Greatest strength           | Advanced infrastructure and centralized coordination                                   |
| Greatest weakness           | Dependence on enslaved specialists and ancient infrastructure                          |

The Gilded Coil is the most technologically advanced region of modern Mystaria, a sprawling collection of ancient Imperial cities, cultivated valleys, artificial wetlands, and magically sustained agricultural estates.

It is the homeland of the Naga, who consider themselves the legitimate successors of the Serpentari Imperium.

Where the Canopy Kingdoms derive strength from living forests and Concordance, and the Scarlands harbor fractured powers and hidden dangers, the Gilded Coil embodies a different philosophy:

Civilization is the art of forcing an inhospitable world to become hospitable.

The Gilded Coil's cities do not merely occupy the surrounding environment. They actively transform it.

Rivers are redirected into fertile valleys, arid plains become orchards, temperatures are regulated through vast subterranean mechanisms, and ancient Imperial ruins are transformed into thriving urban centers.

Much of this apparent prosperity depends upon the engineering of the Kobolds, who maintain the magical infrastructure that allows the Empire to function.

And most of those Kobolds are enslaved.

The Naga boast that their realm offers greater comfort, sophistication, and security than nearly anywhere else in Mystaria.

In many respects, they are correct.

The cost of that comfort is another matter.

## II. Geography — The Land That Obeys

The Gilded Coil occupies a series of warm, mineral-rich valleys surrounded by dry uplands, rocky plateaus, and broad expanses of semiarid wilderness.

Its principal settlements follow the ancient Imperial waterways and road systems. Several rivers descend from distant mountain ranges, feeding enormous reservoirs, artificial lakes, and irrigation channels.

Without technological intervention, much of the region would be considerably drier and less productive.

The Serpentari originally selected these lands because of their vast mineral deposits, underground mana currents, geothermal springs, and extensive natural caverns.

The Naga inherited these advantages alongside the ruins of the civilization that exploited them.

### The Three Landscapes

The Inner Gardens

The fertile regions surrounding functioning Imperial cities. Carefully maintained climates support orchards, flowering gardens, warm lakes, intensive agriculture, and luxurious estates.

These are among the most comfortable environments in Mystaria, provided the magical infrastructure remains operational.

The Outer Reaches

Agricultural frontier lands, quarry towns, mining settlements, caravan routes, and estates beyond the strongest terraforming fields.

Conditions are harsher, settlements more isolated, and Starspawn attacks considerably more troublesome.

The Silent Works

Regions where Imperial terraforming systems failed during or after the God War.

They contain abandoned cities, ruined canals, unstable magical environments, dangerous Starspawn territories, and enormous machines whose original functions have been forgotten.

The sharp distinction between flourishing Inner Gardens and dangerous Silent Works is one of the region's defining characteristics.

A traveler may stand in a blooming orchard with comfortable temperatures and abundant freshwater, then walk beyond the nearest functioning Imperial aqueduct and find themselves in a barren expanse of broken stone.

In some districts, the difference is visible from one side of a road to the other.

## III. The Imperial Cities — Machines Disguised as Palaces

Every major city in the Gilded Coil is built around an enormous surviving structure from the Serpentari Imperium.

Unlike modern Naga architecture, ancient Imperial buildings were designed to accommodate enormous serpents measuring seventy to one hundred and twenty feet long.

Their doors resemble city gates. Their corridors are broad enough for caravans. Their stairways include tremendous ramps and winding channels that once allowed colossal serpentine bodies to move between floors.

Ancient audience chambers can contain entire modern marketplaces.

The Naga have gradually filled these structures with smaller palaces, administrative buildings, artisan districts, workshops, public baths, and residential neighborhoods.

Consequently, a single Imperial palace may now house thousands of inhabitants.

The contrast between old and new construction is striking.

Ancient Serpentari stonework is massive, austere, and nearly indestructible. Modern Naga additions are ornate, comfortable, and overflowing with golden ornamentation, cushions, polished stone, delicate bridges, and magical conveniences.

The result is an architectural civilization built quite literally inside the skeleton of another.

### The Worldspindles

At the center of each major Imperial city stands a Worldspindle, a surviving Serpentari terraforming installation.

Originally, these colossal machines were operated through the Imperial Grammar and vast networks of enchantments.

Modern Naga generally cannot reproduce their original designs.

Instead, Kobold artificers have constructed intricate systems of computational Orbs, energy regulators, and magical interfaces that allow surviving Worldspindles to function in limited, controlled ways.

These modern additions are collectively called the Coilworks.

A functioning Worldspindle can regulate substantial portions of the surrounding landscape.

Its capabilities depend upon the available mana, ancient machinery, natural geography, and the expertise of the Kobolds maintaining it.

### How Terraformation Works

| Mana  | Worldspindle applications                                                                  |
| ----- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| Red   | Regulating heat, maintaining warm soil, heating reservoirs, powering industrial processes  |
| Blue  | Directing existing water, controlling irrigation, managing environmental measurements      |
| Green | Encouraging vegetation, maintaining productive orchards, restoring damaged soil ecosystems |
| White | Stabilizing structures, reinforcing canals, protecting delicate environmental systems      |
| Black | Accelerating controlled decomposition, processing waste, removing unstable enchantments    |

The Worldspindles cannot simply conjure infinite water, fertile land, or free energy.

They redistribute and transform existing resources, using enormous amounts of mana to maintain conditions that might not otherwise be sustainable.

This is their greatest practical limitation.

A city built along a substantial river has far more productive potential than one established in a nearly waterless desert.

Similarly, a region with strong natural mana currents can sustain more sophisticated terraforming than one dependent upon expensive imported power.

### The Cost of Paradise

A functioning Worldspindle requires continuous maintenance.

Thousands of magical components must be inspected, worn mechanisms replaced, computational Orbs recalibrated, and mana reserves carefully managed.

Kobold engineers monitor these systems through enormous networks of Magic Mirrors.

If maintenance fails, the surrounding environment begins reverting toward its natural condition.

Canals lose their reinforcement. Irrigation becomes unreliable. Artificially warmed agricultural districts grow colder. Delicate crops fail.

A serious breakdown can threaten an entire city.

The Gilded Coil is wealthy because its cities transform nature. It is vulnerable because they must never stop doing so.

## IV. The Great Cities of the Gilded Coil

Sathryx — The Hollow Throne

Imperial capital and administrative center. Built around an immense Serpentari palace whose central throne has stood empty since the God War.

Home to the Grand Coilnet archives, the largest governmental Mirror network, and the most powerful functioning Worldspindle. Sathryx's famous terraced gardens conceal densely populated servant districts and vast Kobold workshops.

Velmora — The Garden of Steam

Agricultural and medical center surrounded by geothermal springs.

Its Worldspindle maintains the Empire's most productive greenhouses, medicinal gardens, and experimental farms. Naga aristocrats travel here for luxurious baths and specialized treatments. Kobolds maintain its complex temperature and irrigation systems.

Serrath Quay — The Thousand Locks

Maritime trade center built around enormous Imperial floodgates.

Tidekin maintain its waterways, Kobolds operate the ancient locks, and Vren merchants conduct business beneath covered markets. The city connects the Empire's inland economy to distant coastal trading networks.

Kheth-Vael — The City of Ten Thousand Mirrors

Technological capital and center of advanced Orbcraft.

Its academies train Kobold engineers, its foundries manufacture computational Orbs, and its immense archival mirrors preserve fragments of Serpentari knowledge. The city is famous for invention, espionage, and explosions that officials insist were entirely intentional.

## V. Government — The Empire of Matriarchs

The Naga Empire is not ruled through a simple chain of command.

It is a federation of hereditary Naga Coils united by a central imperial government, common laws, technological infrastructure, and a shared determination to preserve Naga supremacy.

Its supreme political institution is the Conclave of Coils.

The Conclave consists of powerful Matriarchs whose families control major cities, Worldspindles, agricultural territories, and essential infrastructure.

From among themselves, they select a High Matriarch, who governs imperial affairs, foreign diplomacy, taxation, military coordination, and disputes between the great families.

In practice, her authority depends heavily upon the cooperation of other Matriarchs.

A provincial Matriarch who controls a vital Worldspindle can wield enormous political influence even if her actual Naga family contains only a handful of individuals.

### The Three Political Traditions

The Empire's principal ideological divisions correspond to established Naga factions.

The Keepers of the Empty Throne believe the Naga should preserve Imperial traditions until the surviving Serpentari return. They are especially powerful among conservative aristocrats and custodians of ancient ruins.

The Sovereign Coil believes the Naga have inherited the right to rule independently. Its members support technological advancement and strengthening the Empire rather than simply preserving Serpentari traditions.

The Unbound Daughters reject the inherited institutions of slavery and subservience. Some advocate gradual reform, while others seek complete emancipation and a new relationship between Naga and Kobolds.

The Conclave is dominated by the first two traditions, although the Unbound Daughters enjoy quiet support among certain younger Matriarchs and independent Kobold Workshops.

## VI. Economy — Wealth Built upon Dependence

The Gilded Coil possesses one of Mystaria's most diversified economies.

Its greatest exports are not swords or armies, but things that other civilizations find difficult to produce.

| Economic sector   | Principal products and services                                       | Main workforce                 |
| ----------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------ |
| Manatech          | Computational Orbs, Mirrors, regulators, enchanted instruments        | Kobolds                        |
| Agriculture       | Grain, fruit, medicines, flowers, luxury crops                        | Moontouched, Bloomkin, Kobolds |
| Manufacturing     | Precision components, metalwork, jewelry, magical equipment           | Kobolds, Bloomkin, Duskpaws    |
| Information       | Recordkeeping, long-distance messages, accounting, logistics          | Kobolds, Naga, Corvyn          |
| Transportation    | Canal shipping, caravan logistics, warehousing                        | Tidekin, Duskpaws, Kobolds     |
| Infrastructure    | Worldspindle maintenance, irrigation, waste processing                | Kobolds, Tidekin, Duskpaws     |
| Finance and trade | Loans, contracts, insurance, international commerce                   | Naga, Vren                     |
| Domestic services | Household administration, childcare, hospitality, personal attendants | Moontouched, Bloomkin, Kobolds |

### Technology as an Export

The Empire manufactures portable magical devices that other nations cannot easily reproduce.

Small Orbs capable of performing calculations, storing records, and operating simple enchantments are popular among wealthy foreign merchants.

Likewise, Magic Mirrors are increasingly fashionable in foreign aristocratic households.

However, the Empire carefully controls the export of advanced Orbcraft.

Most sophisticated devices require periodic Kobold maintenance, proprietary components, or access to compatible mana supplies.

A foreign noble may purchase a magnificent magical Mirror, but replacing its computational core could require an expensive visit from an Imperial Kobold technician.

This arrangement generates considerable continuing revenue.

### The Importance of Mana

Worldspindles consume enormous quantities of mana.

The Empire draws power from natural mana currents, charged reservoirs, compatible magical devices, and various mana-producing inhabitants.

Power must be carefully allocated between public infrastructure, private industry, military defenses, and luxury estates.

This creates a hidden political struggle.

A wealthy Matriarch might demand additional mana to maintain the climate around her private gardens, while a provincial town requires the same energy to sustain essential irrigation.

Imperial law prioritizes infrastructure according to official importance.

Naturally, the people deciding what constitutes official importance are usually the owners of the gardens.

### The Cost of Labor

Enslaved labor allows Naga households to control large workforces without negotiating ordinary wages.

However, the system is not free.

Food, housing, transport, medical treatment, security, specialized education, equipment, and replacement labor all impose substantial expenses.

Kobold engineers are especially costly to train and maintain. Losing an experienced Chief Artificer may cripple a Workshop for years.

The Empire therefore faces an uncomfortable economic truth:

Its most valuable workers are often the people it grants the least freedom.

## VII. Slavery — The Foundation of the Golden Order

Slavery is not a peripheral institution in the Gilded Coil. It shapes nearly every major aspect of society.

In the great Imperial cities, enslaved people commonly constitute a majority of the population.

Enslaved residents constitute a majority in the central Imperial cities. The proportion varies among wealthy estates, industrial districts, and frontier settlements; no exact empire-wide or central-city percentage is established.

Naga themselves are a small minority. Their exceptionally slow reproduction means they cannot personally perform or supervise every task required by their civilization.

Instead, they govern through hereditary authority, enslaved specialists, and elaborate technological administration.

### How the Empire Maintains Its Slave Population

The system persists through inherited status, internal population growth, trade, and extensive legal institutions.

Most enslaved people are born within existing households or Workshops rather than newly captured.

Children generally inherit their mother's legal status, although exceptional contractual arrangements may alter this rule.

Household populations are maintained through recorded births, marriages, adoption arrangements, transfers, and purchases.

The Empire also purchases enslaved people from foreign traders, especially through maritime networks and Vren commercial intermediaries.

Military prisoners, convicted debtors, and people condemned under certain Imperial laws form smaller additional sources.

Because many enslaved communities have lived within the Gilded Coil for generations, they possess deep cultural ties to the land.

A fourth-generation enslaved Kobold may never have seen another country.

They may genuinely regard Sathryx as their homeland while resenting the Matriarch who owns their Workshop.

### The Scale Register

The Naga maintain an immense technological slave registry called the Scale Register.

It operates through interconnected computational Orbs and Magic Mirrors.

The Register records legal ownership, household affiliation, labor assignments, family relationships, transfers, and identification collars.

A wealthy estate can use its administrative Mirrors to review its registered workforce, assign tasks, and monitor approved travel through connected checkpoints.

However, the system is not omniscient.

It cannot automatically perceive every person's location. Tracking depends upon registered devices, functioning sensors, accurate records, and available communications.

Duskpaw smugglers and sympathetic Kobold technicians have become skilled at exploiting these limitations.

### Collars and Restraints

Every enslaved person is required to wear an identifying collar, as established by Mystaria's broader slaveholding customs.

Within the Gilded Coil, collars typically display the insignia of the owning Coil and an identification mark recognized by the Scale Register.

Ordinary collars are physical objects. Expensive examples incorporate enchantments, but sophisticated magical restraints are too costly to equip every enslaved laborer.

Additional restraints are commonplace during transportation, confinement, punishment, and particularly sensitive assignments.

Gags and muzzles are also frequently used to enforce temporary silence, especially during public ceremonies, disciplinary proceedings, or when captives are suspected of coordinating an escape.

They are not generally worn continuously during ordinary work.

Kobold engineers must communicate intricate technical instructions. Tidekin must coordinate waterway operations. Moontouched attendants must respond to household needs.

Silencing them permanently would interfere with the very labor their owners expect them to perform.

### The Hierarchy of Servitude

Not every enslaved person occupies the same social position.

An accomplished Kobold Chief Artificer may supervise hundreds of workers, manage substantial technical resources, and advise a Matriarch on important policy decisions.

An enslaved Corvyn witch might be treated as a prestigious household adviser.

A Moontouched attendant could enjoy a comfortable private room, fine clothing, and substantial personal affection from her owner.

Yet all remain subject to the same underlying legal inequality.

A privileged slave may possess considerable authority over others without possessing legal authority over their own life.

This produces resentment between enslaved communities, particularly when privileged supervisors are required to enforce the rules imposed upon less fortunate workers.

### Emancipation

Manumission is legally possible, though uncommon.

A Matriarch may free a favored servant, reward exceptional service, or permit a household to purchase emancipation under specified terms.

However, release does not automatically grant citizenship, property, or unrestricted access to Imperial technology.

Former slaves may remain economically dependent upon their previous households.

Some Unbound Daughters advocate recognizing free Kobold Workshops and granting equal legal status to all sapient peoples.

Traditionalists argue that abolishing slavery would destabilize the economy and threaten the maintenance of the Worldspindles.

Their opponents respond that an economy dependent upon unwilling workers is fundamentally unstable already.

## VIII. Law Enforcement — The Eyes of the Coil

The Naga Empire possesses sophisticated law enforcement institutions supported by Manatech.

Unlike the relatively decentralized knightly justice of the Canopy Kingdoms, Imperial law emphasizes registration, investigation, documentation, and administrative enforcement.

### The Principal Institutions

The Scale Guard

The Empire's principal police and security force. Led by Naga officers and supported by mixed-race personnel, it patrols cities, protects public infrastructure, guards government facilities, and enforces Imperial law.

Its equipment includes magical communication devices, reinforced restraints, and portable defensive enchantments.

The Mirrorwardens

Investigators who specialize in using the Coilnet, Scale Register, archival records, and Manatech surveillance.

They investigate fraud, smuggling, sabotage, illegal ownership transfers, and threats to Imperial infrastructure.

Their greatest weakness is their dependence upon accurate information. A compromised Orb can make an innocent person appear guilty or erase evidence of a powerful Matriarch's wrongdoing.

The Coil Tribunals

Courts presided over by Naga magistrates, responsible for criminal law, property disputes, contractual disagreements, and slave ownership cases.

Their proceedings are meticulously documented, but enslaved people possess fewer legal protections than free citizens.

Corruption is particularly dangerous because judicial decisions can be recorded as authoritative facts within the Empire's administrative systems.

The Spindlewatch

A specialized infrastructure protection service responsible for Worldspindles, mana reservoirs, relay stations, and other critical machinery.

It employs many Kobolds because only they can perform the most delicate Orbcraft maintenance.

Its members occupy an uncomfortable position: enslaved technicians are often responsible for safeguarding the mechanisms that help preserve their own enslavement.

### Imperial Justice and Arrest

Some magistrates admire Arrest's teachings concerning evidence, responsibility, and legitimate authority.

However, her opposition to inherited slavery places them in direct conflict with her actual philosophy.

The Empire has occasionally permitted Drider investigators to examine particularly serious crimes, but their presence is politically uncomfortable.

A Drider might recognize the procedural sophistication of the Coil Tribunals while rejecting the laws those tribunals enforce.

The Naga prefer investigators who appreciate the first point and remain tactfully silent about the second.

## IX. Ecology — The Artificial Paradise

The Gilded Coil possesses a highly unusual ecology because large portions of its landscape are artificially sustained.

As elsewhere in Mystaria, Starspawn constitute its wildlife. Ordinary nonsapient animals do not exist as a separate ecological population.

### Engineered Habitats

Within the Inner Gardens, the Worldspindles sustain unusually productive ecosystems.

Green-affinity Starspawn encourage plant growth. Blue-affinity aquatic creatures inhabit reservoirs and waterways. White-affinity varieties help maintain stable environments.

Briarbacks are particularly welcome in certain cultivated gardens because their natural green-mana influence encourages vegetation.

Prismrays sometimes inhabit deep reservoirs, where their blue-mana abilities interact with artificial currents.

Gravetufts and other black-affinity scavengers contribute to decomposition and waste processing, although they can cause problems around delicate enchantments.

The Naga have learned to accommodate some Starspawn rather than simply attempting to eradicate them.

This is partly pragmatic.

A Starspawn whose body is destroyed will eventually undergo Celestial Recall and return. Repeated violence does not permanently eliminate the problem.

Some agricultural estates therefore establish negotiated territorial arrangements, safe corridors, and mutually beneficial relationships with unusually intelligent Starspawn.

Others attempt coercion, frequently discovering that an immortal creature can nurture resentment for a very long time.

### Ecological Instability

Artificial environments are not necessarily balanced environments.

A Worldspindle that encourages excessive growth can produce dense vegetation that overwhelms nearby settlements.

Improper water management can create stagnant wetlands or destabilize river ecosystems.

Black-mana waste processing may cause dangerous deterioration if allowed to spread beyond its intended boundaries.

Furthermore, powerful mana concentrations attract certain Starspawn.

Some creatures deliberately inhabit the edges of terraforming fields, feeding upon cultivated ecosystems or residual magical energy.

Consequently, the Empire maintains extensive environmental monitoring systems.

Kobold engineers frequently discover that modifying one ecological system creates unexpected consequences elsewhere.

Their usual response is to design another machine.

This sometimes helps.

## X. The Peoples of the Gilded Coil

The Gilded Coil contains substantial populations of several races, each occupying distinct economic and social positions.

Importantly, a person's satisfaction with their occupation, household, or community does not necessarily mean they approve of their legal status.

Many enslaved residents love the cities where they were born while opposing the institutions governing their lives.

### Population and Social Tensions

| Race        | Position in the Empire                         | Sources of contentment                                                         | Sources of discontent                                                     |
| ----------- | ---------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------ | ------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Naga        | Ruling aristocracy                             | Luxury, authority, security, technological prosperity                          | Political rivalry, demographic decline, technological dependence          |
| Kobolds     | Principal enslaved engineers                   | Research facilities, Workshop communities, approval of imprinted masters       | Lack of autonomy, forced imprinting arrangements, ownership of inventions |
| Moontouched | Common household and agricultural slaves       | Stable Hearth Circles, comfortable households, gardens, personal relationships | Collars, coercion, family separation, lack of freedom                     |
| Bloomkin    | Craftspeople, administrators, fine technicians | Skilled work, miniature communities, intellectual opportunities                | Hereditary servitude, limited rights, separation from Thornguard partners |
| Duskpaws    | Maintenance workers, traders, smugglers        | Economic opportunity, urban networks, resourceful communities                  | Restrictive surveillance, prejudice, criminal exploitation                |
| Tidekin     | Canal operators, maritime laborers, merchants  | Abundant waterways, Raft communities, watercraft employment                    | Saltbound slavery, dangerous labor, restrictions on movement              |
| Corvyn      | Rare but influential advisers and witches      | Archives, research, access to magical knowledge                                | Captivity, surveillance, interference with Witchbonds                     |
| Vren        | Merchants, financiers, contractors             | Profitable trade, political leverage, access to sophisticated services         | Legal disputes, rival aristocrats, restricted independence                |

### Naga — The Golden Aristocracy

The Naga enjoy a standard of living that would astonish many foreign nobles.

Their homes possess magical lighting, advanced sanitation, temperature regulation, information Mirrors, and elaborate bathing facilities.

Even relatively modest Naga households often have access to amenities unavailable to wealthy citizens elsewhere.

Their greatest anxieties arise from the fragility of this prosperity.

The Naga reproduce slowly, possess strong hereditary loyalties, and depend upon Kobold engineering for nearly every major technological advantage.

Some younger Naga resent the rigidity of the aristocracy and fear that their civilization has become too dependent upon institutions it cannot easily reform.

A progressive Matriarch might love her family, her ancestral city, and her Kobold companions while wondering whether ownership is necessary to preserve any of them.

### Kobolds — The Indispensable Servants

Kobolds form the intellectual and technical backbone of the Empire.

Many receive excellent engineering educations, work in sophisticated laboratories, and enjoy remarkable opportunities to experiment.

Their biological tendency toward imprinting can produce genuine affection for their Naga masters.

A Kobold may sincerely adore the Matriarch who raised them, feel proud to wear her colors, and dedicate years to creating a machine that will make her happy.

Yet Kobolds possess ambitions of their own.

Some wish to establish independent Workshops, own their inventions, or decide which projects deserve their attention.

Others resent the deliberate management of hatchlings' imprinting environments.

A growing minority believes the Empire's greatest technological limitation is not a lack of mana or resources, but its refusal to allow its most inventive population to govern itself.

These Kobolds are especially dangerous to the established order because they understand precisely how that order functions.

### Moontouched — The Comfortable Captives

Moontouched are common in wealthy households, hospitality establishments, agricultural gardens, and communal caregiving roles.

Their natural empathy and cooperative tendencies make them valued companions.

Many live in comfortable environments with regular meals, warm bedding, and opportunities to form Hearth Circles.

Some feel genuine affection toward their Naga owners, particularly when raised in households that emphasize gentle treatment.

However, Moontouched are especially sensitive to coercion disguised as kindness.

They may be praised for devotion while being denied the freedom to leave.

A Moontouched servant might have an affectionate relationship with a Matriarch who sincerely believes providing comfort makes their ownership legitimate.

Surrender's Tenderhands condemn this reasoning, reminding their followers that what is taken cannot be surrendered.

Moontouched cannot generate conventional mana, but they can operate externally powered Manatech and use compatible magical items through their established equipment-only Soul Capacity.

### Bloomkin — The Little Specialists

Bloomkin frequently work as precision technicians, miniature instrument makers, recordkeepers, and fine craftspeople.

Their extraordinary dexterity and Intelligence make them valuable assistants to Kobold engineers.

Some specialize in maintaining delicate Mirror components or assisting with white-mana stabilizing systems using externally supplied energy.

However, they cannot perform full Concordance independently. That unique magical partnership still requires Thornguard.

Bloomkin communities often maintain elaborate miniature neighborhoods within the Imperial cities.

They enjoy social gatherings, communal meals, craft competitions, and the exchange of tiny handmade treasures.

Some appreciate the relative security of the Gilded Coil.

Others resent having exchanged hereditary servitude under Thornguard Banners for another form of bondage under Naga households.

Hearthwhispers are particularly active among these communities, quietly promoting literacy, mutual assistance, and emancipation.

### Duskpaws — The Cracks in the Machine

Duskpaws occupy an unusual social position.

They are exceptionally useful in maintaining crowded urban environments, especially sanitation systems, storage facilities, and difficult-to-reach machinery.

Their agility and dexterity make them excellent technicians, while Fraycraft gives them a dangerous understanding of structural weaknesses.

The Empire both values and distrusts them.

Many Duskpaws operate legitimate businesses, participate in urban trade, and maintain thriving communal Caches.

Others belong to underground organizations that specialize in smuggling, forged registration records, stolen Orb components, and the transportation of escaped slaves.

The Underkeys are particularly active in the Gilded Coil.

An enslaved Duskpaw who works on the Scale Register's maintenance network might possess exactly the knowledge needed to make another person's ownership record disappear.

This makes Duskpaws disproportionately influential in the Empire's hidden resistance movements.

### Tidekin — The Voices of the Canals

Tidekin are common along the Empire's waterways and port districts.

They serve as boat operators, canal inspectors, navigators, merchants, and maintenance specialists.

Their blue-mana Wakecraft is particularly useful in managing existing water currents and investigating accidents through Mirrorwake.

Independent Tidekin Rafts sometimes prosper through commercial partnerships with the Empire.

Enslaved Tidekin, often called Saltbound, face more restrictive conditions.

They may be assigned to dangerous maintenance operations, confined to particular waterways, or prevented from joining independent Rafts.

Nevertheless, Tidekin retain their famously irreverent attitudes.

A Naga official may possess three administrative Orbs, a magically reinforced collar, and a century of political experience, yet remain completely incapable of preventing a Tidekin worker from commenting on the official's terrible judgment.

Gags and muzzles are sometimes imposed during disciplinary proceedings, but Tidekin speech remains essential to maritime coordination.

Their communities are also among the most vocal critics of the Empire's claim to be perfectly orderly.

### Corvyn — The Witches Behind the Mirrors

Corvyn are extremely rare throughout Mystaria, so even their concentrated communities in the Gilded Coil remain numerically small.

Their political significance, however, exceeds their population.

Naga Matriarchs highly prize Corvyn witches as advisers, historians, investigators, and magical specialists.

Their Veilsight allows them to detect certain supernatural phenomena that ordinary Manatech cannot easily recognize.

Their Living Ink and Witchbonds provide access to magical techniques across several mana colors.

Some Corvyn enjoy access to extraordinary libraries and research facilities.

Others are deeply unhappy with the scrutiny imposed upon their tattoos, familiars, and private magical practices.

A Corvyn who possesses dangerous knowledge may find herself simultaneously honored as an irreplaceable adviser and restricted as a potential security threat.

Independent Flights often distrust the Empire, particularly because Corvyn history includes enslavement under the Serpentari.

### Vren — The Merchants of the Red Roads

Vren maintain a comparatively small but economically influential population in the Gilded Coil.

They operate trading houses, contractual services, lending institutions, and commercial partnerships.

Their familiarity with complicated agreements makes them valuable intermediaries between Naga aristocrats and foreign powers.

Some Vren benefit enormously from the Empire's commercial sophistication.

Others resent Naga attempts to subordinate Vren Blood Bonds to Imperial registration laws.

Competition between Vren contractors and Naga legal institutions can become exceptionally bitter.

Vren also face the practical problem of the Gilded Coil's bright, warm climate. Their sunlight vulnerability makes covered markets, underground establishments, and nighttime commercial activity particularly important.

Their relationship with the Empire is therefore one of profitable cooperation and mutual suspicion.

### Firstshaped Residents

Firstshaped occur among the Gilded Coil's established races and retain their full intelligence and racial identity.

The Empire's technological infrastructure can provide useful accommodations, including accessible controls, specialized furniture, and adapted equipment.

However, Firstshaped slaves are particularly vulnerable to being treated as ornamental possessions.

Firstshaped Naga occupy an especially distinctive position. They retain full membership in their Coils despite their enormous, completely handless serpentine bodies.

Some command estates through spoken instructions, adapted controls, prehensile tails, and Kobold-operated Mirrors.

A Firstshaped Naga Matriarch may possess no hands whatsoever and nevertheless govern an entire city district.

### Military Strengths

The Naga Empire excels at defensive warfare.

Its cities possess reinforced walls, automated alarms, sophisticated communication systems, and enormous logistical resources.

Naga officers can coordinate distant forces through the Coilnet while Kobold technicians maintain defensive machinery.

The Empire also possesses substantial financial resources and can field professional soldiers alongside equipped retainers and contracted mercenaries.

### Military Weaknesses

Much of its technological advantage disappears beyond the range of established infrastructure.

Portable Manatech is useful, but cannot reproduce the capabilities of an Imperial city's Worldspindle or Grand Orb.

Its field armies also struggle in terrain that prevents the deployment of heavy equipment or reliable mana supplies.

A Thornguard knight supported by a skilled Bloomkin Shieldmate remains an exceptionally dangerous opponent.

Vren infiltrators can exploit political corruption, while Duskpaw saboteurs can damage machinery at vulnerable points.

The Empire can defend its cities brilliantly.

Conquering distant territories is considerably more difficult.

### The Demographic Weakness

The Naga's greatest structural vulnerability is their small population.

Their Great Molts occur infrequently, producing relatively few children.

Meanwhile, the Kobolds and several other enslaved populations reproduce much more rapidly.

Every passing generation increases the numerical disparity between rulers and workers.

The Naga compensate through institutions, technology, wealth, and their ability to cultivate genuine loyalty.

But the system cannot easily survive a widespread collapse of that loyalty.

## XII. The Great Contradiction of the Gilded Coil

The Gilded Coil embodies both the greatest achievements and the most persistent failures of Serpentari civilization.

It has preserved extraordinary architecture, cultivated beautiful environments, developed sophisticated technology, and created some of the most comfortable cities in Mystaria.

Its inhabitants enjoy conveniences that many outsiders would regard as miraculous.

Yet much of that prosperity rests upon slavery.

The Naga insist that their civilization demonstrates the benefits of order, responsibility, and protective authority.

The Kobolds genuinely love many of their masters.

The Moontouched often enjoy the companionship of the households where they live.

Bloomkin find pride in their craftsmanship. Tidekin adore their waterways. Duskpaws thrive in the vast urban labyrinths.

These attachments are real.

So is the fact that many of those people cannot freely leave.

The Empire's future may therefore depend upon a question its Matriarchs are reluctant to ask:

If the Gilded Coil is truly the greatest civilization in Mystaria, why must so many of the people who built it be compelled to remain?

That question is not merely philosophical. It threatens the foundations of the Empire's economy, technology, laws, and political authority.

And somewhere beneath the enormous empty throne of Sathryx, ancient Serpentari machinery continues to operate, tended by generations of Kobold engineers who understand increasingly more about its functions.

Perhaps one day they will discover what the throne was actually built to control.
