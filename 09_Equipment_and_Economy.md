### Centaur equipment affinity — CANON

Centaurs take **half equipment-derived Soul Strain** and gain **half equipment-derived numerical stat bonuses**, each rounded upward **once at the item total**. Other physical properties, armor functionality, weapon properties, and activated powers remain unhalved. This reduction does not apply to other Soul Strain such as dragonmarks or Celestite soul destruction. See `07_Centaurs_and_Smithcraft.md`.

## November 5, 2026 — Modular smithcraft, shop access, and prayer-charged reservoirs (LATEST)

### Approved design rules and supersession

**Fully Modular Architecture B approved.** Equipment is modular in form, material, quality, magical installation, and combat application. The quality I–V craftsmanship ladder is retained. **Moonlit Forge +1 through +5 equipment reinforcement and its independent Gem-price ladder are RETIRED / SUPERSEDED for current design**; do not offer a second divine enhancement track. **Historical Moonlit Forge prices and Moonlit Garden equipment prices are not controlling for current new designs**, though the older catalogue remains historical lore and the agreed five-quality benchmarks may inform economics. Shimmer shop goods are fairly priced and may be higher-level items purchasable in advance, but usable physical equipment is constrained by actual STR/DEX or handling requirements, and the best combinations are expected to need direct Centaur craftsmanship, multiple disciplines/master smiths, and substantial materials. The five-quality stat-bonus/Soul-Strain benchmark table remains in effect **pending detailed reallocation**; avoid treating suggested rune bonuses as additional unrestricted budgets.

**Physical item strength** is balanced by form/coverage, appropriate weapon stat requirements, weight, SPD penalties and strength/handling (not mainly Gem cost). **Magical enhancements** are balanced chiefly by **cumulative equipment Soul Strain** and item limits rather than cost. Multiple items cannot freely multiply the same named magic technique: **multiple implements cannot enhance the same magic simultaneously**. Heavy armor cannot ordinarily be layered as multiple independent suits; **integrated light-equivalent underarmor at Medium/Heavy covered joints/gaps** replaces separate light armor layer bookkeeping, without double-counting coverage (latest approved armor ruling below). Exact item-slot counts, local protection stacking and strain caps remain under design.

Weapons may scale with **STR, DEX, either STR or DEX, or a deliberately rewarding combination of STR and DEX**, with corresponding physical requirements. Simple/less powerful weapons such as a dagger or longsword may use either; unarmed attacks may use either. Balanced-stat weapon designs should earn significantly more generous scaling in return for qualifying in BOTH attributes and sacrificing single-stat specialization. Existing base multipliers (unarmed 0.85, dagger 1.0, gauntlets 1.0) remain until explicitly replaced; long/greatsword proposed baselines and STR 400 progression have no finalized universal model. This direction does not grant additional attacks or free Skill Points.

### Prayer-charged divine reservoirs (CANON principle; numerical completion unresolved)

Former `Manacistern` is a **working name**, subject to replacement. These reservoirs are **divine technology**, charged by a creature with **compatible mana affinity** through actual **prayer/devotion**, not by freely transferring ordinary personal MP and not by passively regenerating with rest. Prayer may be sincerely directed toward **any person or object of devotion**, not necessarily one of Mystaria's three goddesses. This draws upon a **basic form of the devotional/divine-energy principle** associated with the Shimmer and Legends, but does not grant Hero powers or independent mana generation without the special apparatus. Religious convention often **incorrectly insists the prayer must address recognized deities**; smiths often know otherwise but are constrained from explaining this to their masters by entrenched social and religious norms.

**Recharge design target:** approximately **1 MP per prayer turn for each 10 effective END** of the praying compatible-affinity contributor at full sincerity; an **unenthusiastic prayer loses 90% efficiency** (produces only 10% of the devotional rate), while wholehearted devotion to another is fully efficient. Exact rounding, partial progress, turn time, maximum concurrent contributors, capacity by item quality, prayer quality adjudication, strain, and exploitation constraints are **UNRESOLVED**. The *recharge rate* should not be mistaken for the reservoir's *storage capacity*. Praying consumes real participant effort and action time; it is not an unpriced, instant combat refill. Historical one-color capacity and discharge tables in the earlier proposal have not been approved.

## November 5, 2026 — Passive elemental runes: Conditional Contact Effect chosen (latest)

**APPROVED DESIGN SELECTION:** Of the three previously reviewed passive elemental damage procedures, the author selects **B: Conditional Contact Effect**. A passively enchanted weapon can impart a limited elemental consequence when its strike **actually makes appropriate contact** with armor or the target, even where the physical strike does not penetrate or cause an injury. A **complete dodge yields no elemental contact**. This is not the separate full elemental Power packet (Option A) or a mandatory physical-Power split (Option C).

**Preserved boundaries:** Ranks I–IV remain substantially physical weapons with progressively stronger passive elemental effects; rank V transforms the weapon entirely into its element and **replaces, not stacks, the original material bonus**. Passive elemental runes never require an activation action and should generally be weaker than activatable Sorcery gems. Element-specific resistances and defenses must be especially effective. Fire can heat armor, ice can freeze limbs or weaken susceptible armor, shock excels against conductive metal armor and is weak against suitable glass/insulating metals, and toxic can deliver hex whose quantitative side effects remain unresolved. Ordinary protection, cover, shielding and the material's actual thermal/electrical/chemical pathways still determine whether contact can reach the wearer. Do not treat touching an isolated shield as automatically burning its bearer.

**STATUS OF NUMBERS:** Rank I–V contact coefficients, status effects/durations, resistance multipliers, elemental-material interactions and injury eligibility are **PROPOSALS ONLY** pending author review in `24_Modular_Equipment_Design_Options_DRAFT.md`. No new MP or SP activation costs established, no free extra attacks, and no alteration to the approved physical injury thresholds, six-category evasion table, END baseline Resistance, or equipment Soul Strain economy.

## November 5, 2026 — Approved Hero Gem economy and wealth progression (LATEST)

**Status: CANON, November 5, 2026 author approval.** The following is a **typical-Hero economic balancing target**, not an entitlement, mandatory equipment loadout, compulsory spending pattern, or automatic reward for every kill. Gem earnings remain subject to the Hero's patron and the circumstances of the qualifying achievement; ordinary mundane quests do not automatically create divine Gems.

### Equipment wealth target

The **current equipment portfolio's original purchase value** (not resale value or cumulative lifetime purchases) at Hero level `L`, for **1 ≤ L ≤ 100**, is modeled as:

```text
W(L) = 3 + 3997 × ((L − 1) / 99)^2.3 Gems
```

This is a target curve; round portfolio benchmarks to whole Gems for planning. It gives **W(1) = 3 Gems** and **W(100) = 4,000 Gems**. Level 1 is calibrated around **one qualifying encounter or quest**; at level 100, XP pacing is approximately **50 equal-level Henchman-equivalent encounters per level**. The 4,000 Gems represents a **typical Hero who replaces equipment at every quality tier**, not an unusually thrifty Hero. Equipment quality I–V's established **14 / 30 / 50 / 70 / 90 Gem benchmarks** remain unchanged. They are baseline quality values, **not a cap on finished-item price**; material, form, craftsmanship and magical installations can make a completed item much more valuable. Final modular component price tables remain unresolved.

### Approved expenditure and buyback principles

- By level 100, **approximately 30% of all Gems earned throughout the Hero's lifetime** should have been spent on **consumables**. This is a typical spending allocation, not a required purchase or automatic deduction. Gem-costed Nymbrin alchemy should be balanced against this budget.
- Gem Shops buy back **items they previously sold** for **50% of the item's original purchase price**. This applies to eligible previously sold shop items, not automatically to every found, commissioned, or otherwise acquired object. Whether an individual item is eligible follows its actual shop provenance. The rule does not establish arbitrary resale prices for items never sold by a Gem Shop.
- Replacement losses are part of **expected normal Gem earnings**, so Heroes who regularly upgrade are not systematically priced out of the target portfolio.
- No separate free Gem reimbursement is generated by selling equipment; the resale amount is a transaction, while the reward curve budgets for the typical loss.

### Reference full-replacement portfolio (approved balancing model)

For economic calibration, use the **representative** quality milestones below. These are **not equipment-use level gates**, and actual Heroes can replace pieces gradually or retain old equipment.

| Hero level | Reference equipment tier | Target portfolio W(L), rounded |
| ---: | --- | ---: |
| 1 | Starter | 3 Gems |
| 10 | I | 19 Gems |
| 25 | II | 157 Gems |
| 50 | III | 796 Gems |
| 75 | IV | 2,049 Gems |
| 100 | V | 4,000 Gems |

The reference Hero sells the preceding complete portfolio at the **next** milestone: at level 25, sell the level-10 portfolio; at 50, sell level-25; at 75, sell level-50; at 100, sell level-75. Thus the four reference resale losses are **9.5 + 78.5 + 398 + 1,024.5 = 1,510.5 Gems**. These are **expected economic losses**, not extra mandatory shop fees. Because individual Gem payout and buyback rounding has not been established, half-Gem quantities here are **budget arithmetic only**, not a declaration that fractional Gems physically circulate.

Define **R(L)** as the cumulative expected resale losses from these milestone transitions reached by level L:

```text
R(L) = 0.5 × [
  (L ≥ 25 ? W(10) : 0) +
  (L ≥ 50 ? W(25) : 0) +
  (L ≥ 75 ? W(50) : 0) +
  (L ≥ 100 ? W(75) : 0)
]
```

For this **reference full-replacement schedule**, cumulative lifetime Gem earnings are budgeted as:

```text
E(L) = [W(L) + R(L)] / 0.70
```

At level 100: **W = 4,000**, **R ≈ 1,510.5**, **E ≈ 7,872 Gems lifetime earned**, and **0.30 × E ≈ 2,362 Gems spent on consumables**. These are approximate targets, not guaranteed personal totals. A Hero who retains gear or spends differently may have a different portfolio; do not force their actual earnings to equal the reference calculation. At level 1, the model's 3-Gem equipment target can be reached through one qualifying encounter or quest; the mathematical gross-income planning figure E(1) is about 4.29 Gems, **not** a mandate to award fractional Gems or to require consumable spending before the first purchase.

For **ordinary qualifying encounters**:

## November 6, 2026 — Melee weapon shapes and hand equipment: author-approved design (LATEST)

**The user has selected a modular three-way melee-shape catalogue:** **Light** (normally one-handed daggers, knuckles, short knives and similar), **Medium** (normally one-handed arming/longswords, maces, axes, picks, etc.), **Heavy** (normally two-handed greatswords, mauls, polearms, etc.). Class is the **form/handling architecture**, not a fixed mass: dense materials such as Adamantite can increase the STR requirement without changing a dagger's reach into that of a greatsword. Specific shapes are in the proposal draft; individual numerical requirements and full weapon shape catalog are not yet final.

**Approved preferred damage-form concept:** each weapon has a **primary** damage form and may carry **explicitly listed alternative forms**, used without extra attacks. **Piercing** has relative **Injury 1/5, Armor Bypass 5/5**, narrow coverage; **Slashing** has **Injury 5/5, Armor Bypass 1/5** and maximal coverage; **Blunt** has **Injury 3/5, Armor Bypass 3/5**, medium coverage. These are approved **comparative five-step design ratings, NOT fixed percentages, Power multipliers or automatic damage**. Piercing penetrates at a point, blunt transmits trauma through armor; slashing especially excels at broad multi-target contact. The user explicitly prefers reducing blunt's **effective injury ratio rather than a one-injury-state hard cap**: an overwhelmingly powerful maul can still inflict devastating injury. Do not double-penalize blunt with both a low Power ratio and an extra generic injury cap. Preserve physical defense, applicable coverage, material-specific effects, and Shimmer Guard.

**Approved dual-wield direction:** dual wielding grants **one meaningful mechanical secondary-weapon assignment** each attack/defensive exchange: **Attack** makes the combined threat **harder to evade**; **Defense** improves the wielder's ability to **avoid being hit**. This does NOT grant extra actions, automatically double attacks/Power or change basic injury thresholds; exact evasion-tier/Speed adjustment and qualification checks are pending. A sufficiently strong character may one-hand a normally two-handed weapon and dual-wield normally two-handed weapons; **150% of normal heavy-weapon STR requirement for one-handed handling and 200% for dual-heavy** are adopted as working thresholds from the author's acceptance of recommendations, **subject to final geometry/DEX and material calibration**. Strong grip alone does not guarantee safe leverage with very long polearms. Dual wield requires available independent hands/limbs; equipment conflicts must be resolved.

**Shields held in hand (approved direction):** a shield **occupies a hand** and can increase **Defense**. The user's request explicitly reserves a more nuanced shield system for the later armor pass: distinguish passive covered-area armor, active block/deflection and possible dodge benefit only when justified; do not grant an automatic full-body or universal Resistance increase. The shield hand generally cannot simultaneously serve as a free dual-wield attack weapon, and shield bash needs an explicit attack option. Numeric shield block, coverage, breakage, attacks and hand exceptions remain unresolved.

**Weapon shape attributes distinct:** **Reach** is distance accessible to contact; **Coverage** is width/targets an attack motion can actually reach. Slash weapons typically cover broad arcs while thrusting weapons follow narrower lines. The user approved keeping them distinct. Damage form may change by deliberate weapon face/edge/point use. STR, DEX, either or both are recognized scaling routes; precise comparison coefficients and prerequisites remain outstanding. Do not silently restore older combat-only +10 in addition to the approved +10 racial base rebase.

**General battle/group cross-reference:** The user explicitly requires weapon coverage and damage to apply to **groups of all enemy types** (monster hordes, gangs and armies), not exclusively Centaur machines. One attack may fell multiple genuinely covered lower-tier enemies when sufficient Power overcomes each target's applicable Resistance/armor; this does not mean extra attack actions, infinite damage spillover or automatic pressure from weak mobs. Revising old 'horde enemies' into statistically coherent **group/squad combatants** is an important OPEN DESIGN TASK. Construct casualties generally count machines felled; living horde groups require their own injury/Guard/individual exception rules.

## November 6, 2026 — Reach, close quarters and weapon form baseline (LATEST AUTHOR RULING)

**APPROVED DISTINCTION:** Every melee weapon has separate **handling class** (Light/Medium/Heavy), **reach class** (**Short/Medium/Long**), and **attack coverage** (area/geometry contacted by the specific attack). Reach is not raw weapon mass, and coverage is not synonymous with reach. The existing piercing/slashing/blunt relative roles and author-approved ratings remain unchanged. The suggested armor-rating bypass **percentages and exact injury coefficients remain deferred** until the armor system is designed; retain them strictly as noncanon draft.

**APPROVED REACH ROLES:** **Short reach** is excellent in grapples, confines and as a backup weapon and can be used wherever the wielder has enough space to physically move. **Medium reach** is the versatile middle ground. **Long reach** can, when properly controlled, **prevent a Short-reach opponent from attacking** until they close, while making it **more difficult for Medium-reach opponents to close and attack** rather than absolutely denying them. Reach defense is **conditional on actually maintaining the distance/control**; if the long-weapon user is under *genuine qualifying tactical pressure*, the controlling reach advantage is lost. A Short-reach fighter who has entered a **grapple inside the Long weapon's functional reach** cannot be struck with an ordinary Long-reach attack until the wielder changes position, escapes, changes to a legal shorter option, or employs a specifically applicable technique. The long-weapon wielder is not barred from otherwise legal unarmed or escape actions. Relative SPD/DEX, movement, terrain and opponent capability still determine whether the approach or grapple succeeds. Mere weak/ineffective enemy presence never automatically strips reach control under the approved stat-based pressure philosophy. Exact evasion category adjustments, action costs and distance thresholds remain unresolved.

**Proposed mundane proficiency:** Record **Choking Up** as a prospective technique in the **NONCANON** SP-purchased mundane Proficiencies design backlog: adjust grip on a long-reach weapon to fight at nearer distances. This is **not** an approved skill, SP cost, prerequisite or automatic built-in universal bypass of reach limitations.

**SHIELDS ACCEPTED FOR FUTURE ARMOR PASS:** Handheld shields occupy a hand and aid **Defense**; develop three distinguishable roles: (1) passive **location-specific coverage**, (2) active **directional blocking/parrying**, and (3) a contextually applicable **defensive maneuver/evasion** benefit. Do not grant a universal full-body Armor Rating or automatically combine every shield benefit each exchange. Details on hand/forearm restraints, shield bash, shield damage and numeric effects remain unresolved. Offhand dual-wield Attack versus Defense choice remains separately approved and cannot provide free attack actions.

**LIGHT BASELINE CLARIFICATION (LATEST):** The author declares the old **dagger 1.00× attack-rating modifier a nonessential placeholder**. ## November 6, 2026 — Finalized coverage labels and both-attribute weapon scaling (LATEST)

**APPROVED COVERAGE NAMES:** The five intuitive attack-coverage categories are **Point**, **Narrow**, **Sweep**, **Wide Sweep**, and **Line**. These are *attack geometries*, not distances: Point is one concentrated location; Narrow is a compact frontward attack area; Sweep is a broad arc; Wide Sweep is a larger arc; Line follows a straight attack path, potentially reaching more than one aligned enemy only if the technique and penetration permit. An attack can change coverage when using a weapon's explicitly supported alternate form. The accompanying **Short/Medium/Long Reach** categories are separate from Coverage and **Light/Medium/Heavy handling weight**. **Canonical names do not yet fix meters, angular widths, casualty limits or automatic hits.** The comparative close-combat illustration (Short dagger vs Long spear: open approach → inside weapon reach → grapple) is retained as a useful *illustrative design reference*, not as precise distance measurements.

**REACH ENTRY, APPROVED PHILOSOPHY:** Getting inside the controlled reach of a longer weapon requires **an opportunity and successful execution, not counting movement in feet/meters**. A Short fighter's willingness to be stabbed is not enough to automatically pass the Long fighter's weapon and establish inside fighting or a grapple. Sufficiently superior defender SPD allows the long wielder to maintain control of the approach, conditional on awareness, posture, space, usable weapon and actual tactical pressure. A defender making an accurate player-stated DEX prediction of the approaching fighter's action can improve the defense in accordance with the **already-approved DEX prediction standard (effective DEX >= 75% of opponent effective SPD, with error rules and >=200% contingency exception)**. Do not award a second automatic bonus merely because DEX is high without a correct prediction. If the long wielder becomes genuinely Pressured or the Short fighter gains an inside grapple, previously approved reach-control limits apply. **The defender having >=1.5x the attacker's effective SPD is a suggested, UNAPPROVED numerical benchmark** for resisting a straight, telegraphed entry, not a universal insurmountable reach lock; compare alternatives before ratification.

**APPROVED GENERAL POWER FRAMEWORK:** For applicable ordinary physical melee shapes, **Weapon Power = (A × F)^2** using *already-rebased new effective attributes* (no second legacy combat-only +10). The weapon's final calibrated rating modifier `F` includes its relevant weapon form/shape rules and any applicable lawful modifiers **without blindly multiplying overlapping light-class and dagger-specific bonuses**. Governing attribute `A`: STR-based `effective STR`; DEX-based `effective DEX`; either-stat `max(effective STR,effective DEX)`; and **both-stat** `0.65 × (effective STR + effective DEX)` **only if both weapon-specific minimum STR and DEX prerequisites are met**. The user approved this initial 0.65 both-stat choice as the starting weapon scaling rule; its weapon requirement values, individual F factors and material/quality interactions remain unresolved. This preserves incentives for balanced builds but does not create a universal free scaling boost for a one-stat specialist using a both-stat weapon below requirements.

**NOT YET APPROVED:** Medium 1.15 and Heavy 1.30 attack-rating coefficients, type-specific numeric form coefficients, armor bypass percentages (explicitly deferred to armor), weapon-specific stat minimums, quantitative coverage/attack count, exact 1.5x SPD reach-control threshold and final movement-exchange procedure.

## November 6, 2026 — Ranged reload, aim and mount distinctions (LATEST)

**Approved reload identity:** For ordinary bows, drawing/nocking produces no special opening beyond their normal shot; light crossbows create a modest exploitable opening, with heavier mechanical reloads more vulnerable. **Single-shot pistols** use a **full-turn meaningful action** spent carefully reloading. **High-capacity magazines** likewise require a **full-turn loading/replacement action**; ordinary shots from a still-loaded magazine do not each demand that loading turn. **Siege loading** creates severe vulnerability and may require one or more full turns depending on the specific engine; exact loading/crew cycle remains unapproved. Exposed reloaders are not guaranteed hit, and the opening does not create free actions for everyone nearby. Each weapon's shot capacity, exact reload length (beyond the named examples), grip and loading tools still require profile design.

**Approved aiming:** spending a meaningful action on careful **Aim** instead of a shot produces an opportunity to improve the *next* shot's hit reliability. In the ordinary one-shot-per-action case, aim then fire means **one shot in two attack opportunities** rather than two; there is no 50% reduction in actual projectile Power and no doubled damage. Targeting bonus, duration, stacking, target change, movement and interrupt conditions are still unresolved. The opponent's SPD, DEX-based timing and cover remain relevant.

**Approved mount variation:** Fixed side mounts constrain arc; articulated mounts increase aim flexibility; supported heavy mounts distribute load but do not nullify recoil; assisted-loading mounts can improve loading only through an actual functioning mechanism. Suitable configurations may combine subject to physical clearance, anatomy, construction quality, controlled ammunition feed and action economy. No independent extra attacks from extra barrels or harness guns.

## November 6, 2026 — Firearm handling: three requirements (LATEST)

## November 6, 2026 — Approved armor ensemble, coverage, ratings, rear vulnerability and later pricing (reconciled)

**ARMOR CLASSES AND COVERAGE, APPROVED:** The **protective class** is **Light, Medium or Heavy** and the **coverage extent** is **Partial, Standard or Full**; *Full* supersedes the earlier proposal *Comprehensive*. The extent of coverage does not itself lower or raise the AR of a protected surface. A **Full Light** outfit may incorporate a padded coat, flexible torso protection, light helm or hood, gloves, bracers, kneepads, shin guards, and protective boots; a **Full Medium** outfit may combine brigandine/mail and appropriate head, torso, limb and extremity protection; a **Full Heavy** outfit may use breast/back plate, enclosed helmet, articulated plates, heavy gauntlets and plated boots. These examples describe visual construction, not new equippable combat slots.

**APPROVED ARMOR RATING:** On covered main surfaces of ordinary reference construction, **Light AR 25 / Medium AR 45 / Heavy AR 75**, regardless of Partial/Standard/Full coverage. All numbers refer to the overall covered surface's armor rating, not to separate layers. A **Partial Heavy** vital cuirass remains AR 75 across its covered portions, not AR 25 because some limbs are bare. This explicit approval supersedes any previous "AR 25/45/75 not approved" references.

**BUILT-IN UNDERARMOR, APPROVED:** Medium and Heavy armor automatically include appropriate padding/mail/flexible defenses at their **protected** structural gaps and joints: those validly targeted joints use **Light-equivalent AR 25** instead of the outer shell's AR 45 or 75. They are not added together. Do not separately equip, price or calculate a Light suit underneath Medium/Heavy for these effects: the older mechanics permitting independently tracked light layering under plate are **superseded**. Underarmor need not cover an intentionally bare region, and a genuinely exposed face, eye or other skin surface when legitimately struck still has **AR 0**. Wearing a suitable helmet/face guard can prevent exposed openings where the fitted equipment actually covers them. Specific targeting probability/accuracy, and joint-versus-exposed-region procedures remain open.

**ONE ENSEMBLE BURDEN, APPROVED DIRECTION:** Treat the equipped armor as **one protective outfit** for physical STR/support and SPD/mobility calculations rather than a separate penalty for each glove, greave or boot. A wearer can strip the armor down to protect vitals while exposing peripheral regions to gain some mobility, subject to later numerical design. A heavily armored but partially exposed fighter remains Partial Heavy, not reclassified as Light. Material/quality may later change this burden, but their numeric modifiers are unresolved.

**REAR PROTECTION, APPROVED:** A conventional attack must actually exploit a rear angle made viable by **surprise or earned tactical position/Pressure**; an opponent merely standing behind an alert fighter does not automatically claim a rear attack. When a rear attack validly connects with protected armor, add **5 percentage points to applicable armor penetration**, capped at 100%. Example: a base 50% penetrating attack becomes 55%, not 50% × 1.05. Apply it before the squared armor contribution. This is not the previously suggested −15% AR, does not grant automatic Pressure, and has no additional armor benefit against an already exposed AR 0 area. A magically homing attack needs its own declared mechanics.

**DM AND ECONOMY WARNING, AUTHOR-APPROVED:** The AR contribution to Defense Power is squared. At low attribute levels, even Light armor can be a tremendous advantage, and early full Heavy armor may render ordinary opponents ineffective. **Normal availability and fair prices should pace acquisition**, particularly heavy plate, so Heroes typically take meaningful progression time to obtain superior armor until attack Power and tactical options can contest it. Do not introduce arbitrary level restrictions or claim numeric Gem prices as approved. Truly rare or plot-awarded powerful armor can be allowed as a deliberate GM campaign-balancing choice. Respect approved existing Gem/equipment wealth progression and fair-price shop guidance when prices are eventually designed.

**ACID ENCHANTMENT, APPROVED FUTURE DIRECTION:** Acid is an **offensive elemental enchantment effect** and qualifying acid attacks **reduce Armor Rating**, limited by a strict **minimum AR 0**. They do not automatically reduce natural END, and Acid does not automatically introduce a new mana color. Corrosion magnitude, persistence, stacking, restoration, armor-material responses, and exact enchantment skills/costs remain unresolved until enchantment design.

**OPEN DESIGN:** Armored-ensemble STR/SPD burdens for each Light/Medium/Heavy × Partial/Standard/Full combination; final range and geometry of covered-gap targeting; weapon-form armor penetration numbers (0/50/65% are only earlier test cases); shields and blocking, material/quality effects, finalized gem prices/distribution and acid-specific numbers. These approved armor baselines should precede that calibration.


## November 6, 2026 — Shields: independent defense, Dented durability, requirements and enchantments (APPROVED direction)

**THREE DURABILITY LOSSES:** Approved track **Intact 0/3 → Dented 1/3 → Cracked 2/3 → Broken 3/3** (the earlier proposed label 'Strained' is superseded by 'Dented'). Dented and Cracked are **visible damage only and never impose a functional penalty**; on the third accumulated loss the shield is **Broken and useless until repaired**. An extraordinary strike may inflict 2–3 durability losses simultaneously. Do not assign 1 durability damage to every weak impact regardless of shield Defense. Shield protection is its **own defensive quantity** from shape, material, quality and eventually installed magic, **not** the owner's END² nor an automatic addition to armor AR. An attack that collides with a shield is resolved against shield integrity; exact impact-to-loss formula, overpenetration and repair remain TBD.

**SHAPES AND STAT REQUIREMENTS:** Buckler has low STR/moderate DEX demand, quick control and minimal passive coverage; Standard shield moderate STR/DEX, balanced body obstruction; Tower shield high STR/moderate DEX, broad cover and more cumbersome maneuvering. Each shield has **individual STR and DEX requirements**, exact values TBD, which influence ability to raise and handle it. An intact, competently wielded shield makes legitimate aimed attacks **against parts of the wearer other than the shield** harder to hit: provisional design values **Buckler +1, Standard +1, Tower +2 hit-difficulty categories** relative to the established six-tier ladder, when direction/coverage/awareness warrant; a shield itself remains a target. No additional fully independent generic evasion bonus and no stacking identical cover/deflection/shield obstruction for the same geometric obstacle. Shields can deliberately intercept an incoming attack and suffer durability instead of a direct body hit, when a physically possible block is chosen.

**PRESSURE LIMITATION (LATEST USER RULING):** **Under Extreme Pressure (net 3+ meaningful PP) an operator cannot claim active shield blocking/parrying or its intentional body-obstruction/deflection difficulty bonus.** An actually interposed shield still exists and may passively receive an attack that physically hits it. This does **not** erase body armor, walls, other defenses or the independent Trapped condition. An aware Trapped defender at lower PP can still block if movement/angle permits; this newer shield-specific rule supersedes earlier general implications of unconditionally available shield blocking during Extreme Pressure.

**ENCHANTMENT PLANNING:** Shields shall have **their own enchantments**, likely borrowed from body armor enchantments or designed closely analogously. Preserve established equipment modular architecture and Soul Strain rules; no shield-specific slot count, price, spell, elemental effect or durability healing is approved yet. Full numerical rating, material scaling, stat minimums, load burden, durability/overflow, repair are reserved for explicit later approval.
