## Part One — The Tongues of the Living World

**Approved cultural canon** · Speech, swearing, insults, compliments, and the language of power

“You can learn where a stranger comes from by how they curse. You can learn what they fear by what they won't say. And you can learn who owns them by how carefully they choose their words.”

The races of Mystaria should sound distinctly different, even when speaking the same language.

A Tidekin makes an insult sound like a compliment. A Naga makes an order sound like a favor. A Vren makes a threat sound like a promise. A Moontouched can refuse someone so politely that it takes several moments for the other person to realize what happened.

And a Kobold can turn a three-word question into a twenty-minute engineering lecture.

Mystaria's linguistic identity follows three principles:

1. Speech reveals cultural values. Each race has things it considers worthy of praise, shameful, or sacred.
2. Language changes with power. A free citizen, an enslaved person, and a noble might use the same words with very different meanings.
3. Insults have histories. The cruelest expressions aren't necessarily vulgar. They attack a people's origins, relationships, or deepest cultural insecurities.

## I. The Common Languages of Mystaria

Mystaria uses a shared trade language with strong regional dialects; separate complete constructed languages for each civilization are not required by this ruling.

Roadspeech is the common tongue used by merchants, adventurers, travelers, and most cosmopolitan settlements. It descends from several post-Imperial languages and is widely understood.

Three other important linguistic traditions influence it.

| Tradition            | Spoken where                         | Defining characteristics                                                         |
| -------------------- | ------------------------------------ | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Imperial Coil Speech | Gilded Coil, Naga courts, government | Formal, hierarchical, indirect, rich in possessive titles and legal distinctions |
| Canopy Cant          | Canopy Kingdoms                      | Emphasizes duties, promises, kinship, rank, and reciprocal obligations           |
| Red Cant             | Vren Blood Courts                    | Precise wording, double meanings, intimacy, contractual expressions              |
| Roadspeech           | Across Mystaria                      | Flexible, direct, full of borrowed curses and expressions from other races       |

These are broad linguistic traditions, not rigid racial languages. Kobolds and Tidekin living in the Gilded Coil may speak Imperial Coil Speech perfectly, while a Naga adventurer might sound like an ordinary traveler.

For the dialogue below, their speech is rendered in readable English, with characteristic phrasing and occasional untranslated cultural expressions.

## II. The Voices of Mystaria

### 1. Naga — The Polite Weight of Authority

Formal · Possessive · Patient · Subtly threatening

Naga aristocratic speech revolves around authority presented as responsibility.

They favor complete sentences, deliberate pauses, and questions that are not really questions.

A Matriarch rarely needs to raise her voice. She finds it much more satisfying to make another person realize that she never expected to be refused.

Characteristic phrasing: “I would prefer…,” “You will be comfortable…,” “Be sensible, darling,” and “We have already considered that possibility.”

They often use we to refer to themselves and their Coil, even when the decision was entirely personal.

A particularly elegant Naga can make “You disappoint me” feel considerably worse than an ordinary threat.

Naga also use affectionate possessives, such as my little engineer, my clever one, or my dear companion. These may express genuine affection, but when directed toward enslaved people they also reinforce ownership.

Common expressions:

- “By the Empty Throne.” A serious oath invoking the abandoned Imperial seat of Sathryx. Common in politics, legal disputes, and moments of shock.
- “Mind your tail.” Behave with dignity; don't embarrass yourself or your family.
- “A monarch needn't hiss.” True authority should not require noisy threats.
- “You've shed your judgment.” You've acted foolishly. Mild among relatives, quite cutting in formal company.

Rare and serious: “Your Coil has forgotten its mother.” This accuses an entire family of abandoning its lineage and responsibilities. It is an insult capable of provoking a longstanding feud.

A Naga's greatest compliment is often an acknowledgement that someone deserves genuine authority:

“You have the judgment of a Matriarch.”

For an enslaved Kobold, this may be an extraordinary honor—or an infuriating compliment from someone unwilling to actually grant that authority.

### 2. Kobolds — The Language of Possibility

Enthusiastic · Technical · Self-correcting · Frequently overconfident

Kobolds tend to speak through hypotheses, practical observations, and rapid revisions.

They frequently interrupt themselves, not because they're unintelligent, but because they've identified an improvement to what they were about to say.

A Kobold engineer might begin with “It won't work,” stop, and immediately continue with “Actually, it will work if we reverse the polarity of the third lattice, replace the reservoir, and politely ask everyone to leave the building.”

Their speech also reflects their First Devotion.

Kobolds often unconsciously imitate their imprinted master's vocabulary, accent, or favorite expressions.

A Kobold raised by a graceful Naga Matriarch may speak with aristocratic elegance.

One raised by an impatient Duskpaw might employ clipped sentences and a remarkable variety of inventive profanity.

This imitation is an important part of Kobold identity, but it does not mean they lack personalities of their own.

Common expressions:

- “Sparks and screws!” Mild surprise, irritation, or delight.
- “Son of a glitch!” Strong frustration or disbelief, especially at an important failure.
- “It was supposed to do that.” An extremely common response to unexpected machine behavior.
- “Almost done.” Usually implies progress, but work may continue indefinitely.
- “Bright as a first spark.” Genuine praise for an ingenious idea, invoking Kobold imagery of light and discovery.

Kobolds don't ordinarily swear by the gods. They recognize divine beings but do not worship them.

Rare and devastating: “Are your scales just for show?.”

For an imprinted Kobold, this questions whether the admired master deserves the qualities reflected in the Kobold’s own scales. It is one of the cruelest accusations such a Kobold can make.

Kobolds particularly appreciate being praised for originality, rather than mere usefulness.

“That was your idea, wasn't it?” can mean more to an independent-minded Kobold than an expensive reward.

### 3. Moontouched — The Gentle Refusal

Warm · Considerate · Quietly stubborn · Disarmingly direct under stress

Moontouched favor language that acknowledges another person's wishes.

They ask permission frequently, offer choices where possible, and avoid declaring what someone else must feel.

“Would you like some help?” is more natural to them than “You need help.”

They have a distinctive habit of apologizing for inconveniences without apologizing for their decisions.

“I'm sorry this disappoints you. My answer is still no.”

Their religious traditions surrounding Surrender give particular importance to gift, trust, welcome, and freely.

Among close friends, their speech can be playful and mischievous rather than solemnly gentle.

Common expressions:

- “Oh, mooncrumbs.” A mild everyday curse.
- “Sweet Surrender!” Sudden shock or alarm.
- “A gift has an open hand.” A genuine gift cannot be forcibly reclaimed or used to establish ownership.
- “There's room at the hearth.” You are welcome here.
- “Some doors are best left closed.” Some boundaries should be respected.

Rare and cutting: “You don't know the difference between love and keeping.”

Moontouched reserve this for people who confuse affection with possession.

It is especially painful when spoken to a Naga master who sincerely believes she treats her slaves with kindness.

The highest compliment among Moontouched is often:

“I would choose you again.”

It means that, even without obligation, fear, dependence, or magical influence, the speaker would freely choose the same relationship.

### 4. Bloomkin — Small Words, Sharp Edges

Precise · Earnest · Observant · Proud of craftsmanship

Bloomkin often speak with the precision of expert artisans.

They notice details others overlook and can be surprisingly blunt about incompetence.

A Bloomkin may apologize for interrupting a noble conversation, then explain in exacting detail why the noble's expensive equipment is dangerously defective.

They tend to dislike compliments that emphasize their size instead of their skill.

Calling a talented Bloomkin “an adorable little helper” is likely to earn a chilly response.

Calling them “the finest white-mana shaper I've encountered” may earn lifelong respect.

Common expressions:

- “By the fine stitch.” A mild oath, especially among craftspeople.
- “Loose thread!” Alarm at an overlooked problem.
- “The smallest join bears the greatest strain.” Seemingly insignificant people or details may be essential.
- “Measure the promise.” Understand an obligation before agreeing to it.
- “That's a clean seam.” Something has been done exceptionally well.

Rare and cruel: “You were made to be held.”

This mocks the Bloomkin's historical dependence upon Thornguard and their frequent enslavement.

Bloomkin find the implication that their abilities exist only to serve someone else's power deeply insulting.

An especially meaningful compliment is “You made this whole,” acknowledging that the Bloomkin's contribution was essential rather than supplementary.

### 5. Thornguard — The Weight of an Oath

Ceremonial · Direct · Protective · Proud

Thornguard speech reflects their knightly traditions.

They frequently frame decisions in terms of duty, honorable conduct, and the obligations created by promises.

Even mundane disputes can sound suspiciously like formal legal proceedings.

A Thornguard might say, “I gave my word that your dinner would be ready,” with the solemnity of someone promising to defend a fortress.

Young knights sometimes take this habit to ridiculous extremes.

Common expressions:

- “By my banner!” A common oath.
- “Thorns take it!” A moderately strong curse.
- “Stand behind me.” Both a practical instruction and an expression of care.
- “The shield remembers.” A promise remains binding even when circumstances become difficult.
- “An oath cuts both ways.” Obligations apply to all parties, not merely the weaker one.

Rare and extremely insulting: “Your oath is only a collar.”

This accuses a Thornguard knight of disguising domination as protection.

Because the Oath of Protection has historically institutionalized Bloomkin servitude, the expression is particularly controversial.

The highest praise is not necessarily “You're brave.”

It's “I'd trust you at my back.”

For a Thornguard, this expresses complete confidence in someone's courage and loyalty.

### 6. Tidekin — The Art of Being Unbearable

Playful · Quick-witted · Sarcastic · Impossible to silence gracefully

Tidekin speaking patterns revolve around jokes, mockery, improvisation, and the deliberate refusal to take pompous people seriously.

They particularly enjoy addressing dangerous people with excessive familiarity.

“Darling,” “sweetheart,” and “your magnificence” can all be affectionate—or devastatingly sarcastic.

Their insults often arrive disguised as helpful observations.

“That's a lovely sword. Do you actually know what to do with it?”

A Tidekin who stops joking may be considerably more dangerous than one who continues.

Common expressions:

- “Oh, puddles.” A mild annoyance.
- “Drown the moon!” A strong curse of shock or frustration.
- “You're swimming uphill.” You're making something needlessly difficult.
- “Lovely splash. Shame about the landing.” Someone's grand attempt ended badly.
- “Let the current argue.” Stop wasting breath and let events prove your point.

Rare and serious: “May you never find water.”

Because water is central to Tidekin life and Wakecraft, this is not a casual insult. Traditionally, it is a bitter curse reserved for grave betrayal.

A Tidekin's most sincere compliment often sounds understated:

“You're worth sharing a boat with.”

It means the person is dependable, enjoyable company, and someone the Tidekin would trust in a dangerous situation.

### 7. Duskpaws — The Language Between Locks

Quick · Practical · Wry · Fond of double meanings

Duskpaws favor short expressions, indirect warnings, and jokes that make sense only after one recognizes the hidden implication.

Their speech is heavily influenced by their Caches and the traditions arising from the Night of a Thousand Locks.

A Duskpaw might say “That door's expensive” when they mean the guards on the other side will be difficult to avoid.

They also use locked, open, hidden, and found metaphorically.

Common expressions:

- “Rust and ruin.” A common curse.
- “Fray it all!” A stronger curse.
- “Every lock has a story.” Everything has a vulnerability or hidden history.
- “Keep a second key.” Always have another plan.
- “The Cache remembers.” Family and community obligations endure.
- “Nothing stays shut forever.” A promise of eventual freedom, sometimes used as a threat.

Rare and deeply insulting: “You'd sell the last key.”

This accuses a Duskpaw of betraying someone else's chance at freedom for personal gain.

Duskpaws admire resourcefulness, but distinguish cleverness from disloyalty.

A particularly sincere compliment is:

“I'd show you the hidden door.”

It means someone is trusted with the community's most carefully guarded secrets.

### 8. Vren — Every Word Has Teeth

Intimate · Precise · Elegant · Dangerous when suddenly plainspoken

Vren aristocratic speech is filled with private implications, affectionate threats, carefully chosen promises, and deliberately ambiguous language.

They often speak as though negotiating a contract, even when discussing something mundane.

“My dear, you are welcome to decline” may mean precisely that.

Or it may mean the Vren has already ensured that every alternative is unpleasant.

Vren are especially attentive to promises.

A casual “I swear I'll return” can provoke a raised eyebrow and the question, “Do you?”

Common expressions:

- “Blood and ashes.” A common curse.
- “Damn the dawn!” A strong curse, particularly during a crisis.
- “Mind what you promise.” Both advice and warning.
- “That bargain has teeth.” An agreement contains dangerous consequences.
- “Blood remembers.” Obligations and inherited grievances are not easily forgotten.
- “Until the last drop.” A declaration of exceptional loyalty or determination.

Rare and cutting: “Your blood is worth less than your word.”

This suggests that a Vren's most fundamental supernatural identity possesses no honor.

Another especially cruel insult is “You have no one who would bond with you.”

It attacks social worth rather than physical power.

Their most intimate compliment is often “I would share my pulse with you.”

To someone familiar with Bloodbond, this communicates an exceptional willingness to establish mutual vulnerability and trust.

### 9. Corvyn — The Witness Who Says Little

Laconic · Observant · Dry · Disturbingly well-informed

Corvyn tend to favor concise statements.

They often allow silence to do much of the conversational work.

Their humor can be so dry that the listener doesn't immediately realize they've been insulted.

A Corvyn might observe an argument for ten minutes and finally remark, “You've both forgotten the door is open.”

Then leave through it.

Their cultural emphasis on witnessing, living ink, and transmitted knowledge makes careless assertions particularly embarrassing.

Common expressions:

- “Black feathers.” Mild exasperation.
- “Ink rot!” A strong curse.
- “The ink remembers.” Records and consequences endure.
- “Witness before judgment.” Understand something before condemning it.
- “That's not a secret. It's an embarrassment.” A particularly Corvyn dismissal.
- “Let it dry.” Wait before acting or drawing conclusions.

Rare and serious: “May your ink forget you.”

This is a curse upon someone's identity, history, and magical inheritance.

A Corvyn rarely uses it lightly.

One of their greatest compliments is “I'd put that in my ink.”

It means a statement, discovery, or individual is worthy of permanent remembrance.

### 10. Driders — The Measured Verdict

Precise · Judicial · Restrained · Occasionally mischievous

Driders favor language rooted in evidence, proportionality, responsibility, and restraint.

They often speak as though weighing a case.

“On what grounds?” is a common response to an unsupported claim.

However, they are daughters of Arrest, whose personality also includes cleverness and playfulness.

An individual Drider can be surprisingly teasing, particularly when someone makes an obviously foolish argument.

Common expressions:

- “By the First Web.” A solemn oath.
- “Tangled threads.” Mild frustration or confusion.
- “Silk and sentence!” A stronger curse when patience runs out.
- “The knot tells.” Examine how something is connected to discover its meaning.
- “Judgment is not appetite.” Punishment should not be driven by personal desire.
- “Loose silk catches nothing.” A rule or obligation without proper enforcement is ineffective.

Rare and profoundly insulting: “You enjoy the sentence more than the verdict.”

This accuses a Drider of abusing judicial power for personal satisfaction.

It strikes directly at her relationship to Arrest's ideals.

The highest praise is “Your judgment is sound, even against yourself.”

It recognizes someone capable of applying the same principles to their own actions that they demand of others.

### 11. Untouched Serpentari — The Burden of an Ancient Name

Variable · Historically sensitive · Often deliberately modern

Untouched Serpentari are a distinct humanoid heritage, not the colossal true Imperial Serpentari.

Their speech should reflect that distinction.

Some cultivate older Imperial expressions, adopt formal etiquette, and take great pride in their ancestry.

Others deliberately reject antiquated vocabulary because they resent being treated as living Imperial relics.

They may be particularly sensitive to people confusing them with the hidden surviving true Serpentari.

Common expressions among traditionalists:

- “By the First Coil.” A historical oath.
- “Old scales, new roads.” Ancestry does not determine one's future.
- “The past has a long tail.” Ancient actions continue to have consequences.
- “Don't mistake the echo for the voice.” Descendants are not their ancestors.

Rare and offensive: Calling an Untouched Serpentari a “false serpent” or demanding to see their supposed giant serpent form.

A powerful compliment is:

“You are more than what survived.”

It recognizes the individual's achievements rather than merely praising their ancestry.

### 12. Firstshaped — The Body Is Not the Mind

Firstshaped are not a separate civilization with one universal dialect.

Their speech generally reflects their parent race and upbringing.

A Firstshaped Naga might sound every bit as aristocratic as another Naga. A Firstshaped Tidekin might be equally sarcastic.

The difference is how others address them.

Firstshaped are frequently subjected to animalizing language despite being fully sapient people.

The particularly offensive practice of using commands associated with domesticated animals—“Stay,” “Heel,” or “Good pet”—is insulting when imposed by outsiders.

Some Firstshaped reclaim these words jokingly among close friends.

That does not make them acceptable from strangers.

A meaningful compliment is one that recognizes their capabilities without treating their body as either a defect or spectacle.

And a Firstshaped Naga has no hands, regardless of how often an outsider carelessly tells her to “lend a hand.”

### Slurs Created by Slavery

The Gilded Coil also has a vocabulary shaped by its enormous enslaved population.

| Expression     | Meaning                                                                                                                                                         |
| -------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Collar-born    | Someone born into slavery. Official records sometimes use the term neutrally, but spoken contemptuously it implies servitude is a natural identity.             |
| Loose collar   | A slave considered insufficiently obedient. Common in disciplinary reports and slaveholder gossip.                                                              |
| House-polished | An enslaved person who has learned flawless aristocratic etiquette. Can be praise or a mocking accusation of excessive deference.                               |
| Chain-licker   | An enslaved person accused of betraying others to please an owner. Very serious among some enslaved communities; often unfairly applied.                        |
| Silk collar    | An enslaved person with unusual privileges, luxury, or social influence. Often used resentfully by less privileged laborers.                                    |
| Unregistered   | Official Imperial term for someone absent from the Scale Register. In certain contexts it is used as an accusation of criminality, even against free travelers. |

An important cultural distinction: Saltbound is the established name for enslaved Tidekin, but is not automatically a slur. It becomes insulting when a speaker uses it to deny the individual's name, accomplishments, or identity.

For example, an Imperial official might accurately record someone's legal status as Saltbound.

A hostile spectator who shouts, “Get back in the canal, Saltbound!” is clearly using the term to demean them.

Similarly, collared can be a straightforward description or a deliberate insult depending on context.

### The Forbidden Word of the First Coil

One particularly serious historical insult is:

“Hollow.”

Because of the Serpentari Imperium's ancient Hollow Coil infertility, this word can carry a very different meaning when applied to Imperial bloodlines.

In ordinary conversation, hollow still means empty or vacant.

But “Hollow blood,” or “May your Coil go hollow,” can invoke the catastrophe that preceded the Imperium's decline.

These expressions are rare and vicious, especially among people who preserve Imperial history.

They are not equivalent to casually calling someone foolish.

A Naga Matriarch might overlook a foreigner's mockery of her appearance but never forgive a rival who wished the Hollow Coil upon her descendants.

### Everyday, Stressful, and Taboo Usage

Everyday profanity

Refers to ordinary irritation, mishaps, and inconvenience. “Mooncrumbs,” “Loose thread,” and “Oh, puddles” can be spoken in public without much reaction. They are also easy for travelers to borrow from other races.

Stress profanity

Emerges during combat, panic, severe pain, or genuine emergencies. A normally soft-spoken Moontouched may suddenly shout “Sweet Surrender!” A meticulous Kobold may abandon technical terminology in favor of “SON OF A GLITCH!”; the warning “Oops!” signals imminent catastrophe.

Taboo curses

These are often statements of rejection or condemnation rather than ordinary swear words. They can permanently damage relationships, provoke duels, or lead to legal consequences. Their force comes from being rarely spoken.

A particularly useful writing principle is that characters often abandon their customary polished speech under genuine stress.

A Vren who normally speaks in elaborate contractual language might simply shout, “MOVE!”

A Naga Matriarch who usually gives indirect instructions might snap, “Get my children out. Now.”

A Tidekin who jokes constantly might say nothing at all.

Those changes can communicate danger more effectively than adding another layer of profanity.

## V. Turns of Phrase That Outsiders Get Wrong

Some Mystarian expressions are misunderstood because their literal meanings differ from their cultural implications.

| Expression                                      | Intended meaning                                                      | Common misuse                                                                                                        |
| ----------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| “The Coil embraces.” — Naga                     | Our family or institution offers protection, inclusion, or authority. | Foreigners mistake it for an unconditional promise of affection. It may also imply entering the Coil's jurisdiction. |
| “Almost done.” — Kobold                 | The project is progressing, though completion may be distant or never truly reached; tone determines how seriously to take it.   | Naga employers interpret it as a literal promise of immediate completion.                                            |
| “I would choose you again.” — Moontouched       | A profound expression of freely given trust.                          | Slaveholders repeat it as though affection proves consent to ownership.                                              |
| “Measure the promise.” — Bloomkin               | Understand the full terms and consequences before committing.         | Thornguard traditionalists sometimes use it to justify imposing more elaborate oaths.                                |
| “Stand behind me.” — Thornguard                 | Let me protect you; I will take the danger first.                     | Some knights use it to silence capable partners who should be allowed to stand beside them.                          |
| “Let the current argue.” — Tidekin              | Allow evidence or events to settle the disagreement.                  | Outsiders assume it means avoid responsibility or do nothing.                                                        |
| “Keep a second key.” — Duskpaws                 | Prepare contingencies; preserve a path to freedom.                    | Imperial authorities treat it as evidence of criminal intent.                                                        |
| “Blood remembers.” — Vren                       | Relationships, promises, and their consequences endure.               | Foreigners believe it means every Vren can magically access ancestral memories.                                      |
| “I'd put that in my ink.” — Corvyn              | This is worth preserving permanently.                                 | Naga collectors sometimes mistake it for an offer to provide magical services.                                       |
| “The knot tells.” — Drider                      | Connections and evidence reveal underlying truth.                     | Outsiders assume the Drider is suggesting that restraint itself proves guilt.                                        |
| “Old scales, new roads.” — Untouched Serpentari | History matters, but descendants must choose their own path.          | Imperial revivalists misuse it to suggest a return to Serpentari domination.                                         |

### Why Misuse Matters

Misusing a phrase can be funny, embarrassing, insulting, or politically dangerous.

A Naga Matriarch who announces, “My servants would choose me again,” may intend to praise their loyalty.

To a Moontouched listener, the statement misses the entire point.

If the servants cannot freely choose to leave, their decision to remain cannot establish what they would choose under freedom.

Likewise, a Thornguard who tells a Bloomkin “Stand behind me” might genuinely intend protection. The Bloomkin may hear an assertion that they are unqualified to make their own decisions.

Neither interpretation is necessarily fabricated.

That's what makes these cultural misunderstandings useful for storytelling.

## VI. What Each Race Most Wants to Hear — and Least Wants to Hear

A useful distinction for dialogue is that a good insult attacks a person's pride, while a good compliment recognizes something their culture considers meaningful.

Insults that work on one race may be meaningless to another.

| Race                 | Particularly effective compliment                                             | Particularly effective insult                                          |
| -------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Naga                 | “Your people flourish because of your judgment, not merely your inheritance.” | “Without your Kobolds, you would not know how to open your own doors.” |
| Kobolds              | “No one taught you that. You invented it.”                                    | “You only know how to imitate someone greater.”                        |
| Moontouched          | “I trust you because I know you would never demand it.”                       | “Your kindness is just fear of being disliked.”                        |
| Bloomkin             | “The entire undertaking depended on your skill.”                              | “You're a clever accessory, nothing more.”                             |
| Thornguard           | “You kept your promise when breaking it would have been easier.”              | “You don't protect people. You collect dependents.”                    |
| Tidekin              | “I couldn't have pulled that off without you.”                                | “You're only useful when someone else gives you a river.”              |
| Duskpaws             | “You found a way through when everyone else gave up.”                         | “You'd trade anyone's freedom for a full purse.”                       |
| Vren                 | “Your word is worth more than any contract.”                                  | “No one trusts you unless you've bound them.”                          |
| Corvyn               | “You saw what all of us missed.”                                              | “Your ink carries other people's wisdom, never your own.”              |
| Driders              | “You were fair when fairness cost you something.”                             | “You call it justice because you enjoy holding the silk.”              |
| Untouched Serpentari | “Your achievements belong to you, not your ancestors.”                        | “You're wearing a history you could never live up to.”                 |
| Firstshaped          | “I trust your judgment and skill.”                                            | “You're only clever for an animal.”                                    |

These work best when the speaker has enough personal knowledge to make the remark credible.

A stranger insulting a Kobold's intelligence might be dismissed as ignorant.

Their beloved master saying “You only know how to imitate me” could be devastating.

Likewise, a Vren can laugh at being called a leech. Being told by someone they genuinely love that their promises cannot be trusted may wound them far more deeply.

### Compliments That Change With Social Position

Mystaria's slaveholding cultures make praise unusually complicated.

A master might say “You are irreplaceable” and sincerely mean it as a compliment.

An enslaved person might hear “I will never allow you to leave.”

Similarly, “You've earned my trust” can be meaningful from an equal, but frustrating from an owner who still refuses to grant autonomy.

Some of the strongest compliments in Mystaria therefore involve freely relinquishing power.

A Naga Matriarch who tells a Kobold, “You may choose your own work,” communicates something more consequential than praise.

A Thornguard who tells a Bloomkin, “I trust you to protect yourself,” acknowledges independence.

And a Vren who offers to dissolve a Bloodbond without demanding anything in return makes an extraordinarily significant gesture.

# VII. Speech in Practice — Scenes Across Mystaria

The following are illustrative situations, showing how these speech patterns might sound in everyday life.

## Scene 1 — A Kobold and Her Matriarch

Kheth-Vael · The City of Ten Thousand Mirrors

A Kobold engineer has finished repairing an essential Grand Orb. Her Naga owner arrives to inspect the work.

Naga Matriarch: “My clever little spark. I knew you would make everything right.”

Kobold: “Yes, Mistress. Well, mostly right. The third lattice is stable, the fourth is behaving, and the fifth—oh, the fifth is beautiful. Absolutely beautiful. Also, theoretically explosive.”

Matriarch: “Theoretically?”

Kobold: “Increasingly less theoretically.”

The Matriarch examines the trembling Orb.

Matriarch: “Do I need to evacuate the palace?”

Kobold: “That would be a wonderfully supportive decision.”

Matriarch: “And afterward?”

Kobold: “Afterward, Mistress, I'd like my name on the repair record.”

The Matriarch pauses.

Matriarch: “Of course, my dear. Such a clever little creature deserves recognition.”

The Kobold looks at her.

Kobold: “Not your little creature, Mistress. My name.”

The Matriarch's expression changes almost imperceptibly.

Matriarch: “...We shall discuss it after the evacuation.”

The conflict is subtle. The Matriarch believes her affection constitutes respect; the Kobold wants recognition as an individual rather than an extension of her owner.

## Scene 2 — A Saltbound Champion

Serrath Quay · Thousand Locks Regatta

A Tidekin racer has just won a prestigious championship. Her Naga owner is furious because she departed from the approved racing strategy.

Matriarch: “You were instructed to follow the eastern channel.”

Tidekin: “And you were instructed by the river not to put a boat there. Funny how neither of us listened.”

Matriarch: “You are fortunate that your victory has pleased me.”

Tidekin: “Darling, I'm fortunate the boat didn't sink. Your pleasure's a delightful extra.”

The Matriarch gestures toward the Tidekin's collar.

Matriarch: “Mind yourself.”

The Tidekin looks at the championship trophy.

Tidekin: “Wouldn't dream of forgetting myself, Mistress. Everyone else keeps trying to do that for me.”

The surrounding spectators go uncomfortably quiet.

Matriarch: “You are testing my patience.”

Tidekin: “Then I'm pleased to report that we're both competing today.”

Tidekin sass is often a form of resistance, but the consequences of such speech vary dramatically with the power of the listener and the protections offered by fame.

## Scene 3 — A Promise in the Canopy

Canopy Kingdoms · A Thornguard and his Bloomkin Shieldmate

A Thornguard knight and his Bloomkin partner prepare to face a dangerous enemy.

Thornguard: “Stand behind me.”

Bloomkin: “No.”

Thornguard: “I have sworn to protect you.”

Bloomkin: “And I have spent twenty years learning to shape your white into something useful. Do stop confusing those facts.”

Thornguard: “My oath—”

Bloomkin: “Measure the promise.”

The knight falls silent.

Bloomkin: “You promised to keep me safe. You did not promise to make every decision for me.”

The knight looks toward the approaching enemy.

Thornguard: “Then stand beside me.”

The Bloomkin smiles.

Bloomkin: “That's a clean seam.”

A familiar protective expression becomes an argument over whether an oath establishes duty or authority.

## Scene 4 — A Moontouched Warlock's Refusal

An isolated roadside inn · A traveling adventurer

A wealthy merchant offers a Moontouched Warlock payment to drain mana from an unwilling captive.

Merchant: “The prisoner owes me. This is simply the quickest way to recover my property.”

Moontouched: “Is it?”

Merchant: “Naturally.”

Moontouched: “Then you won't mind if I ask them whether they agree.”

Merchant: “Their agreement is irrelevant.”

The Warlock closes the external mana vessel she had been examining.

Moontouched: “Oh. Then so is my participation.”

Merchant: “You would refuse twenty Gems?”

Moontouched: “I'm afraid so.”

Merchant: “Your goddess teaches surrender.”

Moontouched: “Yes. Perhaps you should try it.”

The merchant stares.

Moontouched: “Of the demand, I mean. Not the prisoner.”

Moontouched defiance is frequently polite, but it can be uncompromising. Their friendliness is not necessarily compliance.

## Scene 5 — A Vren's Bargain

A Blood Court · Contract negotiations

A Vren noble offers an adventurer employment.

Vren: “My dear, the task is simple. Retrieve the ledger, bring it to me, and I shall see you rewarded.”

Adventurer: “How much?”

Vren: “Enough to make your journey worthwhile.”

Adventurer: “A number, please.”

The Vren smiles.

Vren: “How refreshingly vulgar.”

Adventurer: “I've met your kind before.”

Vren: “Then you know how seriously we take our promises.”

Adventurer: “Exactly. Which is why I'm asking what you're promising.”

The smile broadens.

Vren: “Oh, I do like you.”

Adventurer: “That's another thing I'd prefer not to put in writing.”

Vren speech is often designed to invite assumptions without committing to them. Experienced adventurers learn to demand specifics.

## Scene 6 — The Mirrorwarden and the Duskpaw

Sathryx · An Imperial checkpoint

A Mirrorwarden stops a Duskpaw suspected of tampering with the Scale Register.

Mirrorwarden: “Your travel record is incomplete.”

Duskpaw: “Terrible thing, incomplete records. You should tell whoever maintains them.”

Mirrorwarden: “Where were you last night?”

Duskpaw: “Somewhere with excellent company and poor ventilation.”

Mirrorwarden: “Was that somewhere the eastern registry station?”

Duskpaw: “Can't say. Terrible memory.”

Mirrorwarden: “Our Orbs remember.”

The Duskpaw glances at the smoking Grand Orb behind the desk.

Duskpaw: “Well, darling, that one's having a difficult morning.”

Mirrorwarden: “Did you sabotage it?”

Duskpaw: “Every lock has a story. This one looks like it had a very short one.”

Duskpaw humor often refuses to confirm or deny anything. Their statements can be technically true while carefully avoiding the question.

## Scene 7 — A Drider's Evidence

Gilded Coil · A dispute before an Imperial tribunal

A Naga Matriarch demands that a Drider investigator return an enslaved Corvyn suspected of stealing an enchanted artifact.

Matriarch: “She belongs to my household. I am entitled to have her returned.”

Drider: “On what evidence do you accuse her of theft?”

Matriarch: “The artifact is missing.”

Drider: “Yes.”

Matriarch: “She was near the archive.”

Drider: “As were seven others.”

Matriarch: “She has a history of disobedience.”

The Drider raises one hand.

Drider: “That is not evidence of theft.”

The Corvyn, who has been silent throughout, tilts her head.

Corvyn: “The knot tells.”

The Drider turns.

Drider: “You have something to add?”

Corvyn: “The Matriarch's nephew entered the archive at midnight. He left with a blue case.”

The Matriarch stiffens.

Drider: “How do you know this?”

Corvyn: “I was watching.”

Matriarch: “You were spying?”

Corvyn: “Witnessing. There's a distinction.”

Driders insist on evidence; Corvyn insist upon the value of what has been witnessed. A Naga may regard both as intolerably inconvenient.

## Scene 8 — A Firstshaped Matriarch

Velmora · A diplomatic audience

A foreign merchant stands before a Firstshaped Naga Matriarch, whose enormous serpentine body coils around the throne dais.

Merchant: “My lady, perhaps you could lend a hand with the contract.”

The chamber goes silent.

The Matriarch has no hands.

Matriarch: “How imaginative.”

Merchant: “I meant—”

Matriarch: “I know what you meant.”

A quill rises psychokinetically from the table. It moves toward the contract.

Matriarch: “You wish me to sign. A reasonable request.”

The quill writes with flawless precision.

Matriarch: “Next time, ask for my signature.”

Merchant: “Of course, my lady.”

Matriarch: “Excellent. We are learning.”

Firstshaped individuals often endure careless assumptions. This Matriarch responds with the same composed superiority expected of a Naga aristocrat.

## Scene 9 — An Adventurer and a Nightfox

An abandoned Imperial garden · Starwarden Pursuit

A Tidekin Wakewright crouches beside a Nightfox guarding the entrance to a ruined pavilion.

Tidekin: “Hello, gorgeous. Now, I've been informed you have the token we're looking for.”

The Nightfox watches silently.

The Tidekin sends a delicate ring of water rolling across the ground.

Tidekin: “A little game? You take that, I take the token, everyone has a wonderful afternoon.”

The Nightfox bats the water ring aside with an entropy orb.

Tidekin: “Well. That's a no, then.”

The Starspawn lowers its head and curls its tail around the token.

Tidekin: “Counteroffer?”

It stares.

Tidekin: “You're a ruthless negotiator.”

Nightfoxes cannot speak mortal languages. Their understanding and personality emerge through behavior, gesture, and magical responses rather than dialogue.

## Scene 10 — The Hero Who Spoke Too Freely

A frontier tavern · Shimmer terminology meets ordinary speech

A newly arrived Hero examines her Shimmer quest journal while discussing a dangerous expedition with an experienced Duskpaw guide.

Hero: “I think I should grind a few more levels before we go.”

Duskpaw: “Grind what?”

Hero: “Levels.”

Duskpaw: “Of the mountain?”

Hero: “No. My levels.”

The Duskpaw considers this.

Duskpaw: “Have you considered taller boots?”

Hero: “That's not what I—”

Duskpaw: “You've got twenty Gems and a map, sweetheart. Spend the Gems on rope and stop arguing with yourself.”

Heroes retain Earth expressions and Shimmer terminology. Ordinary Mystarians may understand levels or ranks in a general sense, but they cannot see another person's Shimmer interface and often misunderstand Earth gaming slang.

## VIII. Regional Speech and Social Etiquette

Speech should also change with location, not just race.

| Region          | Characteristic habits                                                          | Social consequences                                                                                    |
| --------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------ | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| Gilded Coil     | Elaborate titles, indirect requests, legal terminology, possessive endearments | Incorrect honorifics can be insulting; speaking too directly may be interpreted as deliberate defiance |
| Canopy Kingdoms | Oath formulas, ceremonial greetings, references to duty and lineage            | Casual promises can be taken more seriously than a traveler intended                                   |
| Scarlands       | Practical warnings, shortened names, direct bargains, borrowed curses          | Elaborate aristocratic language may be interpreted as dishonesty or pretension                         |
| Blood Courts    | Carefully defined promises, affectionate address, ritualized threats           | Ambiguous wording may carry contractual significance                                                   |
| Corvyn Flights  | Concise observations, references to memory and witnesses, meaningful silences  | Talking too much without evidence is considered undignified                                            |
| Tidekin Rafts   | Teasing, familiar nicknames, competitive wordplay, playful exaggeration        | Excessive formality can suggest distrust, arrogance, or an attempt to hide something                   |

### The Language of Slavery and Resistance

Within the Gilded Coil, speech can also reveal who possesses legal power.

An enslaved person might refer to a Naga owner as “Mistress,” address a free official by title, and speak entirely differently among trusted companions.

Some enslaved communities use deliberately ambiguous phrases that allow criticism to be disguised as ordinary conversation.

“Another beautiful day in the gardens” might genuinely describe pleasant weather.

Spoken after an exhausting day repairing the Worldspindle, it may be bitter sarcasm.

“Every lock has a story” might refer to the history of an ancient gate—or to a possible escape.

An enslaved Kobold saying “Almost done” could be describing a repair, proposing an improvement, or quietly preparing to sabotage a mechanism.

These expressions should not function as universally understood secret passwords. Their meaning depends on context, relationships, and shared experiences.

A Naga might recognize one form of resistance while entirely missing another.

Just as importantly, not every enslaved person speaks defiantly. Some are frightened, some are loyal to their owners, some are quietly rebellious, and others have complicated feelings about the society they inhabit.

Their voices should remain individual.

## IX. A Few Final Cultural Distinctions

There are several speech habits I would make particularly characteristic of Mystaria.

First, curses invoking gods should reflect the speaker's relationship with them. A Moontouched may swear by Surrender, a Drider by Arrest, and a Vren by Baphomet, but a Kobold rarely invokes a goddess sincerely. Kobolds are far more likely to swear at a malfunctioning machine.

Second, insults about slavery should have very different meanings depending on who speaks them. A free aristocrat calling someone “collar-born” carries a different implication than an enslaved person using the expression among friends.

Third, phrases invoking the Serpentari Imperium should often sound archaic. Traditionalist Naga may use them ceremonially. Young Duskpaws and Tidekin may parody them. Untouched Serpentari may resent being expected to use them at all.

Fourth, the most intense curses should be reserved for exceptional situations. If every Corvyn says “May your ink forget you” whenever she drops something, the expression loses its power.

Finally, a character's most sincere words should often sound unlike their public persona. A Tidekin might become completely serious when expressing love. A Vren might abandon all contractual elegance to make a genuine promise. A Naga might ask rather than command. A Kobold might stop imitating their master and speak entirely in their own voice.

Those moments can reveal more about a character than any amount of elaborate dialogue.

## X. Suggested Writing Convention for Mystaria

For consistent dialogue across future stories, Treat each race's speaking patterns as cultural tendencies, not compulsory accents.

A Naga raised among Tidekin could develop a sharp, teasing manner. An enslaved Corvyn brought up in a Naga household might speak impeccable Imperial Coil Speech. A Kobold imprinted on a Vren could speak with unnerving contractual precision.

Individual personalities, age, education, occupation, freedom, and upbringing should matter as much as ancestry.

The goal isn't for every member of a race to sound identical. It's for readers to recognize the culture beneath the individual's choices.

The defining idea: Mystaria's language should make relationships audible. A person can reveal where they come from through an oath, where they stand through a title, and what they truly believe through the one phrase they refuse to say.

### A. Kobolds — The Language of Light

Kobold speech should now be rich in metaphors about illumination, reflections, brilliance, and darkness.

### The Special Meaning of “Oops”

Among Kobolds, Oops has developed into an unofficial emergency alarm.

It is a single-word warning that something is about to go catastrophically wrong.

A Kobold might shout it after noticing an overloaded mana reservoir, an unstable Worldspindle component, or a dangerously incorrect calculation.

They do not have to be responsible for the problem.

In fact, an experienced Kobold shouting “Oops!” usually means they have noticed something everyone else missed.

The Three Levels of Kobold Alarm

Low

“Broken mirrors.” — Everything is going wrong for me.

Moderate

“Son of a glitch!” — We have a serious problem.

Critical

“Oops.” — Something catastrophic is imminent.

In established Kobold Workshops, hearing Oops is sufficient reason to stop work and seek immediate safety.

Foreigners sometimes misunderstand this.

An Earth-born Hero might casually say “Oops” after dropping a spoon, only to discover that every Kobold in the room has taken cover.

Because of this, deliberately shouting “Oops” as a prank is considered extremely irresponsible in Kobold society.

### Why “Are Your Scales Just for Show?” Hurts

This phrase has a special significance when spoken by an imprinted Kobold to their master.

Kobolds develop physical characteristics reminiscent of the individual upon whom they imprint. Their scales can literally reflect their master's appearance.

The insult therefore asks whether the person whose traits they proudly bear actually deserves their admiration.

It can be roughly understood as:

“I shaped myself in your image because I believed you were worthy. Was there ever anything worth admiring?”

A Kobold might endure harsh treatment without ever speaking these words.

If they finally do, the relationship may already be irreparably damaged.

### B. The New Slavery Slur — Muzzle-Mouth

Imperial Coil Speech · Derogatory · Moderately to severely offensive

Muzzle-mouth is a degrading expression used for enslaved individuals who are frequently muzzled or gagged.

It is especially common among cruel overseers, aristocratic servants, and slaveholders who consider verbal resistance a form of disobedience.

The term suggests that the individual is so troublesome, disrespectful, or incapable of appropriate speech that silencing them is their natural condition.

Unlike Saltbound or collar-born, it is almost always intended contemptuously.

A Naga overseer might say:

> “Keep that muzzle-mouth away from the visiting Matriarch. I will not have another ceremony interrupted.”

It can also be used to humiliate people who no longer wear such restraints.

An emancipated former slave might still be called a muzzle-mouth by someone seeking to remind them of their previous treatment.

### Who Uses It?

It is particularly common in the Gilded Coil but has spread into other slaveholding regions.

Tidekin are frequent targets because of their habit of insulting their owners and supervisors. Duskpaws, rebellious Moontouched, and politically outspoken Kobolds may also be targeted.

Among enslaved communities, the insult is sometimes reclaimed with bitter humor.

A Tidekin might declare:

“They called me a muzzle-mouth. Apparently I'm much more interesting when I'm allowed to speak.”

The reclaimed usage does not make the term acceptable from outsiders.

Importantly, being a muzzle-mouth is not a legal classification. It describes a person's treatment and reputation, not a distinct type of slave.

### A Few Particularly Effective Cross-Cultural Insults

A Tidekin telling a Naga “You're seeing too many forks, darling” mocks the Naga's tendency to overplan.

These expressions can cross racial boundaries, but they are usually most effective when spoken by someone who understands the tradition being criticized.

# VI. Revised Dialogue Examples

## Scene 1 — The Most Dangerous Word in the Workshop

Kheth-Vael · A Kobold engineering workshop

Kobold Engineer: “Almost done, Mistress!”

Naga Matriarch: “You said that yesterday.”

Kobold: “And I was correct yesterday. I'm almost done today too.”

Matriarch: “How long until it's finished?”

Kobold: “That depends on whether you want it to work.”

## Part Three — The Language of Magic: Speaking in Colors

**Canon status: APPROVED — definitive conversational terminology.** This entry was explicitly approved in the recovered exchange, after the revised linguistic proposal.

Because magical energy is familiar throughout Mystaria, speakers generally refer to it by **color** in casual conversation: **red, blue, green, white, black**. The word **mana** is usually reserved for factual, academic, technical or precise circumstances, especially where it could be confused with an ordinary visible color. Non-spellcasters use color-language too.

| Formal / technical | Everyday Mystarian |
|---|---|
| “You are exceptionally talented at shaping white mana.” | “You shape the white like nothing I've ever seen.” |
| “My black mana reserves are exhausted.” | “I'm out of black.” |
| “This machine requires blue mana.” | “This thing runs on blue.” |
| “She's generating too much red mana.” | “She's building up too much red!” |
| “Can you detect green mana nearby?” | “Can you feel any green around here?” |
| “His soul cannot store foreign mana colors.” | “His soul can't hold anything but black.” |
| “The enchantment is drawing excessive white mana.” | “It's drinking the white faster than it should.” |

### Cultural examples (illustrative, from the approved exchange)

- **Kobold:** “Broken mirrors! We're losing blue!” — a device malfunction.
- **Naga:** “Her control of the blue is exquisite.” — praise for a Crownmind.
- **Tidekin:** “Darling, I've barely got enough blue left to freeze my drink.”
- **Vren:** “Careful. I have plenty of black left.”
- **Moontouched Warlock:** “I can't make my own, sweetheart. But if you've got some red, I can certainly make it useful.”

### Mechanical boundary

This is a **terminology convention**, not a change to the magic system. MP remains a formal measurement; the five colors keep their established properties and costs. Rules, Shimmer interface, research, and technical discussions may still say *mana capacity*, *mana regeneration*, *mana manipulation*, or *MP*. In dialogue favor *shape the white*, *draw the blue*, *spend the red*, *run out of black*, as appropriate.

Nymbrin speech casually calls a kingdom's debt its **tab**, jobs **gigs**, and investments **bets**. Their commercial metaphors are playful and fun-seeking, becoming precise when actual terms matter. ## November 6, 2026 — Cogsong (APPROVED NAME)

**Cogsong** is the approved proper name for Centaur clockwork's mechanically encoded, music-box-like acoustic form of Starsong. It is transmitted by a **mandatory Command Loom** and cannot be spoken as a valid command by an unaided living creature, including Starspawn. Unlike Kobold Cipherlight, Cogsong relies on sound and suffers signal attenuation, environmental masking, decoding delays and acoustic obstructions. Silent, Town, Battle and War remain the named signal conditions, with exact range and latency modifiers UNAPPROVED. Prior proposed names Cogchant, Clockcant, Brassong and Anvilverse are superseded; they are NOT alternative canonical names. Do not conflate Cogsong with ordinary spoken Starsong or override established Speaking-in-Colors conventions.
