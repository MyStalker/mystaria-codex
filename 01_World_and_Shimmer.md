## Source-derived world and divine canon

## 1. Mystaria: Overview

Mystaria is a dark fantasy world inhabited by intelligent animal-inspired peoples, ancient ruins, mana-shaped ecosystems, and the Starspawn: intelligent magical creatures that have replaced ordinary nonsapient wildlife. Much of its political history was shaped by the Serpentari Imperium, an oppressive civilization of immense serpents whose sorcery and slave-based economy once dominated the world. Enslavement persists in different forms across the surviving kingdoms, alongside movements for emancipation, legal reform, and rebellion. The world can be frightening and morally complicated, but also allows whimsy, intimate companionship, eccentric heroes, and comedy.

Five colors of mana—red, blue, green, white, and black—govern magic. Souls generate, store, and shape mana according to their affinities and innate limitations. The Celestial Realm is home to the goddesses and is the ancestral homeland to Starspawn. Reincarnated Earth humans chosen by a goddess become Heroes and gain access to the Shimmer, an interface for advancement and extraordinary conveniences.

Three central goddesses embody radically different philosophies. Arrest presides over justice, restraint, and authority; Surrender over love, trust, nature, and freely chosen submission; Baphomet over revenge, pain, chaos, and betrayed promises. They are complicated individuals who disagree sharply about slavery, lawful punishment, and the proper use of power.

### Approved sky and Shimmer appearance — November 4, 2026

- **Sky color (CANON):** Mystaria's skies generally tend toward shades of **purple**, and occasionally **red**. This is a world characteristic rather than a universal, unchanging sky color. The underlying cause and exact timing are not yet established.
- **Shimmer color (CANON):** The Shimmer's identifying color is **purple**. Individual patron-associated styling may still differ, consistent with the established statement that its appearance reflects the Hero's patron. Its mechanics and access restrictions are unchanged.
- **Editorial identity (VISUAL CANON):** Purple is also Mystaria's signature color for titles and recurring rulebook backgrounds; this is not a mandate to recolor all in-world objects. See `11_Visual_Style_Guide.md` for usage details.

## 2. The Serpentari Imperium and the God War

The Serpentari, enormous **naturally aquatic** intelligent serpents capable of assuming humanoid forms, were among the earliest and most sophisticated mortal manipulators of mana. Their true forms were adapted to underwater existence and travel. They established an immense private network of deep, submerged waterways spanning Mystaria; the hidden **Scaled City** is a persistent but unverified rumor about its final destination. For precise canon and unresolved names, see [`34_Ancient_Serpentari_Aquatic_Waterways_Canon.md`](34_Ancient_Serpentari_Aquatic_Waterways_Canon.md). They developed the Imperium of the First Coil, a civilization of palaces, magical academies, immense infrastructure, and comprehensive enslavement. They engineered Naga as privileged estate overseers and administrators and enslaved the ancestors of several modern peoples. They rejected worship of the gods, considering divinity a superior form of power to be understood and eventually rivaled.

The **God War** was fought **between the gods and the Serpentari**, not between the gods themselves. During it, gods and mortal followers fashioned Starspawn as immortal living weapons in the Celestial Forges. The war devastated Mystaria and ended with the collapse of the Serpentari empire. Several races' defining liberations and blessings trace to the conflict: the Vren were granted vampirism by Baphomet following betrayal; the Duskpaws gained black-mana Fraycraft and escaped their chains in the Night of a Thousand Locks; Corvyn witchcraft originated with the Thirteen Witnesses; and the Firstshaped arose from the interrupted Great Reversion.

Serpentari decline preceded the war. Aristocratic experimentation intended to perfect their descendants caused **the Hollow Coil**, a cumulative hereditary loss of fertility. Powerful lineages became least able to reproduce, and their enormous natural lifespans encouraged complacency. By the war's end the civilization had lost both its population and much of the knowledge necessary to reverse its decline. Fewer than ten Serpentari survive today, all concealed.

## 3. The Canopy Kingdoms — Realm of the Living Crown

**Location:** Central Mystaria. **Government:** Hereditary feudal monarchies. **Principal races:** Thornguard ratfolk and Bloomkin mousefolk. **Religion:** Surrender. **Principal magic:** White-mana Concordance.

The Canopy Kingdoms occupy the Great Canopy, an immense ancient forest whose towering trees rise hundreds of feet above the ground. Interconnected foliage and colossal limbs support castles, villages, markets, bridges, and elevated roads. Forest floors remain dangerous with predatory Starspawn and other hazards. Rivers, waterfalls, hanging gardens, and magically sustained farms nourish the settlements. High platforms enjoy sunlight while lower levels are perpetually shadowed.

Thornguard nobility occupy fortified castles around colossal trunks, with towers, courtyards, tournament fields, and magically reinforced bridges. Bloomkin neighborhoods fit within hollow trees, castle walls, and miniature lower-level dwellings; entire communities may inhabit one estate. Wooden lifts, ramps, and winding walkways accommodate movement between levels. White-mana Concordance fortifies structures against storms, fire, and monsters.

Their peoples were blessed by Surrender during Serpentari aggression: the powerful ratfolk as protectors and clever mousefolk as their complementary partners. Once cooperative, their society developed hereditary Thornguard Banners and the **First Covenant of Protection**, establishing noble authority and the **Oath of Protection** that reduced most Bloomkin to inherited servitude. Kings and queens rule through noble Banners and military vassals. Bloomkin hold little formal authority, though talented Shieldmates exert informal influence.

Thornguard religion emphasizes protective responsibility, obedience, and knightly oaths. Bloomkin Hearthwhispers emphasize the goddess's conviction that trust must be voluntary, making their hereditary subordination a theological contradiction. Institutions include **Crown Bridges** linking cities, white-mana-protected **Living Bastions**, knight-and-Shieldmate **Orders**, **Grand Canopy Tournaments**, internal **Hollow Markets**, and hidden **Rootways** used by smugglers and fugitives. The legendary **Perfect Promise** is a form of Concordance attained only through freely chosen equality. The kingdoms' slave system may therefore be suppressing their greatest magical potential.

# PART II — THE THREE GODDESSES

## 4. Arrest — Goddess of Justice and Restraint

Arrest is a brilliant, playful, practical spider goddess of justice, restraint, lawful punishment, order, and responsibility. In person she appears as an elegant anthropomorphic spider with black chitin, four arms, eight crimson eyes, and a large arachnid abdomen. Her symbol is a silver web surrounding scales of judgment.

Arrest believes law exists to protect people, no person is above judgment, and authority creates accountability rather than exemption. Punishment should ordinarily be proportionate; restraint is preferable to needless killing; and unjust laws should be revised. She accepts bondage only as a legitimately imposed judicial sentence, rejecting inherited slavery of innocent people. She created the all-female Driders as daughters and agents of justice. She enjoys strategy, puzzles, management games, and cleverly exploited rules. She even visits modern Earth, where she has a comfortable apartment and an affection for video games.

Arrest is Surrender's romantic partner and has a tense rivalry with Baphomet. Although an advocate of principled justice, she privately possesses sadistic and masochistic appetites that become dangerously apparent in her secret Velvet Heresy, the Buried Verdict.

---

## 5. Surrender — Goddess of Love and Freely Given Trust

Surrender is a gentle, shy, emotional but wise bunny goddess of love, nature, affection, fertility, trust, and **voluntary** submission. She is an anthropomorphic rabbit with soft white fur and long floppy ears, usually wearing pastel dresses. She loves Arrest and freely trusts her to take the lead in their relationship. She maintains a complicated older affection for Baphomet, who was once associated with innocence and forgiveness.

Surrender teaches that dependence is not shameful, that kindness is powerful, and that consent is the distinction between loving surrender and enforced domination. Her defining saying is **“What is taken cannot be surrendered. What is demanded cannot be freely given.”** She created the Moontouched and inspired the mutually dependent white-mana gifts of Thornguard and Bloomkin. She prefers gentle resolutions, rescues, friendships, and finding ways to protect the vulnerable without simply giving them overwhelming destructive powers. Her specially chosen Moontouched Warlocks can redirect borrowed mana but never generate or shape ordinary spells.

She and Arrest spend time on modern Earth; Surrender particularly enjoys cozy games such as Stardew Valley and Animal Crossing. She has gradually allowed her Heroes to purchase conventional weapons as well as healing equipment because she trusts them to defend themselves responsibly. Beneath her compassion she possesses a poorly concealed masochistic side and an even more secret technique for experiencing alternative histories through her Heroes.

## 6. Baphomet — Goddess of Revenge and Broken Promises

Baphomet is an anthropomorphic female lamb goddess of revenge, pain, chaos, betrayal, and broken promises, appearing with white wool, curled horns, and often a bloodstained crimson dress. Once a tender goddess of innocence, patience, and forgiveness, she changed after her trust and followers were repeatedly exploited. She now believes that betrayal creates a debt and that those who abuse power should taste the consequences of their actions.

Baphomet is clever, mischievous, cruel, playful, vindictive, and fond of poetic reversals. She likes contracts, exact wording, revenge, and powerful people being made helpless by those they despised. She grants patronage to Vren, Duskpaws, and Corvyn, each interpreting her teachings differently: contractual revenge, breaking captivity, and preserving testimony against forgotten atrocities. Baphomet is sadistic rather than masochistic. She remains emotionally entangled with Surrender and delights in provoking Arrest. She prefers to honor the **exact promises she makes**, while freely exploiting everything left unsaid. Her Velvet Heresy is the Crimson Invitation, through which she violates divine patronage boundaries without accepting responsibility for resulting disasters.

## 18. Magic in Mystaria — The Five Colors of Mana

**Source:** Mana, the fundamental supernatural energy of Mystaria. **Primary Colors:** Red, Blue, Green, White, and Black. **Foundation:** The relationship between mana, the soul, and physical reality. **Practitioners:** Sorcerers, witches, priests, enchanters, Summoners, and magically gifted races.

### The Nature of Mana

Magic in Mystaria originates from **mana**, a supernatural energy permeating the physical world, the Celestial Realm, and the souls of living creatures. Every soul possesses a unique magical structure determining which colors of mana it can generate, store, and manipulate. Most intelligent races possess natural affinities for particular colors. Although additional disciplines can sometimes be learned, manipulating unfamiliar mana is considerably more difficult. Casting spells generally requires three components: **mana as fuel, the soul as a conduit, and magical shaping to produce an effect.** More sophisticated magic may require spoken incantations, gestures, enchanted objects, or ritual inscriptions.

### The Five Colors of Mana

| Color | Properties | Common applications |
|---|---|---|
| Red | Destruction, heat, energy, physical empowerment | Fire, explosive attacks, strengthened muscles, offensive enchantments |
| Blue | Adaptation, transformation, perception, knowledge | Water manipulation, illusions, sensory magic, alchemy |
| Green | Life, growth, regeneration, nature | Healing, vegetation manipulation, biological enhancement |
| White | Protection, stability, order, reinforcement | Barriers, purification, defensive enchantments, magical restraints |
| Black | Entropy, decay, curses, deterioration | Hexes, weakening spells, material degradation, destructive cold |

No color is inherently good or evil. Black mana can remove dangerous enchantments, while white mana can reinforce oppressive restraints. The morality of magic depends upon its wielder.

### Magic and the Soul

The soul acts as both reservoir and regulator of mana. Its natural affinity determines which energies can be safely stored and manipulated. Attempting to channel incompatible mana can cause exhaustion, magical backlash, or spiritual damage. Different races possess distinct limitations. Moontouched lack conventional magical abilities entirely. Thornguard generate abundant white mana but cannot shape it, while Bloomkin can shape white mana but cannot produce sufficient amounts independently. Through **Concordance**, they combine these abilities. Corvyn naturally store only black mana, relying upon Starspawn familiars and enchanted tattoos to channel other colors. The ancient Serpentari mastered all five colors through extraordinary study and sophisticated magical techniques.

### Strengths and Weaknesses

Magic can accomplish feats impossible through ordinary physical means, including healing injuries, transforming matter, reinforcing structures, manipulating natural forces, and creating supernatural bindings. However, mana reserves are finite, and powerful spells demand significant energy. Excessive casting causes exhaustion and potentially dangerous backlash. Complex enchantments require concentration, preparation, and precise magical shaping. Different mana colors can counteract one another. White mana stabilization frequently resists black mana deterioration, while black mana can weaken improperly maintained magical barriers. Environmental conditions, specialized restraints, and suppression can further limit spellcasting. **Powerful magic is not necessarily versatile magic:** a practitioner may possess immense mana reserves yet lack the skill to shape them effectively.

### Starspawn and Summoners

Starspawn are magical creatures originally created by the gods during their war against the Serpentari. Each possesses an immortal **Astral Heart**, preserving its consciousness even when its physical body is destroyed. Starspawn have innate mana affinities and magical abilities corresponding to their species. Summoners establish spiritual relationships called **Accords** with individual Starspawn. These bonds allow Summoners to expend mana to summon their companions, heal them, and invoke their unique Named Spells. Summoning requires magical shaping, making it inaccessible to ordinary Moontouched. Corvyn witches instead establish **Witchbonds**, through which familiars teach them spells and provide foreign mana without granting conventional summoning abilities.

Ultimately, magic in Mystaria is defined not simply by possessing power, but by **the relationship between a soul's limitations, its understanding of mana, and the ways individuals cooperate to overcome those limitations.**

## 21. Starspawn — Children of the Fallen Heavens

**Classification:** Divine Monsters / Celestial Beasts. **Origins:** The Celestial Realm during the God War. **Population:** Extremely abundant; Mystaria's dominant wildlife. **Magical Affinity:** Red, Blue, Green, White, or Black Mana. **Intelligence:** Animalistic to extraordinarily intelligent. **Mortality:** Physically destructible, spiritually immortal. **Civilization:** None; predominantly wild creatures.

### Origins and History

Starspawn are magical creatures originally created as living weapons during the ancient **God War, a catastrophic conflict between the gods and the godlike Serpentari civilization**. Unable to manifest their full divine power without devastating Mystaria, the gods collaborated with their mortal followers to create armies of magical creatures within the Celestial Realm. These creatures were engineered for different battlefield roles, including assault, defense, reconnaissance, and magical disruption. To prevent permanent losses, the gods gave every Starspawn an immortal spiritual core called an **Astral Heart**, allowing its consciousness to survive the destruction of its physical body. Following the war, Starspawn were released into Mystaria. Over millennia, they reproduced, diversified, and completely replaced ordinary nonsapient animals. Their ancestral homeland remains the Celestial Realm, where the goddesses reside.

### Biology and Reproduction

Starspawn vary enormously in appearance, size, and physiology. Most resemble fantastical animals rather than humanoids, including mammalian, reptilian, aquatic, insectoid, and plantlike species. They possess physical needs such as food, water, rest, and shelter. Depending on their species, they reproduce through eggs, live births, spores, or magical budding. Their offspring possess independent Astral Hearts. Every Starspawn has a dominant mana affinity influencing its anatomy, abilities, and behavior.

| Mana | Natural characteristics |
|---|---|
| Red | Heat, aggression, speed, destructive energy |
| Blue | Adaptation, fluidity, perception, transformation |
| Green | Growth, regeneration, vitality, resilience |
| White | Protection, stability, reinforcement, cooperation |
| Black | Decay, curses, deterioration, entropy |

These affinities influence behavior but do not determine personality or morality.

### Immortality and Celestial Recall

Starspawn cannot truly die. When their bodies suffer fatal damage, their Astral Hearts preserve consciousness, memories, and personality. A severely injured Starspawn may be trapped in **the Stillness**, remaining conscious despite being unable to move. If recovery becomes impossible, **Celestial Recall** returns its spirit to the Celestial Realm, where its body gradually regenerates. This usually occurs within days or weeks, although complete bodily destruction can trigger immediate Recall. Starspawn remember previous defeats, injuries, friendships, and mistreatment, learning across multiple bodies.

### Starsong — The Universal Language

All Starspawn instinctively understand civilized speech through magical comprehension inherited from their divine creators. Most cannot physically speak mortal languages. Instead, they communicate through **Starsong**, a universal language combining vocalizations, movements, emotional impressions, and magical resonance. Every Starspawn understands Starsong regardless of species. Some ancient battlefield commanders, magically awakened individuals, and divinely blessed Starspawn can speak directly with mortals. Speaking Starspawn are uncommon and frequently possess exceptional intelligence.

### Ecology and Society

Starspawn occupy every ecological niche in Mystaria, functioning as predators, grazers, scavengers, pollinators, and decomposers. Their abilities influence environments: green Starspawn encourage vegetation, blue varieties shape aquatic habitats, and black varieties accelerate decomposition. Despite their intelligence, Starspawn do not establish civilizations or cities. They generally live as wild animals, forming territories, herds, packs, or solitary habitats. Civilized races depend upon them for transportation, agriculture, construction, protection, companionship, and magical materials. However, dangerous Starspawn can destroy settlements, disrupt trade, damage infrastructure, and threaten travelers. Their immortality makes persistent threats difficult to eliminate permanently.

### Universal Magical Strengths and Weaknesses

**Strengths:** Immortal consciousness and natural bodily reconstruction; innate magical abilities requiring little formal training; universal understanding of mortal speech and Starsong; adaptation through repeated experience; elemental affinities and resistance to their own mana type.

**Weaknesses:** Physical bodies remain vulnerable to injury, exhaustion, and destruction; Celestial Recall requires time; magical suppression can disable their abilities; incompatible mana can disrupt their physiology; instincts may be exploited; despite immortality they remain vulnerable to pain, fear, restraint, and magical imprisonment.

### Summoners and Accords

Summoners establish magical relationships called **Accords** through friendship, victory, exchange, rescue, or coercion. An Accord permits the Summoner to expend mana to summon and heal a companion. Calling a Starspawn's recognized name invokes its powerful **Named Spell**. All magically capable races can become Summoners except Moontouched, whose souls cannot sustain the shaping required. Corvyn witches instead maintain Witchbonds to learn foreign spells and cooperate with familiars, without gaining conventional summoning.

### Elder Stars

**Elder Stars** are extraordinarily ancient Starspawn whose Astral Hearts have accumulated centuries or millennia of experience. Some remember the God War itself. They possess exceptional intelligence, powerful magic, and sophisticated personalities. Some speak, communicate with gods, or recognize forgotten Serpentari artifacts. They generally dwell in isolation and avoid mortal civilization. Their existence suggests Starspawn may have been designed not merely to survive the God War, but to grow stronger in preparation for another.

The following species exemplify how each mana color expresses itself in the wild and through a Summoner's Named Invocation.

## 22. Nightfox — The Moonless Familiar

**Classification:** Black-mana Starspawn. **Family:** One of the Five Celestial Foxes. **Rarity:** Uncommon; golden-marked individuals especially prized. **Habitat:** Dark forests, ruins, caves, graveyards, Scarlands. **Temperament:** Cautious, affectionate, intelligent, mischievous. **Speech:** Cannot speak mortal languages.

Nightfoxes are slightly larger than ordinary foxes, slender and physically fragile, with black velvet fur, large ears, sleek tails, crimson or amber eyes, and softly luminous ringlike markings. Most markings are silver, violet, or gray; golden individuals are revered as witches' familiars. They are naturally nocturnal but sunlight does not burn them as it does Vren.

The five related fox Starspawn were created during the God War for different roles: **Emberfox** (red, incendiary assault), **Mistfox** (blue, scouting and adaptation), **Briarfox** (green, regeneration and environmental influence), **Dawnfox** (white, defense and purification), and **Nightfox** (black, sabotage and attrition). Nightfoxes were effective against Serpentari defenses because their spells weakened enchanted fortifications and equipment.

Nightfoxes produce **Entropy Orbs**, violet-black spheres of cold entropy projected from their mouths. Lesser orbs drain warmth and numb prey; greater orbs damage defenses, weaken armor, and cause severe supernatural cold; experienced individuals can leave lingering clouds of deterioration. This is not ordinary ice: it removes usable energy, weakens materials, and interferes with enchantments. Their **Named Spell, Moonless Star**, releases an enlarged sphere that expands into a destructive cold field, consuming enormous mana and exhausting the creature. Their weak musculature and bones make close combat especially dangerous.

Nightfoxes live in small family territories, often hunting alone but gathering to groom and rest. Females usually bear two to four kits. Their diet includes smaller Starspawn, eggs, fungi, berries, carrion, and residual magical energy; magic cannot replace food. They collect bedding and shiny objects, enjoy warmth and fireplaces, sleep tightly curled, hide favorite trinkets, and sometimes mischievously cool their companions' possessions. They understand mortal speech, communicate with other Starspawn through Starsong, and communicate with mortals through chirps, growls, posture, ears, and tails—but **they cannot talk**.

**Golden Nightfoxes**, sometimes called Gilded Nightfoxes, possess exceptional sensitivity to enchantments, cursed objects, and unstable magical rituals. They are traditional familiars of witches and can maintain Witchbonds through generations. A Corvyn witch with a golden Nightfox gains a patient magical teacher especially suited to advanced black-mana Veilcraft. Witchbonds do not confer the Named Spell or instant summoning. A Summoner, by contrast, may establish an Accord to summon a Nightfox and invoke Moonless Star.

Nightfoxes are useful for magical detection, pest control, night security, companionship, and ritual assistance. Problems include accidental damage from entropy attacks, weakening furniture or enchantments, and criminal use in magical sabotage. Baphomet's followers associate them with hidden consequences and revenge; Surrender values their affectionate natures, and Arrest may use their curse sensitivity in investigations. Ancient golden-marked Nightfoxes may remember magical techniques long lost to mortal witches.

## 23. Summoners — Keepers of the Celestial Accord

**Classification:** Magical Adventurer Class. **Magical Affinity:** Any of the five mana colors. **Eligible Races:** All magically capable races except Moontouched. **Primary Abilities:** Starspawn summoning, healing, and Named Spell invocation. **Source of Power:** Mana and spiritual agreements called Accords. **Divine Allegiance:** Any goddess, or none.

### Origins and History

Summoners are magical practitioners who establish spiritual partnerships with **Starspawn**, the immortal magical creatures originally created by the gods during the God War against the Serpentari. During the war, divine followers developed methods of coordinating Starspawn armies through magical bonds. These techniques eventually evolved into the modern art of Summoning. Unlike conventional sorcerers, Summoners specialize in channeling their mana through other living beings rather than directly manipulating the environment.

### The Celestial Accord

The foundation of Summoning is the **Accord**, a spiritual agreement connecting a Summoner's soul to a Starspawn's immortal Astral Heart. Accords can be established through several methods:

- **Friendship:** A Starspawn willingly bonds with someone it trusts.
- **Victory:** A defeated Starspawn agrees to serve a worthy opponent.
- **Exchange:** Both parties negotiate a mutually beneficial arrangement.
- **Salvation:** A rescued Starspawn forms a bond through gratitude.
- **Dominion:** Magical coercion forces an unstable, potentially resentful bond.

Every Accord is unique, reflecting the Starspawn's personality and its relationship with the Summoner. Starspawn retain intelligence, memories, and individuality. They understand mortal speech, although most cannot speak themselves, instead communicating through body language and Starsong.

### Summoning Magic

Once an Accord is established, the Summoner can expend mana to transport their Starspawn companion from the Celestial Realm or another location in Mystaria. Summoning costs depend upon the creature's size, power, distance, and the strength of their spiritual connection. Summoners can also transfer mana into companions to accelerate healing, restore stamina, and sustain magical abilities. Their signature technique is the **Named Invocation**. By calling a bonded Starspawn's recognized name and channeling mana through their Accord, a Summoner can activate its unique **Named Spell**. These powerful abilities vary enormously between species, ranging from devastating elemental attacks to protective barriers and restorative effects. Named Spells require substantial mana and may exhaust both participants.

### Relationships with Starspawn

Successful Summoners are often skilled caretakers, negotiators, and companions. Starspawn possess preferences, fears, loyalties, and ambitions. Some appreciate affection and companionship, while others demand challenges, food, or exploration. Trust strengthens an Accord, improving cooperation and magical efficiency. A resentful Starspawn may refuse commands, deliberately misunderstand instructions, or resist its Summoner. Because Starspawn possess immortal Astral Hearts, they can maintain relationships across multiple mortal generations. Unlike Corvyn Witchbonds, which allow familiars to teach spells of their mana affinity, **Accords emphasize summoning, healing, and invoking the Starspawn's own abilities**.

### Strengths and Weaknesses

**Strengths:** Exceptional versatility, access to multiple Starspawn abilities, ranged and defensive options, magical healing, and coordination of several companions.

**Weaknesses:** Heavy mana consumption, dependence on bonded Starspawn, limited simultaneous summons, vulnerability when companions are unavailable, and the difficulty of managing independent personalities. Summoners cannot permanently resurrect destroyed Starspawn bodies. Companions undergoing Celestial Recall must recover before returning. Summoning requires active mana shaping, excluding Moontouched from the profession.

### Their Place in Mystaria

Summoners serve as adventurers, monster handlers, caravan guards, explorers, and military specialists. Their greatest strength is not necessarily personal magical power, but their ability to develop effective partnerships with creatures possessing extraordinary abilities.

*“A beast commanded may fight for you. A companion trusted will choose to stand beside you.”*

## 24. Reincarnated Heroes — The Goddesses' Chosen

A **Hero** is an Earth-born human reincarnated into Mystaria in the form of one of its intelligent races by Arrest, Surrender, or Baphomet. Heroes remember their Earth lives and possess the **Shimmer**, an exclusive divine interface that ordinary residents cannot use. Being chosen does not automatically grant extraordinary combat experience or racial powers beyond those of a typical level-1 individual. Heroes retain the biological limitations and magic constraints of the bodies into which they reincarnate.

Heroes begin at **level 1**, with the absolute racial starting attributes, modifiers, and special abilities described in Part VII. Leveling through experience awards Attribute Points; separate Ability Points purchase and improve qualifying racial or class abilities. The five attributes are Strength, Dexterity, Endurance, Intelligence, and Speed. Milestones at effective scores 25 and 100 represent heroic and legendary power.

A Hero's patron affects the activities that award XP. Arrest favors justice, investigation, legitimate capture, legal problems, and clever use of rules. Surrender rewards protection, compassion, friendship, peaceful resolution, and befriending Starspawn rather than killing them. Baphomet rewards revenge, sabotage, exposure of hypocrisy, creative rule-breaking, and mischievous humiliation. Accomplishments may earn Gems, the currency of patron-specific divine shops, including Arrest's **Ordered Armory**, Surrender's **Moonlit Garden**, and Baphomet's **Crimson Bazaar**. Their divine quests can be serious, minor, eccentric, or comically petty, and may award XP, Gems, Ability Points, and occasionally Attribute Points.

Ability progression is divided into **five tiers**: Tier I available at level 1, Tier II from level 10, Tier III from level 25, Tier IV from level 50, and Tier V from level 80. Higher tiers must be bought or unlocked; leveling does not automatically grant every power. A Drider might advance Verdict Thread, a Tidekin Witness Wake, a Vren Crimson Clause, and a Warlock Open Channel. Heroes are still mortal and must learn their abilities and manage resources carefully.

## Shimmer XP and encounter threat — approved November 5, 2026

**Status: APPROVED CANON.** This section is the operative numerical Shimmer XP progression. It does not replace the established patron-specific criteria for whether an action earns XP, the existing level-up attribute awards, or the separate Skill Point economy.

### XP required to gain a level

For a Hero at current level **L**:

```text
XP required to advance from level L to L + 1 = L × (L + 1)
```

This is the **XP required for the next level**, not a cumulative lifetime-XP threshold. Progress toward the next level carries forward when a reward exceeds the remaining requirement; recompute the next requirement at the new level.

| Current level | XP to next level | Equal-level Henchman XP | Henchmen needed from zero progress |
| ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| 1 | 2 | 2 | 1 |
| 2 | 6 | 4 | 2 |
| 5 | 30 | 10 | 3 |
| 10 | 110 | 20 | 6 |
| 25 | 650 | 50 | 13 |
| 50 | 2,550 | 100 | 26 |
| 75 | 5,700 | 150 | 38 |
| 100 | 10,100 | 200 | 51 |

This targets approximately one qualifying equal-level encounter to advance at level 1 and fifty at level 100 (the discrete result is **51**).

### Threat classes and suggested relative level bands

Use the **Hero's current level** as the comparison point. The level bands below are **encounter-design guidelines**, not automatic overrides of actual threat. The GM designates an encounter's threat class based on combat capability, numbers, equipment, magic, terrain, coordination, and special mechanics. A boss need not exceed the Hero's level if its encounter design justifies the designation.

| Class | Suggested enemy level relative to Hero | Intended threat | XP modifier |
| --- | --- | --- | ---: |
| **Horde** | Up to and including 50% | Swarmable, individually trivial | ×0.1 |
| **Minion** | Above 50% through 80% | Minor threat individually | ×0.4 |
| **Henchman** | Above 80% through 110% | Nearly equal combatant, slightly weaker or comparable | ×1 |
| **Elite** | Above 110% through 140% | Miniboss, substantial tactical challenge | ×2 |
| **Boss** | Above 140%, **or specially designated** | Major prepared, prolonged encounter | Special: two levels |

These bands are comparative guidelines, not minimum level requirements or a mandate that every enemy at a given ratio has a particular role. **Horde** refers to the threat posed by each member; it does not multiply a single enemy's XP by the number in a swarm.

### Standard encounter and quest XP

```text
XP = 2 × encounter level × threat modifier
```

For a qualifying defeated enemy, **encounter level** is that enemy's level. The same threat-equivalent structure can price a patron-qualifying quest, investigation, rescue, peaceful resolution, or other accomplishment, using a GM-assigned equivalent challenge level and class. **No killing requirement** is implied. This does not make ordinary mundane quests automatically award XP: the accomplishment must still qualify under the Hero's patron's established XP principles.

Round a fractional ordinary reward to the nearest whole XP, with a minimum of **1 XP for a qualifying encounter**. This rounding convention is for the calculation only and does **not** establish that trivial or repetitive actions always qualify for an XP award. No additional anti-farming numerical penalty is established by this approval.

For example, a level-100 Henchman awards **200 XP**; a level-100 Horde enemy awards **20 XP**; a level-100 Minion awards **80 XP**; a level-100 Elite awards **400 XP**, if the GM assigns those respective threat roles.

### Boss XP: advance exactly two levels

A **qualifying Boss** awards enough XP for the Hero to advance **exactly two levels from their level at the moment of victory**, accounting for their current progress toward the next level. This **replaces** the ordinary enemy-level multiplier, regardless of the boss's own level.

If the Hero is level **L** and has **P** XP already accumulated toward level **L + 1**, the Boss XP award is:

```text
Boss XP = [L × (L + 1) − P] + [(L + 1) × (L + 2)]
```

The award advances the Hero from **L** to **L + 2**, with **zero remaining progress** toward **L + 3**. Example: a level-50 Hero with zero progress receives **2,550 + 2,652 = 5,202 XP** and reaches level 52. A level-50 Hero with 500 XP progress instead receives **4,702 XP** for the same two-level result.

**Unchanged boundaries:** Patron-specific XP qualification, mundane quest bookkeeping, Shimmer level-up stat gains, racial/Craft/Sorcery tier gates, SP prices, and other existing progression rules remain intact. No new repeat-farming restriction or special party XP division rule has been approved.

## 29. The Shimmer — Revised Attribute and Racial System

**System:** Absolute Attribute Progression  
**Starting Level:** 1  
**Attributes:** Strength, Dexterity, Endurance, Intelligence, Speed  
**Starting Attribute Range:** 1–10 before racial modifiers  
**Racial Multipliers:** Generally +10% to +30%, or penalties up to −20%  
**Progression:** 1 Attribute Point per Hero level

### I. Attribute Mechanics

The Shimmer measures attributes using an **absolute scale shared by all races**. Attribute values represent the same capabilities regardless of species.

A Moontouched with 10 Strength possesses the same measurable lifting and striking power as a Naga with 10 Strength. A Moontouched with 100 Strength can overpower a Serpentari with 80 Strength.

| Attribute | Abbreviation | Governs |
|---|---|---|
| Strength | STR | Lifting, striking power, grappling, muscular force |
| Dexterity | DEX | Coordination, precision, reflexes, balance, fine motor control |
| Endurance | END | Durability, stamina, physical resilience, exhaustion resistance |
| Intelligence | INT | Reasoning, memory, learning speed, analytical ability |
| Speed | SPD | Movement speed, acceleration, rapid repositioning |

#### Attribute progression

As of November 5, 2026, each established Hero racial starting base attribute is increased by **10 before racial multipliers**. Each level still grants **2 allocatable base-stat points**, and every fifth level grants **+1 to all five base stats**; the historical one-point rule in this section is superseded. **All attributes have a hard minimum of 1**, including monsters, after applicable modifiers. Monsters may have stats below 10.

Racial percentage modifiers apply to the base attribute, including all subsequent increases.

**Effective Attribute = max(1, Base Attribute × Racial Multiplier)**

For example, a Thornguard with **18** new base Strength and a **+25%** multiplier possesses **22.5** effective Strength. Raising the base Strength to 19 produces **23.75** effective Strength.

Effective attributes retain decimal values. Milestones use the final modified value.

| Effective Attribute | Classification |
|---|---|
| 1–34.99 | Ordinary to exceptional mortal (migrated historical milestone) |
| 35–109.99 | Heroic and supernatural |
| 110+ | Legendary |

Attributes determine capability, while anatomical differences still influence reach, equipment compatibility, and methods of movement. A character's size does not secretly multiply their Strength.

#### Special racial abilities

Certain advantages require additional definitions.

**Bloomkin — Cooperative Intuition:** Bloomkin excel at recognizing others' intentions, coordinating complex group activities, and understanding social dynamics. This is natural perception rather than telepathy.

**Naga — Ancestral Echoes:** Naga occasionally inherit impressions of ancestral experiences, granting fragments of knowledge, memories, or instincts. These impressions are incomplete and cannot replace personal education.

**Vren — Blood-Fueled Regeneration:** Vren naturally recover from injuries by consuming blood. Regeneration requires adequate nourishment and does not grant immunity to fatal injuries or environmental hazards.

**Corvyn — Veilsight:** Their eyes naturally perceive magical auras, residual enchantments, curses, certain invisible creatures, and supernatural connections. Veilsight cannot automatically read minds, identify lies, or penetrate every magical concealment.

**Drider — Anatomy revision (November 4, 2026):** Driders have two arms, six long spider-like legs, dark drow-like skin, pointed ears, and four solid-color eyes without pupils; eye color varies by individual. Natural wall climbing, vibration detection and biological silk production remain unchanged; no replacement ability or numerical stat change was specified. See `02_Races.md`.

### IV. Untouched Serpentari — Sovereign Aptitude

Untouched Serpentari are treated as an ordinary selectable race for Shimmer attributes and progression.

They are unmodified, humanoid Serpentari standing approximately seven to nine feet tall. They possess scaled bodies, serpentine heads, arms, legs, and long tails, but **lack the colossal true form and extensive magical enhancements of ancient Imperial Serpentari**.

Their distinctive racial advantage is **Sovereign Aptitude**.

#### Sovereign Aptitude

Untouched Serpentari possess extraordinary natural learning capabilities, allowing them to master physical, intellectual, and technical disciplines much faster than ordinary individuals.

The ability provides four advantages:

- **Accelerated Learning:** Mundane skills and proficiencies develop at approximately three times the ordinary training rate.
- **Observational Imitation:** After observing a physical technique once, they can attempt to reproduce its basic movements, provided their anatomy and attributes permit it.
- **Exceptional Retention:** Skills, instructions, and practical knowledge remain accessible after exceptionally long periods of inactivity.
- **Rapid Adaptation:** They recognize patterns in unfamiliar tools, fighting styles, and technical procedures with unusual efficiency.

Sovereign Aptitude cannot copy spells, divine powers, supernatural abilities, or skills requiring physical attributes the individual does not possess.

It accelerates training rather than granting instant mastery or additional Shimmer experience.

Untouched Serpentari receive no additional Ability Points.

### V. System Balance and Restrictions

Races are not required to possess identical starting power. Their attributes, racial advantages, and magical abilities contribute separately to their overall capabilities.

The following principles govern balance:

- **Statistical differences are intentional.** Driders possess the highest starting total due to their exceptional rarity.
- **Lower-stat races may receive compensatory Ability Points**, capped at three.
- **Strong racial abilities do not count as attribute increases.** Flight, venom, regeneration, specialized perception, and unusual anatomy operate through their own rules.
- **Significant biological limitations remain relevant.** Vren, for example, retain their severe sunlight vulnerability and blood dependency despite their high physical attributes.
- **Spellcasting is independent of racial advantages.** The five-color mana system, racial magical disciplines, and adventurer class abilities remain unchanged.
- **Racial modifiers persist across all levels**, allowing natural differences to remain relevant without limiting a Hero's ultimate potential.
- **True Serpentari, Starspawn, and gods** are excluded from normal racial benchmarks because their capabilities fall outside ordinary Hero starting statistics.

**Core principle:** All Heroes operate on the same absolute attribute scale. Racial starting values, multipliers, and biological advantages determine their initial capabilities, while leveling and individual development determine how far they can ultimately progress.

## 30. Shimmer Core Functions — Inventory, Navigation, Codex, Messages, and Quests

*“If you're going to send mortals into a dangerous world, the least you can do is give them a map.” — Arrest*

The **Shimmer** is a divine interface available exclusively to Heroes reincarnated from Earth by Arrest, Surrender, or Baphomet. Its appearance reflects the patron, but all Heroes share basic systems. It is opened through conscious thought and controlled mentally, by gesture, or by spoken command. Ordinary inhabitants cannot see or operate it. It cannot be physically stolen or separated from its owner. It does not make a level-1 Hero automatically powerful and does not bypass their race's weaknesses.

> **Current approved visual ruling (November 4, 2026):** The Shimmer is recognizably **purple**, while patron-specific stylistic variation remains possible. This updates its presentation, not its features or mechanical rules.

Its main menu contains **Status, Inventory, World Map, Codex, Quests, Messages, Shrines, Gem Shop, and Divine Channel**, with a free local clock and north-pointing compass.

### Inventory — The Pocket Between Worlds

Every Hero receives **six free inventory slots** capable of storing nongem-shop items as well as divine purchases: tools, weapons, clothing, food, potions, treasures, and discoveries. One slot holds one reasonably portable object or a stack of small identical items. **Approved weapon and universal ammunition sizing (November 12, 2026; latest author correction):** one personal weapon occupies **one Shimmer storage inventory slot**. **All ammunition counts as the same size for Shimmer storage:** one separate slot holds **30 individual ammunition pieces** of a given ammunition stack, regardless of whether they are arrows, bolts, firearm cartridges or other ammunition. This supersedes the earlier arrow-only wording and earlier unresolved bolt/cartridge capacities. Thirty pieces permit thirty ordinary single-projectile attacks only if that attack uses exactly one piece; rapid volleys and other actions expend their actual established ammunition counts. More ammunition requires additional storage slots. This storage rule does not change the separate **eleven equipped-gear positions**, or turn an equipped weapon into a stored weapon. Unusually large non-personal gear retains the existing object-size limit; no additional weapon-ammunition categories, combat bonuses, or mixed-stack rights are newly invented. Larger objects may use multiple slots or exceed inventory limits. Living creatures, including Starspawn, cannot be stored. Equipped gear does not use slots; Gems remain in a separate wallet. Stored food does not spoil, but storage does not repair damaged items. Objects vanish into a shimmer of light on storage and reappear on retrieval, never inside solid matter or another being. Objects retain their normal weight, size, and handling requirements after retrieval.

### Shrine Network — Divine Fast Travel

Fast travel occurs **only from a shrine belonging to the Hero's own patron goddess to another registered, active shrine of that same goddess**. Arrest's shrines form the **Silver Roads**, Surrender's the **Moonpaths**, and Baphomet's the **Crimson Ways**. A Hero must physically visit a shrine before registering it. At the departure shrine, the Shimmer lists visited destinations in the patron's network. The Hero must remain within its sacred bounds and complete a brief uninterrupted activation. Travel is blocked by destroyed or suppressed shrines, incompatible patron networks, and some sealing magic. Normally only the Hero travels; consenting companions require a group-travel privilege. This baseline shrine travel is **free** once compatible shrines are known. Arbitrary point-to-point waymark teleportation is **not** available.

### Earthname Registry — Instant Hero Messaging

Heroes can send instantaneous messages across Mystaria to other reincarnated Heroes **if they know the recipient's name from Earth**. The Shimmer identifies the original Earth identity even if the recipient has a different race, body, name, or goddess in Mystaria. Common names may require disambiguation when the sender does not know which person is intended. Text conversations and small group chats are possible across goddess allegiances. Heroes may silence, block, or voluntarily share details and locations. A name lookup does not automatically disclose race, level, location, patron, or current identity. The Registry cannot contact ordinary Mystaria residents or living people on Earth. Many Heroes keep their Earth names private.

| Tenth | Detailed lore and rare interactions |

A tooltip can provide practical information, such as whether a plant is edible, where an object is commonly found, or what a weapon is made of, without automatically revealing every secret.

### Cities — Points of Interest

In populated settlements, the Shimmer marks publicly identifiable **inns, blacksmiths, healers, markets, libraries, guild halls, quest boards, shrines, government buildings, and transit points**. The Hero can filter markers or ask the map for directions. The interface learns through public signs, observation, and accumulated local knowledge. Hidden rooms, secret criminal networks, and concealed slave markets do not automatically appear until discovered.

### Clock, Compass, and Environment

Every Hero receives a free **Celestial Clock** and **True Compass**. These display local time, date, approximate sunrise/sunset, north, and facing direction. The Shimmer may display basic temperature, weather, and known hazards. Powerful spatial or divine disturbances can make readings unreliable; the interface marks uncertain data rather than fabricating perfect accuracy.

### Mundane Quests — Adventurer's Journal

The Shimmer tracks ordinary jobs accepted from inhabitants as well as divine quests. A **mundane quest** can record the quest giver, objectives, promised payment, location, and progress. Heroes may manually create tasks such as visiting a town or learning a skill. Mundane quests award ordinary wages, favors, possessions, or reputation according to their real-world agreements; they **do not automatically generate Gems or XP**. However, a completed activity may independently qualify for the Hero's goddess-specific XP rules. The journal cannot compel a patron to pay or guarantee a quest giver has told the truth.

### Divine Communication — The Divine Channel

Every Hero can send private messages to their patron goddess through a **Divine Channel**, but goddesses are not obliged to answer immediately or solve every problem. Communication can take the form of Shimmer text, divine voice within the mind, a private projected avatar, dream visits, or, very rarely, direct physical manifestation. Physical manifestations require considerable energy and risk complicated divine interference.

**Arrest** communicates practically and intelligently, especially about investigations, laws, riddles, and strategy. She gives clues but dislikes automatically solving every problem. **Surrender** is warm, encouraging, and concerned about her Heroes' companions and safety; she sometimes sends support or small gifts unprompted. **Baphomet** teases, provokes, presents questionable opportunities, and sometimes offers spontaneous mischievous quests. A Hero normally contacts only their own patron directly; another goddess may be reached through a patron-arranged conversation, but not freely through the same channel.

The goddesses' direct assistance is limited by philosophy, resources, and the risk of attracting attention. Arrest will not automatically identify every criminal, Surrender will not eliminate every monster, and Baphomet will not personally punish everyone who insults a Hero. Exceptional interventions are possible but never guaranteed.

## Forbidden divine history

## 32. The Velvet Heresies — The Forbidden Indulgences of the Three Goddesses

**Classification:** Forbidden Divine Interventions. **Governing Law:** The Quiet Accord. **Practitioners:** Arrest, Surrender, and Baphomet. **Secrecy:** Absolute; each goddess conceals her own violations. **Primary Offenses:** Temporal manipulation, memory suppression, excessive punishment, and unauthorized divine intervention.

The **Velvet Heresies** are secret violations of divine law committed by Mystaria's three principal goddesses. Although Arrest, Surrender, and Baphomet possess different philosophies, each has discovered methods of circumventing restrictions imposed upon them following the God War against the Serpentari.

These restrictions, collectively called **the Quiet Accord**, were established to prevent another catastrophic conflict between divine powers. The Accord limits interference in mortal affairs, particularly the alteration of established history, manipulation of mortal consciousness, exploitation of divine dependents, and unauthorized interference with another deity's followers.

Goddesses may select reincarnated Heroes, grant blessings, assign quests, and provide divine rewards. However, their authority is restricted to particular relationships and circumstances. Directly manipulating another goddess's Heroes, rewriting history, or abusing their divine privileges for personal gratification violates the Accord. The goddesses are not incapable of breaking these laws; forbidden interventions may generate disturbances in reality, attracting scrutiny or weakening divine authority.

**Surrender's Unlived Mercy** originates from her secret fascination with helplessness and suffering. Compassionate by nature, she also possesses masochistic desires and enjoys vicariously experiencing her Heroes' ordeals while remaining safe. She creates temporary alternative histories in which generally willing Heroes experience capture, enslavement, brainwashing, monstrous predators, or other grim fates. Afterward she restores their original histories, offers memory options, and pays substantial Gems for silence.

**Arrest's Buried Verdict** reflects her sadistic and masochistic fascination with domination, predatory justice, and punishment. She encourages selected Heroes to hunt the guilty and impose exceptionally severe sentences, sometimes going beyond her own principles: branding, execution, brainwashing, and collective punishment of relatives. Arrest conceals questionable experiences through **suppression, not erasure, of memories**. Instinctive consequences can persist even when conscious recollection is inaccessible.

**Baphomet's Crimson Invitation** allows her to contact individuals outside her permitted divine patronage: Heroes chosen by other goddesses, ordinary mortals, and even Starspawn. She cannot directly grant Gems or Hero experience to outsiders, so she gives her Heroes intermediary quests to deliver valuables, arrange rewards, or perform miracles with granted magic. She especially enjoys the suffering of those who have earned her anger and makes little effort to rescue anyone from their consequences.

Each goddess excuses herself. Surrender insists the original history survives untouched. Arrest believes the culpable deserve frightening judgment. Baphomet claims she merely offers bargains and opportunities without compelling acceptance. None is comfortable with having her own logic applied against her.

The **Unspoken Archive**, a forgotten celestial repository, records traces of forbidden acts: discarded possibilities from Surrender, sealed testimonies from Arrest, and patronage violations from Baphomet. Discovery could expose all three and provide enemies such as surviving Serpentari dangerous insights into the structure of divine law.

*The Velvet Heresies show that the goddesses' greatest temptations often emerge from the same ideals they profess to embody.*

## 33. Surrender's Individual Heresy — The Unlived Mercy

**Violation:** Temporal manipulation and exploitation of divine dependents. **Secret Motivation:** Masochism, vicarious helplessness, and fascination with surrender.

Surrender can create temporary alternative histories called **Unlived Mercies**. Drawing on principles related to Starspawn Astral Hearts, she constructs a **Mercy Anchor** linking a Hero's consciousness to an unresolved branching moment while preserving their original continuity. A Hero who escaped a predatory Starspawn might instead experience capture; another might live through years of enslavement, magical subjugation, or brainwashing before returning to the original event. The alternative history is a genuine temporary possibility, not a simple dream or illusion.

Surrender shares their sensations and emotions through the spiritual link while remaining safely within the Celestial Realm. She overwhelmingly prefers Heroes who expressly enjoy such ordeals and consent to experiencing them; a few times she has wrongly assumed that anticipated enthusiasm was adequate permission. Her power is not unlimited time reversal: she needs a plausible alternative at an unfinished moment, substantial divine energy, and a stable Mercy Anchor. She cannot simply undo established deaths, historical disasters, or arbitrary events. Long alternative lifetimes become unstable, and the anchor's collapse could endanger a participant.

When the history dissolves, the Hero returns to the original outcome and chooses whether to retain all, some, or none of the conscious memories. Surrender commonly offers **500–2,500 Gems**, sometimes more, for secrecy. These rewards come from her divine reserves, rather than XP earned in a discarded history. Her masochistic tendencies are only a poorly kept secret, but Arrest and Baphomet do not know her temporal experiments exist. Surrender fears discovery, especially Arrest's judgment that she betrayed the trust she teaches everyone else to honor.

## 34. Arrest's Individual Heresy — The Buried Verdict

**Violation:** Excessive punishment, manipulation of justice, and memory suppression. **Secret Motivation:** Sadism, masochism, domination, and fascination with predatory authority.

Arrest secretly encourages selected Heroes to become more dominating, calculating, and predatory than their natural dispositions would ordinarily allow. Through **Predator's Mandates**, she rewards thorough investigations, ruthless captures, intimidating tribunals, and extraordinarily severe sentences. Although she still favors genuinely guilty targets, her enjoyment of punishment distorts her standards of proportionality. Sentences may include permanent branding, magical brainwashing, painful execution, or punishment inflicted upon an offender's family, even where individual relatives have committed no crime. Arrest attempts to rationalize these acts through increasingly strained judicial logic.

She enjoys the contrast between a victorious, self-assured Hero and a defeated enemy deprived of control. Her masochistic side inspires **the Counterweight**, a private practice through which she experiences echoes of the punishment her authority imposes.

Her preferred method of concealment is **the Sealed Memory**. She suppresses conscious access to memories rather than erasing them; the experiences remain intact within the soul. The affected person can still display learned fears, involuntary aversions, personality changes, or trauma responses without understanding their source. A cruel slave trader might reflexively dread chains, avoid former habits, or panic at a judicial symbol despite remembering none of the sentence that changed him. Such conditioned behavior is not necessarily true remorse or ethical reform.

The sealed memories may reemerge through powerful magic, divine intervention, or emotional triggers. Physical scars, deaths, and consequences remain, forcing Arrest to manipulate testimony and records rather than reality itself. She secretly preserves original versions of altered memories, telling herself that meticulous documentation makes her decisions accountable. The goddess of justice therefore keeps an archive containing evidence of her own violations of justice.

## 35. Baphomet's Individual Heresy — The Crimson Invitation

**Violation:** Unauthorized divine contact and circumvention of patronage restrictions. **Secret Motivation:** Sadistic entertainment, revenge, irony, and unpredictable consequences.

Baphomet secretly contacts individuals whom the Quiet Accord places outside her direct care. Through **Crimson Invitations**, she offers bargains to Heroes serving Arrest or Surrender, ordinary mortals, enslaved people, and even unusually intelligent Starspawn. Rewards may include information, enchanted items, blessings, limited miracles, or opportunities to take revenge on abusive rulers.

She cannot directly award Gems or Shimmer experience to another goddess's Heroes, and ordinary mortals do not possess Hero progression. Her solution is to employ her chosen Heroes as **Crimson Couriers**. A Courier may receive a divine quest to deliver equipment, carry a magically prepared gift, use borrowed divine power to perform a miracle, or set up circumstances in which a Hero of another patron legitimately earns XP under their own divine criteria. Baphomet rewards the Courier, while the intended recipient receives the benefit indirectly.

She enjoys strengthening victims against those who have mistreated them, exposing hypocrites, and setting up dramatic reversals. A captive may receive the key to escape a powerful Naga; a mistreated Starspawn may receive the means to break a restraint. Baphomet does not promise protection beyond exactly what the contract specifies. She may supply information while omitting a known ambush or arrange a spectacular rescue without preventing the chaos it creates. Unlike Surrender she does not restore timelines; unlike Arrest she does not suppress memories. The suffering, especially of those she despises, is part of the appeal.

Her deals are often technically truthful and deceptively incomplete. She insists a freely accepted bargain is not coercion, even when she knows the recipient lacks vital information. Her heresy lies chiefly in **who receives her divine offers**, and how creatively she bypasses the prohibition against aiding people beyond her patronage.

## November 5, 2026 — Hero and Legendary Shimmer Guard (CANON cross-reference)

Heroes possess the divine **Shimmer Guard**, granting two additional low-penalty injury stages beyond an ordinary mortal's three injury advancements. When a Hero proceeds beyond the first Wounded stage, the Guard visibly casts **purple Shimmer light from the eyes**. The Shimmer perfectly diagnoses its Hero's personal injuries and suppresses pain so the Hero can keep acting. A Hero can suppress the visual glow but then experiences full pain; the agreed injury percentage modifiers remain unchanged, with extreme pain adjudicated in context. A healthy Hero does not ordinarily fall to a single overwhelming attack absent DM exception. Renowned **Legends** (mortals or monsters) can manifest their own Guard from mortal belief and reputation, without inheriting the Shimmer's other Hero benefits. ## November 5, 2026 — Global attribute rebasing (CANON)

- New racial Hero **base stats = prior approved base + 10 for each attribute**, then apply the race's usual percentage modifiers. This **intentionally magnifies racial percentage differences** and is not a neutral effective-attribute conversion. Any resulting attribute is **minimum 1**, never negative or zero. Other creatures/monsters may have attributes of **1–9**; Hero starting values are not universal floors.
- **Remove the former combat-only +10** from Power, natural Resistance/Defense Power, and the Speed Evasion Ratio. Compare squared **new effective** values directly. Existing injury thresholds, armor model status and Speed difficulty categories are unchanged.
- Raise effective-attribute **milestone thresholds 25 → 35 and 100 → 110**; leave existing relative racial variation intact. Audit any other ability prerequisites expressed as fixed stat thresholds as their owning source is revised.
- **Natural MP = floor(new effective END)** (minimum consistent with stat floor), subject to ordinary Soul Strain, racial affinity and all existing exceptions. **Moontouched and Drakyn retain 0 natural MP**. Natural MP rise is an explicit intentional buff. **Channel Limits do not rise simply because displayed INT rose**: use a clamped legacy-equivalent INT `max(1, new effective INT − 10)` in the established `ceil(INT / 5)` limit. This leaves slight race-modifier effects by design and avoids negatives. Review all fixed MP costs of existing mana-derived powers **later**, without automatically increasing or altering approved MP/SP prices now.
- Short-ranged attribute-scaling magic also uses `max(1, new effective INT − 10)` rather than raw newly inflated INT, unless its dedicated canon rule differs. This ensures a creature at **new effective INT 11** does not gain a formerly unreachable **greater-than-10-meter** base range merely from the display rebasing. Existing longer-range exceptions such as Warlock Manabending retain their own original scale. See `04_Warlock_Manacraft.md` and `13_Wakecraft_The_Six_Arts_of_the_Living_Tide.md`.
- Adopted provisional practical lifting benchmark (strength + lifting proposal accepted as standard): **one-time waist-height lifting capacity in pounds = `100 × (effective STR / 15)^2`**, subject to actual grip, anatomy, leverage, surface and circumstances. At STR 10 ≈ 44 lb, STR 15 = 100 lb, STR 25 ≈ 278 lb. This is a **tabletop benchmark, not a real-world lifting standard**, and does not mean objects of equal weight can always be maneuvered or thrown.
- Older numerical examples elsewhere in the document using pre-migration values are **historical and superseded** where they conflict with this section.

## November 5, 2026 — Approved attribute-role clarifications

**SPD is reactive movement and velocity**, while **DEX is physical anticipation, precise execution, stealth, lockpicking and practical perception**, and **INT provides learning, memory and evidence-based reasoning**. A well-founded prediction with effective DEX at least **75% of a target's SPD** can reliably create tactical opportunity within the approved pressure-state framework; poor prediction makes the predictor more vulnerable. SPD does not itself predict movement, nor increase attacks. At **INT 100**, photographic memory is an intended baseline; **INT 150** has machine-like recall and potentially near-prophetic reasoning *with adequate data*, never automatic clairvoyance. At **END 150**, brief accidental lava exposure is survivable if immediately escaped; **END 300**, lava is a comparatively minor burn, though volcanic explosions may remain dangerous. Breath/food/water endurance grows roughly **linearly**, with END 300 approximately **10× ordinary oxygen endurance**, not quadratic. Detailed numeric ranges and most chart entries remain under review. Refer to `22_Combat_Endurance_and_Shimmer_Guard.md` and `23_Attribute_Feats_and_Benchmarks_DRAFT.md`.


## November 5, 2026 — Dexterity, non-Hero training and movement-reference clarification

**DEX** now represents immediate physical prediction, precise execution, practical stealth and **counter-stealth** (quietness, cover, timing, reading tells), not automatic mastery of professional techniques such as lockpicking. High DEX does **not** make an openly exposed character invisible. With **DEX ≥ 75% of a target's SPD**, accurate predicted movements can be exploited; at **DEX ≥ 200% of target SPD**, wrong predictions have **no additional wrong-prediction penalty** due to built-in contingency plans. Details and scope: `22_Combat_Endurance_and_Shimmer_Guard.md`.

**Attribute improvement through ordinary training is available to non-Hero mortals**, with no fixed training schedule yet. **Heroes never gain attribute points from training**, relying on Shimmer advancement via experience and level progression; previously approved stat growth remains untouched. **END 25** rather than 15 is the intended ordinary marathon-capable threshold, with circumstances still relevant.

At extraordinary velocities, suitable movement-assisted attacks may gain a **modest Power bonus**, based on **relative closing speed and actual run-up**. The target matching velocity so as to cancel relative closing movement nullifies the charge benefit. **Dash** is peak burst movement, **sprint** is one chart tier below, **sustainable jog** is two tiers below; the numeric speed tiers and proposed +25% Power near 100 mph/+50% near 500 mph **remain UNAPPROVED numerical calibration**. The separate conceptual **Proficiencies** system is also **PROPOSAL ONLY**, to revisit for imported/general tabletop mundane-skill conversations, not the existing magic skill systems.


## November 5, 2026 — Energy resistance glossary guidance (LATEST)

**Immunity, energy-specific:** In Mystaria's ordinary physical and magical damage vocabulary, describing a creature as 'immune' to a form of heat, cold, lightning or other energy means **near-immune**, not genuinely invincible. Sufficiently extreme Elemental Magnitude or an established effective bypass/weakness may overcome the protection. This is a semantic and design ruling, **not** an invented fixed resistance multiplier, mandatory injury on every hit, or a change to unrelated explicitly absolute spiritual/cosmic protections. Even native-element Drakyn are near-immune, never absolutely impervious. Refer to `06_Drakyn.md`, `09_Equipment_and_Economy.md`, `03_Sorcery.md` and `22_Combat_Endurance_and_Shimmer_Guard.md`.
